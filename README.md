An automated trading platform in Go for **MetaTrader 5** symbols (Exness). A **controller** launches and supervises **agents**:

- agents that trade the decisions of a **language model** (OpenAI or Anthropic), each with its own prompt;
- **classic strategy** agents, without an LLM, in four families: trend, momentum, breakout and intraday.

The controller also serves the **web dashboard**, the **Telegram bot** and **XTrader AI**, a language model that watches every agent and only acts to reduce risk. All the operational configuration and the records live in **PostgreSQL**.

![Dashboard of an LLM agent: the day's limit, the accounts' P/L, market sentiment, the chart with the analysis drawn on it, the economic calendar and the model's last decision](docs/images/painel-agente-llm.webp)

> [!WARNING]
> Automated trading carries a real risk of loss. XTrader is not investment advice, and no strategy, prompt or agent in it has been shown to be profitable. Validate any agent in a backtest and on a demo account before a real account. A real account is only accepted with the **Permitir conta real** (Allow real account) option turned on in the agent and in the MT5 account. Read the [Risk disclaimer](#risk-disclaimer) before using it.

## Features

### LLM agent

The `agents/llm/llmagent` agent asks the model for a decision and applies it within risk rules.

- **Context sent to the model:**
  - candles and indicators;
  - price structure and levels;
  - higher timeframes;
  - market session;
  - correlated markets;
  - economic calendar;
  - memory of the latest decisions and trades. Each decision counts by the time it was made: the consultations on the forming candle (interval, reanalysis) see the earlier ones of the same candle. After a restart, the memory comes from the journal, also by the time of each decision;
  - open position, with the candles closed since the entry (`candles_held`), or since the bot started tracking it. The count goes by the candle times, so it includes the closes that the consultation interval skips and does not count the time the market is closed (weekend).
- **Possible decisions:** enter, exit, hold, move the stop loss and take profit, or wait. Each entry carries a stop loss (and a take profit, depending on the prompt) on the order itself. A prompt can also allow pending orders and drawings on the chart (see [SMC/ICT, pending orders and drawings](#smcict-pending-orders-and-drawings)).
- **Rationale:** two or three short sentences, in the language of `-llm-language`: the scenario read and the evidence for the decision, the main contrary evidence and, on an entry, what invalidates it; on a wait, what is missing and what would change the decision.
- **Market sentiment:** besides the decision, every model answer carries its reading of the asset's market (`sentiment`), from 0 (very bearish) to 100 (very bullish), 50 neutral, whatever the action. It is informational only: it does not change the action, does not go through the rules or the minimum confidence, and does not send orders. A value outside 0 to 100, or a missing one, is left out with a note, and the decision is kept. It is stored with the decision (in the database, in the `decision_sentiments` table; in paper, in memory) and shows on the dashboard's gauge and in the decision dialog; the decisions made before this feature, the failures and the analyses not consulted have no reading.
- **Exit that fails** (market closed, quote refused, bridge down, bot stopped): the position is not left without stop loss and take profit.
  - On MT5, the close order goes out with them in place. They go away together with the position, and a refused close does not touch them.
  - In paper, the legs reserve the position: they are canceled before the close and, if it fails, placed again at the same levels.
  - The close is retried, without consulting the model, at every candle close and on the ticks, with a growing interval (30s, 1 min, 2 min... up to 15 min).
  - The failure, a position left without protection and the completed close are reported on the dashboard and on Telegram.
  - On a restart, the pending exit is not resumed: the position keeps its protection, and the model is consulted again.
- **When to consult:** only on the schedule of `llm-consult-interval` (for example, every 5 min), required in live and paper, plus the start of the agent and the **on-demand reanalysis**, from the dashboard or Telegram, with a note for the model. The model is never consulted on a candle close; an LLM agent without the interval does not start (the error shows in the log and on the dashboard). Only the backtest, which has no candles in progress, consults on the closes: those on the schedule, or all of them without the interval.
- **Agent start:** on (re)start, the assets are analyzed at once, in parallel, with the same rules as the other analyses: an asset with no possible entry (for example, when the higher trend is required and it is mixed) is not consulted, and the analysis shows on the dashboard as "Não consultado" (Not consulted), with the reason. Each decision is applied on the asset's next tick, at that tick's price.
- **Repeated "Não consultado":** the analyses of an asset that do not consult the model for the same reason, in sequence (on the consultation interval, with the same position and the same prompt), are kept in a single record: the first one. A note on the record says how many there were, the triggers and the last one, with its time, price and detail when it changes. For example, an asset blocked by news with a consultation every 10 s produces one record, not 360 per hour. A different reason, a model decision, the start of the agent or a reanalysis starts another record.
- **Entry checked at the time of applying it:** the model answers some time after the context is read (up to `llm-timeout`; the start decision, on a later tick). Before sending the entry, the agent reads the position and the price again:
  - if the asset already has a position (for example, a position opened by hand on MT5 during the consultation), the entry is refused;
  - the decision is validated again at the current price, with the same rules as the context: stop and target on the right side, distances, minimum stop distance and risk:reward net of the spread. In live, the price comes from the broker (on MT5, the bid of the last tick), with the spread of the last candle; in the backtest, it is the candle's;
  - if the price cannot be read, the entry is refused too.
  - The refused decision shows as "Recusada pelas regras" (Refused by the rules), with the reason. This also applies to the refusals by the daily limit and by news at the time of applying.
- **Model answer (Anthropic):** the decision is requested through a forced call of the `trading_decision` tool. A model that refuses the forced call (for example, `claude-opus-5-5`, which answers HTTP 400 "tool_choice ... not supported") gets the tool unforced, with a request to answer with it:
  - only that refusal switches the mode; any other 400 error fails the consultation and the next one stays forced. The forced call is tried again once an hour and applies again if the model accepts it;
  - with the unforced tool, if the model answers with text only, the decision JSON is read from the text and goes through the same validations. More than one decision in the text, or none, is an invalid answer;
  - these models think before answering, and the thinking counts toward the token limit: without `llm-max-tokens`, the limit of the unforced answer is 16000 (4096 on the forced call);
  - a cut-off answer (token limit, the model's context window) or a paused one is a consultation failure, with no decision, even when the tool call is complete.
- **Momentary provider failure:** an answer that the API does not charge for, because it did not run the request (`429`, except the exhausted quota `insufficient_quota`; `503`; `529`, Anthropic overloaded), is resent once, after its `Retry-After` or after 1 s, only if the wait is at most 10 s and 5 s of `llm-timeout` are left after it. Otherwise, or if the resend fails, the consultation fails as before and the agent pauses the consultations for the `Retry-After`. The other errors (`500`, `502`, `400` etc.) are never resent.
- **Reasoning effort** (`llm-reasoning`, OpenAI only): `none`, `minimal`, `low`, `medium`, `high` or `xhigh`, as the model accepts (the newer ones accept `none` and `xhigh`, and some do not accept `minimal`). A value the model does not accept is refused by the API (HTTP 400), and the error names the parameter (`the param reasoning_effort`) and gives the API's message. The database accepts these six.
- **Token usage:** each answer logs the input tokens (including those read from the cache), those written to the cache (Anthropic, `cache_creation_input_tokens`, charged separately and not part of the input), the output tokens and the reasoning tokens (OpenAI), and the day's total (UTC, reset on every restart). A 2xx answer that is not valid JSON, or has another format, still counts the tokens when its `usage` object can be read. The cost in money is not calculated: there is no price table in the code.
- **Prompts:** editable and switched while the agent runs. The switch takes effect from the next analysis, and each decision records the prompt and the version used.
- **Protections:**
  - minimum confidence. On a reanalysis with a note from the trader, a decision that reduces the position's risk (EXIT, or the stop moved closer to the price) is applied even below it. The decision records this in its notes ("the min confidence ... waived"), together with the trader's message;
  - minimum risk:reward;
  - maximum spread in ATR;
  - minimum stop distance;
  - blocking near news, in the global window of the [News](#news) tab, the same as for the other agents;
  - daily gain and loss limits per asset, defined in the prompt (see [Daily limits](#daily-limits)).
- **History of the day:** the context carries the asset's positions and orders in the trading day (`daily_history`), with the result of each position.
- **Economic calendar:** read from the MT5 terminal's MCP server, with the address, the token, the switch and the blocking window of the [News](#news) tab, the same for every agent (each agent's `calendar-*` parameters still apply to the window of the model's context).
  - With news disabled, or without the token, no query is made. The context tells the model that the calendar was not read and why (`status`: `disabled`, `missing_token`, `unauthorized` for the token refused by the server, `unavailable` for the service down), and no entry is blocked by news: the lack of a query never counts as an absence of releases.
  - The news in the context (`minutes_from_now` and the value already published) and the blocking of entries use the time of the analysis. In live, it is the current time, including in the consultations on the forming candle (interval, reanalysis and agent start); in the backtest, the candle close.
  - The news block is checked again at the time of applying the entry, like the daily limit: a decision applied inside the news window (a slow model answer, or the start decision applied on a later tick) is refused.
  - Calendar unavailable: the context tells the model that it could not be read (and why), the log warns every 15 min and entries are not blocked by news; with news disabled, only the change is logged. This applies to the server down, a tool with an error, or no currency of the pair with events in the calendar.
  - A tool error (e.g., terminal busy) is retried once, half a second later, and the next reading queries again. Only a currency the calendar does not know goes 1 h without being queried.
  - Unknown currency: any error in the query of a code that is not a currency of the MT5 calendar (e.g., `XTI` from `XTIUSD`). A currency of the MT5 calendar is only taken as unknown when the server refuses the parameter of the call itself (JSON-RPC -32602, invalid parameter). The tool's error message is never enough, whatever its text (e.g., `currency EUR not available`, `Calendar data for currency EUR not available yet`), because it may be transient (e.g., the terminal's calendar still syncing): it counts as a transient error. The currencies of the MT5 calendar are those of the countries in `CalendarCountries` (USD, EUR, JPY, GBP, CAD, AUD, NZD, CHF, CNY, BRL, KRW, HKD, SGD, MXN, ZAR, INR, NOK, SEK and ALL).
  - Skipped currency: the unknown one, or one the calendar returns without any event (queried again on the next reading), produces a warning in the log, at most one per currency per hour.
  - In a two-currency pair (e.g., EURUSD), the unknown currency is skipped and the other one's news stays in the context and in the block. A transient error on one of them, however, leaves the whole pair's calendar unavailable on that reading, with no partial events.
  - Reading the days: each server day is read at once (up to 5000 events). If the server says it cut the answer (`truncated`), the day is read again in halves, down to 15-min intervals; a 15-min interval that is still cut leaves the calendar unavailable on that reading, instead of missing news. An event outside the requested interval, such as one at midnight on a server that includes the end of the interval, is left out, and the same event (ID and time) never enters twice.
  - Cache: the days in progress are read again every 5 min; the finished ones stay in the cache for 24 h and then leave it (a late revision is read again, and a long backtest does not pile up every day in memory).
  - Events without a time: the event's `time_mode` is read. An all-day event (`Event takes all day`), one with no time (`no time`) or a tentative one (`tentative`, only the day known) comes from the calendar at 00:00 server time, which is not its time. It never blocks entries and shows in the context the whole day, with `time_mode` (`all_day`, `no_time` or `tentative`) and the published value only after the end of the day. A tentative or no-time event whose time the calendar already gives (other than 00:00) counts as a timed event, including in the block. Without `time_mode`, the event counts as a timed one, as before.
  - An MCP session that fails to open is discarded, and the next call opens another. The SSE answer is read up to the event that answers the call, without waiting for the server to close the stream (up to 16 MiB).

### SMC/ICT, pending orders and drawings

The `smc_ict` prompt (initial agent `llm-smc`) analyzes the market with Smart Money Concepts / ICT. The features it uses are turned on by lines in the prompt header and work with any prompt; prompts without these lines work exactly as before (same instructions, same schema, same context).

| Header line                     | Effect                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `# Pending orders: limit, stop` | enables the pending orders of the listed types (`limit`: `BUY_LIMIT`/`SELL_LIMIT`; `stop`: `BUY_STOP`/`SELL_STOP`), plus `MODIFY_PENDING` and `CANCEL_PENDING`   |
| `# Drawings: on`                | the model returns the drawings of its analysis (`drawings`), the only ones drawn on the chart: the prompt defines what appears, without the S/R lines (`levels`) |
| `# Context: smc`                | the context gains `smc`, the structure in SMC concepts computed by the code, on the decision timeframe and the higher ones                                       |

An unknown value in one of these lines rejects the prompt, as with the daily limits.

- **`smc_ict` setups:** only two, both in favor of the bias of the higher timeframes (the direction of their most recent break of structure):
  1. **Liquidity sweep + CHoCH:** the liquidity on one side is taken (a wick beyond a swing, the previous day's or week's high/low, or the Asian range, closing back), a displacement closes beyond the last swing on the other side (CHoCH), and the entry is the price's return to the FVG or the order block of that leg, in the discount (buy) or the premium (sell) of the dealing range.
  2. **BOS + retracement:** with the bias, a new break of structure (BOS) with displacement, and the entry on the retracement to the FVG or the order block of the leg, in the discount or the premium.
  - Confirmation: the bias, the break by a close on the decision timeframe (after the sweep, in setup 1), the entry zone intact, and room to the target (the opposite liquidity) for `llm-min-rr`. Without any one of them, `WAIT`.
  - Entry: a limit at the edge of the zone the price returns through; at market, with the same stop and target, when the closed candle already rejects the zone.
  - Stop: beyond the point that invalidates the scenario (the extreme of the sweep, or the origin of the leg of the break), never inside the zone; target: just before the opposite liquidity.
  - Invalidation (the model cancels the pending order): a close beyond the extreme protected by the stop, the zone filled or broken, the bias flipped, a break against the trade before the fill, the target reached before the fill.
  - Management: keeps the position while the structure holds; on each new BOS in favor, moves the stop behind the swing that supports it; exits on a CHoCH against; protects before high-impact news.
  - The prompt sets no risk values: it uses the agent's `llm-min-rr`, `llm-min-stop-atr`, `llm-max-level-atr` and `llm-pending-expiry`. The buffer beyond the stop, a break-even, the killzones and the validity of the pending orders are left for the operator to define.
- **`smc` context** (mechanical definitions over the 3-candle swings of the structure, with no invented size limit: the model weighs each item by its size in ATR):
  - `structure_breaks`: closes beyond the last unbroken swing, `bos` (same direction as the previous break) or `choch` (opposite direction), the 4 most recent;
  - `fair_value_gaps`: three-candle gaps not yet filled (the remaining part, how much has already been taken, size and distance in ATR), the 3 closest on each side;
  - `order_blocks`: the last opposite candle before the leg that broke the structure, marked as mitigated when the price came back to it and discarded when a close goes through it, the 2 closest on each side;
  - `liquidity_sweeps`: wicks beyond a swing that closed back without breaking it, the 3 most recent;
  - `dealing_range`: the leg of the last break, with the equilibrium (50%) and the price's position (below 0.5, discount; above, premium).
- **Pending orders:**
  - They go in through the core's protected order APIs (`CreateOrderLimitProtected`/`CreateOrderStopProtected`), with the stop loss and take profit on the order itself; on MT5, the pending order carries the SL/TP at the broker and is valid until canceled (GTC).
  - Validated as entries, measured from the pending order's price: the type's side of the market (buy limit below the ask, buy stop above it; sell limit above the bid, sell stop below it), stop and target on the correct sides of the entry, distances up to `llm-max-level-atr`, risk:reward (`llm-min-rr`) and stop room (`llm-min-stop-atr`, net of the spread). The minimum confidence, the setup, the direction, the higher-timeframe trend filter, the maximum spread, the daily limit and the news block also apply, checked again at the price of the moment the decision is applied.
  - One pending order per asset, only with no position. While it waits, the model sees it in the context (`pending_order`: type, prices, size, candles waited and who placed it) and can only keep it (`WAIT`), modify it (`MODIFY_PENDING`, with all the new levels; the ones that already exist are moved, never added or removed) or cancel it (`CANCEL_PENDING`).
  - Modification: the limit and the legs are moved at the broker, in the order that keeps each level on the correct side at every step. Brokers cannot move the trigger of a stop order: the stop order is canceled and placed again at the new levels.
  - Canceled by the agent itself, without asking the model, when an entry of its type is no longer allowed (daily limit, news, higher-timeframe trend, spread, direction, or a prompt without that type), when a position appears on the asset (it would add to the position), and after `llm-pending-expiry` candles, if set. The news window is also watched on ticks. The reason goes in the analysis notes, and a cancellation on ticks is notified on the dashboard and on Telegram.
  - Once filled, it becomes the tracked position, with its legs; partially filled, the rest is canceled. Expired, rejected or canceled outside the agent, it leaves tracking, with a note.
  - After a restart, the asset's open pending order (only of a type the prompt uses) is tracked again from the stored orders, with its legs and with `placed_by` read from the decision link. A pending order opened by hand on MT5, under the agent's magic number, is also tracked, like the positions opened outside; with a prompt without pending orders, those pending orders stay untouched, as before.
- **Drawings:** zones (from `low` to `high`), supply (`supply`) and demand (`demand`) zones, levels and markers, each with an id, label, bias, timeframe and times (`from`, and `to` when it ended), linked to the decision that drew them (`decisions[].drawings`). They are validated before being drawn: known type, unique id, label, the decision timeframe or a higher timeframe of the context, times in RFC 3339 up to the last candle, positive prices; an invalid drawing is discarded (up to 20 per decision) with the reason in the notes, without rejecting the decision. Each answered analysis replaces the drawings of the previous one, even when rejected or ignored; a query that failed or was skipped keeps the previous ones. The model receives the current drawings (`chart_drawings`) so it keeps the ids of the ones still valid and leaves out the invalidated ones.
- **What is drawn:** a prompt without `# Drawings:` gets, from the system instructions, the request for the supports and resistances of the analysis (`levels`, up to 8), drawn as dotted **S**/**R** lines across the whole chart. With `# Drawings: on`, that request and the `levels` field leave the instructions and the schema: the chart shows only the drawings the model returns, as the prompt text says (`smc_ict`, for example, draws the nearest liquidity as BSL and SSL levels). A `levels` sent anyway is discarded, with a note on the decision.
- **Supply and demand:** the supply zone is the base the price fell from strongly, the demand zone the base it rose from strongly, with the state (`state`) the model identified: `fresh` (the price has not come back to it), `tested` (it came back and the zone held) or `broken` (a candle of its timeframe closed beyond the far edge: above supply, below demand). The code checks the consistency before drawing and discards, with the reason in the notes: a `fresh` or `tested` zone with an end (`to`), or already broken by the last close of its timeframe in the context; a `broken` one without the candle that broke it (`to`); an unknown state; and the same zone (type, timeframe, `low` and `high`) repeated under another id. Each zone is linked to the asset, the timeframe it was read on and the decision that drew it, and the next analysis replaces it (kept by its id while valid, left out when invalidated).

Each agent runs in `live` mode (launched by the controller) or `paper` mode. From the command line, it also runs backtests.

- **Strategy flags:** a strategy flag is accepted only with a strategy that uses it. A flag that no strategy of the agent uses is rejected at startup, with an error (before, it was accepted and ignored, like `-trailing-atr` on `intradayagent`). An agent saved in the controller with such a flag does not start until the flag is removed from its settings: the reason goes in the controller log (e.g., `-trailing-atr is an input of no strategy of the agent`, on the agent's line), the dashboard shows the last exit (`exit status 1`) and, with **Reiniciar se cair** (Restart if it goes down), the new attempts follow the supervision's growing wait (from 5 s up to 1 min).
  - `-max-risk-pct`: accepted by every strategy of the four agents except `pairs`, which sets no stop: with it, an agent running `pairs` does not start. It rejects the entry that would lose more than this % of the free margin at its stop (both in the account currency, the loss by the point value; see [Account currency](#account-currency)): the strategy's stop (in ATR, beyond the SMA 200 on `matrend`, the Fibonacci or Fimathe levels) or the fixed-value one (`-stop-value`). On `trendconfluence`, it counts the tightest of the configured stops, and one must be configured (`-stop-atr`, `-trailing-atr`, `-broker-stop-atr`, `-broker-trailing-atr` or `-stop-value`), or the agent does not start. The rejected entry goes in the log with the computed loss. Default `0` (no limit): the agents trade as before until they get the parameter. On `fimathe`, the default is `3` (a rule of the methodology), and `-fimathe-max-risk-pct` is still accepted as the same limit (not both together).
  - `-stop-atr` and `-take-profit-atr` (stop and target in ATR 14 from the entry): accepted by every strategy of the four agents except `pairs`, whose two legs protect each other.
    - On the strategies with stop and target in ATR (the signal ones, `orb`, `vwap`, `consensus`...), they change the multiple, as before.
    - On the ones that compute their own levels (`fibonacci`, `fimathe`, `squeezebreakout`, `meanreversion`, `trendpullback`, `scalp1m`, `scalp1mbtc`), they apply only when set: the initial stop and target are placed at these multiples of the ATR from the entry (from the limit price, on limit entries), instead of the strategy's levels. The other exits remain: the stop moved by R or by the channel, the time exit, the opposite signal. Without them, nothing changes.
    - On `matrend`, `-stop-atr` puts the stop at the broker at this multiple from the entry, instead of the stop beyond the SMA 200, and it is then moved by the strategy's trailing; `-take-profit-atr` sets a target, which it did not have.
    - On `trendconfluence`, `-stop-atr` is still the stop checked at the close (the broker stop is `-broker-stop-atr`), and `-take-profit-atr` sets a target at the broker with the entry.
    - On `volumeprofile`, `-stop-atr` is still the room beyond the session extreme, and `-take-profit-atr` replaces the target at the other edge of the value area. On `cmegap`, the stop was already in ATR from the entry, and `-take-profit-atr` replaces the target at the reference.
    - The fixed values (`-stop-value`, `-take-profit-value`) are still accepted and, together, still take precedence over the ATR ones.
  - `-trailing-atr`: accepted and applied, as before, by `trendconfluence` and `matrend`, and now also by `fibonacci` and `consensus` (`trendagent`), by `donchianatr` (`breakoutagent`, the only signal strategy that moves the stop by default) and by every strategy of `momentumagent` (now declared in them). Still rejected with the other strategies of `trendagent` and `breakoutagent` (`emapullback`, `atrbreakout` and the other signals). On `intradayagent`, no strategy moves the stop: once accepted and ignored, it is now rejected.
- **Entry filter (`regime`, `equity-curve`, `meta-model`, `intermarket-symbol`; every strategy agent, no LLM):** filters the entries of any strategy of the four agents without changing its rules. A rejected entry becomes a block for the strategy, like the controller's blocks, and goes to the log once per reason and asset (`[FILTER] … entries refused: …`); exits always pass. Without these parameters, the agent trades exactly as before. A filter still without data (not enough candles, the reference missing or stale, the model without the indicators) rejects the entries. A parameter of a filter without the filter turned on (e.g., `regime-len` without `regime`) keeps the agent from starting, with the error. The filters do not apply to `pairs` (the agent does not start with them): each leg would be judged on its own, and the filter would reject one leg after the other had opened. The default numbers are ours, except where the source is stated. Defaults, all changeable through the parameters:
  - **Regime:** `regime` (`trend` or `range`; off by default) lets entries in only with the market trending, or only with it ranging, as measured by `regime-method`:
    - `er` (default), Kaufman's efficiency ratio ("Trading Systems and Methods"), |C[t] − C[t−n]| / Σ|C[i] − C[i−1]|: `regime-len` 10 (the period of Kaufman's adaptive moving average) and a trend from `regime-threshold` 0.30 up (a common threshold for the ratio, not checked against the book: take it as ours);
    - `adx`, Wilder's ADX ("New Concepts in Technical Trading Systems", 1978): 14 (Wilder's period) and 25 (the usual reading of the index; Wilder talks about the ADXR);
    - `hurst`, the Hurst exponent by rescaled range (Hurst, 1951; Mandelbrot and Wallis, 1969), with the Anis and Lloyd (1976) correction for small samples: 100 returns (ours) and 0.5 (the random walk of the theory). Without the correction, the calculation overestimates H in short windows (a random walk of 100 returns gives about 0.6) and almost everything would look like a trend; with it, random walks stay at 0.5, above is persistent (trend) and below, reversion.
    - Use `trend` on the trend, breakout and momentum agents and `range` on the reversion ones (`zscore`, `meanreversion`, `vwap`). `regime-max-atr-pct` (default 0, off) rejects entries while the ATR 14 is above this percentile of the previous `regime-atr-window` (100, ours) candles (extreme volatility), with or without `regime`.
    - On the chart: a pane with the value of `er` or `adx`.
  - **Equity curve:** no published source (trading the equity curve is a common idea among traders; the rule and the numbers are ours). `equity-curve` N (default 0, off; at least 2) pauses entries while the curve of the strategy's theoretical trades, in R, is below the average of its last N points, and only judges after N closed trades.
    - Each entry attempt with a stop becomes a theoretical trade, one at a time per asset, even when rejected: that is how the curve rises again and releases the entries.
    - It closes at the stop (−1R), at the target (+target/risk R), at the stop when both are hit on the same candle, at the close after `equity-curve-max-bars` (100) candles, or when its real position goes flat.
    - It is kept only in memory: it starts from zero on every agent restart. In the backtest, it starts empty at the start of the period, as in live: the warm-up attempts do not go into it.
  - **Meta-labeling** (López de Prado, "Advances in Financial Machine Learning": the triple-barrier labels, the purging and the secondary model over the primary model's signals): a model estimates the chance of each strategy signal ending positive, and lets entries in only above a threshold. The 18 indicators, the logistic regression with L2 (the book does not prescribe the model), the 70/30 split, the L2 of 1, the threshold of 0.5 and the 100 candles are ours.
    - `meta-record` (JSONL file, e.g., `data/meta/matrend.jsonl`) records each entry attempt with 18 indicators of the moment (ATR, ADX, efficiency, RSI, distance and slope of the EMA 50, Bollinger width, 10-candle return, relative volume, hour and weekday, side) and, when known, the triple-barrier label: 1 with the target before the stop, 0 with the opposite; when it expires at `meta-max-bars` (100) candles or is closed by the strategy, the sign of the result. In the controller's backtests, it records from the start of each segment: run a backtest with it to collect the samples. Each sample says where it came from (`source`: `live`, `paper` or `backtest`; in the backtest, also the run and the segment, `fit` or `validation`).
    - The command `xtrader metalabel --samples data/meta/matrend.jsonl --out data/meta/matrend.json` discards the repeated samples (the same attempt recorded by two backtests of the same period), shows how many came from each source and trains a logistic regression with L2 regularization, split by time (the oldest 70% for training, the rest for testing, purging the training samples that end inside the test), and shows the training and test AUC and, for each threshold from 0.40 to 0.70, how many entries it would let through and their precision. It also accepts `--agent`, `--strategy`, `--pair`, `--source` (`live`, `paper`, `backtest`, comma-separated), `--segment` (`fit`, `validation`), `--train-share` (0.7), `--l2` (1), `--threshold` (0.5) and `--json`. In production, run it as the agents' user, or the model is unreadable to them: `docker exec -u trader -w /app xtrader-controller-1 /app/bin/xtrader metalabel …`, with the paths relative to `/app`.
    - `meta-model` (the model file) lets entries in only with a probability of a positive result (the label above: the target before the stop, or a positive result at expiry or at the strategy's close) ≥ `meta-threshold` (default: the model's, 0.5). An unreadable model keeps the agent from starting. The model stores how far the training samples go (`trained_through`): a backtest with it that starts before that date is rejected, because the period would be in sample; test it on periods after the training.
  - **Intermarket:** `intermarket-symbol` (e.g., `DXYm`, read and never traded) allows the buy or the sell depending on how the reference closes against its own `intermarket-ema` (50) EMA:
    - `intermarket-mode = inverse` (default): buys only with the reference below the EMA and sells only with it above (the dollar against gold); `direct` is the opposite.
    - Only on the pairs of `intermarket-pairs` (default all, e.g., `XAUUSDm`).
    - It uses only the reference candles already closed at the pair's candle, the last one at most 4 candles behind (the EMA 50, the 4 candles and the `inverse` mode as the default are ours). A pair of `intermarket-pairs` that the agent does not trade keeps the agent from starting (a typo would turn the filter off silently).
    - It works in paper, in live and in the controller's backtests, which read the reference candles of the same period; it is rejected in the backtest by `-csv`.
- **Fibonacci (`fibonacci`, `trendagent`, no LLM):** trades the pullback of each impulse in the impulse's direction, at the Fibonacci retracements: buys on the pullback of a rally, sells on the pullback of a drop, one position at a time per asset. The levels are retracements of the impulse, from 0 (the extreme) to 1 (the origin). Defaults, all changeable through the parameters:
  - **Impulse:** `fib-swing = pivot`, from one pivot to the next opposite pivot, each pivot with `fib-pivot = 5` candles on each side (it is only known 5 candles later). The most recent top, from the low since the previous bottom, is the buy impulse; the most recent bottom, from the high since the previous top, the sell impulse. With `fib-swing = range`, the impulse goes from the low to the high of the last `fib-lookback` candles (default 300). A minimum of `fib-min-impulse-atr = 3` ATR at the extreme's candle. The impulse stops being valid at a price beyond the extreme (it continued; the next pivot forms another one) or at a close beyond the origin, and each impulse is traded once (an agent restart forgets the ones already traded). `fib-trend-ema = 200` trades only in the direction of the EMA 200 (default 0, off).
  - **Entry:** `fib-trigger = close`, at market at the close of the candle that rejects the zone from `fib-entry-low = 0.5` to `fib-entry-high = 0.618`: the wick reaches 50%, the candle closes in the impulse's direction (above the open on a buy) and the close does not go past 61.8%. With `fib-trigger = limit`, a limit order at `fib-limit-level` (default 0.618, inside the zone), placed while the price is on the extreme's side and canceled when the impulse stops being valid, when a position appears on the asset, or after `fib-pending-bars` candles (default 20; 0 for only by the impulse). A limit placed before a restart is no longer tracked: the position it opens is, like the others, but canceling the limit is up to the operator.
  - **Stop:** at `fib-stop-level = 0.786`, plus `fib-stop-buffer-atr = 0.25` ATR beyond it (`fib-stop-level = 1` places it at the origin).
  - **Target:** `fib-target = extreme`, at the extreme of the impulse; `extension`, at `fib-target-level` (default 1.272) times the impulse from the origin; `rr`, `fib-reward-risk` (default 2) times the distance to the stop; `none`, no take profit, which requires `trailing-atr`.
  - **Also accepts:** `trailing-atr`, `max-bars`, `min-atr-pct`, `stop-value` and `take-profit-value` (the fixed values replace the Fibonacci stop and target, and are rejected together with `fib-stop-level`/`fib-stop-buffer-atr` and with `fib-target`/`fib-target-level`/`fib-reward-risk`), `stop-atr` and `take-profit-atr` (when set, the stop and target at these multiples of the ATR from the entry, instead of the Fibonacci levels).
  - **On the chart:** the levels of the current impulse (0, the zone, the stop, 1 and the target extension) from the origin on, the pivots and the ATR.

- **Fimathe (`fimathe`, `breakoutagent`, no LLM):** the first trend entry of each mapping of the reference channel of the Fimathe technique (Fimathe 4.0, lesson 3), as the official robot automates it. With the defaults:
  - **Mapping:** at the start of each week (Sunday, 00:00 UTC), with the 1h bars closed before it: the trend from the last two tops and bottoms, the reference channel from the last bottom to the last top (wick, or body if too wide, between 1 and 8 ATR), the neutral zone on the other side of the trend.
  - **Entry** at the close of a 15m candle: back beyond the channel's 50% (price below it or in the neutral zone), or beyond the channel's edge (price that stayed beyond the 50%). A close beyond the neutral zone (reversal zone) ends the mapping. One entry per mapping and per asset, also counting the ones from before a restart.
  - **Exit:** a stop beyond the neutral zone, moved by the subcycle (the channel of the 15m candles before the entry): 0x0 in the first 50% and one step every 50%; no target, or a target at 2 channel heights with the stop locked at 0x0. At most 3% of the free balance lost at the stop (`max-risk-pct`, or `fimathe-max-risk-pct`). With `stop-atr` and `take-profit-atr` set, the initial stop and target are at these multiples of the ATR from the entry, instead of the channel levels, and the subcycle keeps moving the stop; the fixed values are still rejected.
  - **On the chart:** the channel, the neutral zone, the 50%, the expansions, the reversal zone, the planned entry (dashed, not an order), the entry made, the subcycle and the stop moves, with the legend "Fimathe".

- **The rules of the LLM agent's prompts (`meanreversion`, `scalp1m`, `scalp1mbtc`, `trendpullback`, `squeezebreakout`, no LLM):** the rules of the prompts `mean_reversion`, `scalp_1m`, `scalp_1m_btc`, `default` (the `trend_pullback` setup; `free`, the free-weighing one, does not become code) and `breakout`, with the approved numbers where the prompt was vague. What the candles do not carry is left out: the economic calendar, the levels of the higher timeframes and the spread, in whose place `min-atr-pct` applies. Each one computes its own stop and target and measures them in R, the profit of a close in units of the initial risk (from the entry to the first stop): it moves the stop to the entry (break-even, without the spread) and, further on, behind the price, never backward, and closes a stalled trade on time. They accept `min-atr-pct`, the sizing flags (`lot`, `max-risk-pct`...) and `stop-atr` and `take-profit-atr`: when set, the initial stop and target are at these multiples of the ATR from the entry, and R is measured from that stop; `max-bars` and `trailing-atr` are rejected. Short mirrors long:
  - **`meanreversion`** (15m): a ranging market (ADX 14 below 20 and not rising, EMA 9 and 21 less than 0.25 ATR apart, the close crossing the Bollinger 20 middle band at least 2 times in 20 candles); a low at the lower band (20, 2) with RSI 14 below 30 on the candle or the previous one, the candle closing back inside, bullish, with a lower wick of at least 50%. Stop 0.5 ATR below the lowest low of 5 candles, target at the middle band, minimum 1.2R. Exits at the middle band, with ADX above 25 and the DI against, or on a close outside the band again; break-even at 0.7R; exits after 8 candles below 0.2R. The filters can be loosened through agent parameters: `meanreversion-oversold` and `meanreversion-overbought` (the RSI, 30/70), `meanreversion-max-adx` (20), `meanreversion-flat-atr` (0.25) and `meanreversion-wick-pct` (0.5); without them, these numbers apply.
  - **`scalp1m`** (1m): volume ≥ 0.8× the 20-candle average, ADX above 18, London or New York open (08:00 to 17:00 in each city), EMA 9 and 21 separated by ≥ 0.1 ATR and sloping in favor over 3 candles, the close beyond both, the 15m and 1h EMAs 9/21 not opposed; a pullback of up to 5 candles to the EMA 9 without closing beyond the EMA 21, and the candle closing beyond the EMA 9 and the previous high with the MACD histogram in favor or the RSI crossing 50. It does not enter after 3 strong candles in a row (body ≥ 0.5 ATR), more than 1 ATR from the EMA 9, or with RSI above 75. Stop below the pullback, between 1 and 1.5 ATR; target 1.5R, before the 20-candle high; minimum 1.2R. Break-even at 0.8R, trailing of 1 ATR from 1.5R, exit on a close beyond the EMA 21 and after 10 candles without 0.5R.
  - **`scalp1mbtc`** (1m): only the strong impulses: ADX above 20 and rising with the DI in favor, volume ≥ 1.2× the average, the close beyond the SMA 200, no 60-candle high less than 3 ATR ahead; a pullback of up to 3 candles to the EMA 9 without closing beyond it, the candle closing beyond the previous high with the MACD histogram growing. It does not enter after 4 strong candles, more than 1.5 ATR from the EMA 9, or with RSI above 78. Stop between 1.5 and 2 ATR; target 2R, at least 3 ATR away. Break-even at 1R, trailing of 1.5 ATR from 1.5R, exit beyond the EMA 21 or with the ADX falling and the other DI on top, and after 15 candles without 0.5R.
  - **`trendpullback`** (1h): EMA 9 above the 21, the close above the SMA 200, ADX ≥ 20; a pullback of up to 5 candles to the EMA 9 without closing below the SMA 200; the candle closing above the EMA 9 with the MACD histogram turning in favor or the RSI crossing 50, RSI ≤ 70. Stop below the pullback, at least 1 ATR away; target at the highest high of 50 candles, or 2R without one above; minimum 1.5R. Break-even at 1R; from 2R, trailing behind the last bottom (5-bar pivot) or at 1 ATR, whichever is closer; exits on a close below the EMA 21.
  - **`squeezebreakout`** (15m): the box of the 10 candles before the breakout, with a height ≤ 3 ATR and the ATR below the 30th percentile of the last 100; a close beyond the box with a body ≥ 50% of the candle, volume ≥ 1.5× the average (2× against the SMA 200) and ADX rising with the DI in favor. It enters up to 0.5 ATR from the box; further away, it waits up to 5 candles for a retest (within 0.25 ATR of the edge, closing beyond it) and a candle closing beyond the retest candle's high. Stop in the middle of the box, or below the breakout candle if that is tighter and at least 1 ATR away; target at the box height projected from the edge; minimum 2R. Exits on a close back inside the box; break-even at 1R; from 1.5R, trailing by the EMA 9 or the low of the last 2 candles, whichever is closer. With the stop in the middle of the box, the target never reaches 2R: it only enters with the stop below the breakout candle. The filters can be loosened through agent parameters: `squeezebreakout-min-rr` (2), `squeezebreakout-min-volume` (1.5), `squeezebreakout-max-atr-percentile` (30) and `squeezebreakout-max-box-atr` (3); without them, these numbers apply.
  - **After a restart**, the position found keeps the strategy's exits, but without the initial risk: the break-even and the trailing by R stop, and the time exit counts only the candles. `squeezebreakout` also no longer knows the box: the position keeps the broker's stop and target.

- **Consensus (`consensus`, `trendagent`, no LLM):** votes among the rules of the signal strategies (the same as `ema200`, `supertrend`, `donchian`..., with the classic periods of each): buys when at least `consensus-quorum` rules signaled a buy in the last `consensus-window` candles (the current one included) and none signaled a sell in that span; sells in the mirror case. One position at a time per asset. Each rule votes the way it trades on its own: its signal on a candle is the one at that candle's close, computed only from the candles up to it (no look-ahead), and each one computes its own indicators separately (several use the same internal names, e.g. the trend EMA, 200 in `ema200` and 50 in `adxema`). Default timeframe 15m (that of the signal strategies). Defaults, all changeable through the parameters:
  - **Rules:** `consensus-rules = ema200,supertrend,macdtrend,adxema,donchian`: a trend rule (the close crossing the rising EMA 200), a volatility trend rule (the flip of the Supertrend 10/3), a momentum rule (the MACD 12/26/9 crossing the signal line beyond zero), a momentum rule with ADX (close beyond the EMA 50 with the ADX 14 above 25 and the DI in favor) and a breakout rule (the close beyond the 20-period Donchian channel). Accepts any names of the signal strategies, separated by commas, at least 2, each one once; the same rule under another name is refused (`donchian` and `donchianatr` are the same rule with different exits, and only the rule votes). `mtftrend` and `mtfema` were left out of the default: their warm-up (4 and 16 times the timeframe) would become the consensus's warm-up.
  - **Entry:** at market at the close of the candle on which the consensus forms: `consensus-quorum = 3` rules in the same direction within `consensus-window = 3` candles, none in the opposite direction (`consensus-window = 1`: on the same candle). A consensus that still holds on the following candles does not enter again (for example, after the stop), just as each rule fires only on its own event. A rule that signaled both a buy and a sell within the window vetoes both sides.
  - **Stop:** at `stop-atr` (1) ATR 14 from the entry, at the broker.
  - **Target:** at `take-profit-atr` (1.5) ATR from the entry (`take-profit-atr = 0`: no target). The opposite consensus closes the position at market and, if it formed on that candle, opens its own side; while it holds, an opposite position found (e.g. after a restart) is closed too.
  - **Also accepts:** `trailing-atr` (the stop at the broker following the best price since the entry, never backward, as in `donchianatr`; default 0, off), `max-bars` (default 0), `min-atr-pct`, `stop-value`, `take-profit-value` and the sizing flags (`lot`, `max-risk-pct`...).
  - **Warm-up:** that of the longest rule plus the window: 2204 candles with the default rules, about 23 days on 15m.
  - **On the chart:** each rule's indicators, with the rule's name in the group (e.g. `ema200: EMA 200 Trend`, `adxema: ADX 14`), and the ATR 14.
  - **Source:** the rules are the classic ones of the existing signal strategies; the quorum vote within a window is a combination (ensemble) with no published source. The default rules, the quorum of 3, the window of 3, the 15m timeframe and the exits (those of the signal strategies) are our choices, to be validated in backtests.

- **RSI 2 (`rsi2`, `momentumagent`, no LLM):** the 2-period RSI strategy of Larry Connors and Cesar Alvarez ("Short Term Trading Strategies That Work", 2008, chapter "The 2-Period RSI: The Trader's Holy Grail of Indicators?"): buys the pullback of a rising market and sells the bounce of a falling market (the book tests both sides), one position at a time per asset, on the book's daily candles (`timeframe = 1d`). Defaults, all changeable through the parameters:
  - **Trend:** buys only with the close above the SMA 200 and sells only below it (the book's periods, SMA 200, RSI 2 and SMA 5, are fixed).
  - **Entry:** at market at the close of the candle on which the (Wilder) RSI 2 closes below `rsi2-oversold` (10), a buy, or above `rsi2-overbought` (90), a sell. The level counts, not the cross: with no position, it enters on any close with the RSI beyond it. The book also tests more extreme levels, which both flags accept.
  - **Exit:** the book's rule, at market on the first close above the SMA 5 (buy) or below it (sell). If the entry on the other side holds on the same close, it is opened (a combination of ours: the book tests each side separately).
  - **Stop:** Connors's tests use no stop (the book concludes that a stop makes these short-term systems worse). Here the stop sits at the broker only as protection, at `stop-atr` (1) ATR 14 from the entry: the default of the strategies with a stop and target, not a number from the book. For a system that buys weakness, 1 ATR is tight: test a larger `stop-atr` in the backtests.
  - **Target:** also only protection, ours and not the book's, at `take-profit-atr` (1.5) ATR, which may close before the SMA 5 exit. With `take-profit-atr = 0`, no target: the exit is only through the SMA 5, closer to the book.
  - **Also accepts:** `max-bars` (default 0, no time exit), `min-atr-pct`, `trailing-atr` (default 0, off; not a rule of the book, accepted as in every `momentumagent` strategy), `stop-value` and `take-profit-value`, `direction` (e.g. `buy`, only the long side, on an asset in a strong uptrend) and the sizing flags (`lot`, `max-risk-pct`...).
  - **Warm-up:** 210 candles: the SMA 200 plus 10 of slack (ours), with the ATR 14 seed (154 candles) inside that window, since the decision reads only the last candle. On `1d`, it fits in the year of warm-up that the controller's backtest reads before the period, on gold too (about 260 daily candles per year).
  - **After a restart:** the position found keeps the SMA 5 exit and the legs at the broker, because the rule keeps no state.
  - **On the chart:** the SMA 200, the SMA 5 and the RSI 2 in a panel.
  - The edge was measured by the book on US stocks on the daily timeframe; on XAUUSD and BTCUSD it is a hypothesis to be measured in the backtests.

- **Breakout after the news (`newsbreakout`, `intradayagent`, no LLM):** trades the breakout of the range the price draws right after a scheduled economic release (payroll, CPI, rate decision...), with the `orb` rules applied to that range in place of the session's opening range (without the `orb` VWAP filter, on by default there). The rule does not come from a book: it is the opening range breakout (ORB) anchored at the release time, and none of the numbers below has a published source. The releases come from the calendar of the [News](#news) tab, the same one the news guard uses, delivered to the agent by the controller in live and paper, and, in the backtest, from the period's releases read by the controller: only the time, the event and the importance, never the values, so the backtest does not look into the future. Without a calendar (news off, no token, or the agent running outside the controller), it does not enter, and the log says it is waiting for the releases. Defaults, all changeable through the parameters:
  - **Releases:** those with an exact time (all-day or to-be-confirmed ones are left out) of importance `newsbreakout-importance = high` or above (`low`, `medium` or `high`), in the asset's currencies (USD for XAUUSDm and BTCUSDm). `newsbreakout-codes` (default empty: every event) restricts them to a list of `event_code` codes from the MQL5 calendar, separated by commas or semicolons, case-insensitive; check the codes on the server, they are not in the code. Releases at the same time count as one. The calendar is read at most every 5 min on the candle clock (the close of the last candle), not on the computer's clock, so the backtest repeats live trading: a to-be-confirmed release that gets a time later shows up on the next read in both. Each read has up to 5 s, and a read that fails keeps the releases already read, with a warning in the log, and is retried 5 min of candles later.
  - **Range:** the high and low of the candles within `newsbreakout-wait = 15m` after the release (opened at it or later and closed by the end of the wait; the candle that contains the release time is left out). The 15 minutes and the `high` importance are the defaults of the News tab guard: the first entry goes out after the guard releases the entries. With a larger "after" window, raise `newsbreakout-wait` along with it, otherwise the entries inside the window are refused by the guard (a later breakout can still enter). `newsbreakout-max-range-atr` skips the release whose range is wider than that multiple of the ATR (default 0, off).
  - **Entry:** at market on the first close beyond the range, with the previous close inside it, by `newsbreakout-buffer-atr = 0` ATR or more (ours, the "first close beyond the range"; `orb` uses 0.1), on a candle that closes before the end of `newsbreakout-expiry = 1h` after the wait (ours), or before the next release, if it comes first. One entry per release and per asset, also counting those from before a restart, read from the orders stored by the bot (with the same limitation as `orb`: a manual entry in the window, before the restart, also counts).
  - **Stop:** at `stop-atr` (1) ATR 14 from the entry close, as in `orb`.
  - **Target:** at `take-profit-atr` (2) ATR from the entry close, as in `orb`.
  - **Exit:** at market on a close back beyond the middle of the range (the breakout failed), or at the end of the window (the end of `newsbreakout-expiry`, or the next release), as `orb` closes at the end of the session. With the calendar read, a position found outside the window of a release (opened before a restart, or by hand in MT5) is closed too; without it, the position keeps the stop and the target.
  - **Also accepts:** `timeframe` (default 5m), `max-bars`, `min-atr-pct`, `stop-value`, `take-profit-value` and the sizing flags (`lot`, `max-risk-pct`...). `trailing-atr` is refused.
  - **Positions opened by hand:** the strategy adopts any position of the asset on its magic number, like `orb`: one opened by hand in MT5 outside the window of a release is closed on the next candle.
  - **Warm-up:** 156 candles of 5m (the seeded ATR 14).
  - **On the chart:** the high and low of each release's range, on the candles where the breakout is valid.
  - **Limitations:** the backtest does not run the news guard (its blocking and closing in live are not reproduced), and the backtest's releases are those the calendar stores today for the period (a rescheduled release shows up at its final time). Whether the breakout after the news is profitable is a hypothesis to be measured in the backtests.

- **Intraday seasonality (`seasonality`, `intradayagent`, no LLM):** trades the bias of fixed windows of the day, measured walk-forward on the asset's own history: each window's return on the previous days, at the same time in the market's city, indicates the direction it tends to move in. The basis is the persistence of the returns of the same interval of the day over the following days, which Heston, Korajczyk and Sadka show across stocks ("Intraday Patterns in the Cross-section of Stock Returns", Journal of Finance, 2010: a stock's return in a half hour, relative to the others, predicts that of the same half hour on the following days); using the absolute return of a single asset over time is our hypothesis. For gold, Caminschi and Heaney ("Fixing a Leaky Fixing", Journal of Futures Markets, 2014) show the effect of the London PM fixing, in its old format, replaced by the LBMA electronic auction in 2015; the AM fixing window has no cited study. It is a hypothesis to be measured in the backtests: the candles do not carry the spread, and a bias of a few points may not pay for it. Defaults, all changeable through the parameters:
  - **Windows:** `seasonality-windows = 10:30/60@Europe/London;15:00/60@Europe/London;09:30/60@America/New_York`, which are gold's AM and PM fixings (LBMA Gold Price, at 10:30 and 15:00 London time) and the open of the New York stock exchanges (09:30): the times come from the sources, the one-hour duration of each is ours (Heston et al. measure half hours). Each window is `HH:MM/minutos@fuso` (minutes@time zone, an IANA name), separated by `;`, and follows its own city's daylight saving time (10:30 in London is 10:30 UTC in winter and 09:30 UTC in summer). The duration is a multiple of the timeframe, below 23 hours, and the start must fall on a candle open all year round: 10:30 works on 15m, not on 1h, and the agent does not start with such a window.
  - **Bias:** the window's return, from the open of the first candle to the close of the last, on each of the last `seasonality-days = 60` days on which it traded, never that of the day about to be traded. Only a day with the candles at both ends counts: gold's weekend, a holiday or a gap in the data are left out (BTC's weekends count). With at least `seasonality-min-days = 20` days, it computes the t statistic t = mean / (deviation / √n), with the sample standard deviation. The 60 and the 20 days are ours.
  - **Entry:** at market at the close of the candle that ends at the start of the window, when |t| ≥ `seasonality-min-t = 2` (near the usual 5% significance level, a statistical convention, not a number from the papers: with the minimum of 20 days, the 5% critical t is about 2.09, and 2 corresponds to p ≈ 0.06; there is also no correction for the number of windows tested). Buys with a positive mean and sells with a negative one; with `0`, trades every window by the sign of the mean. One position at a time per asset: a window that starts with another position open is not traded (the New York open, at 14:30 London time for most of the year, overlaps half an hour of the PM fixing). Of windows that start together, the one with the highest |t| wins.
  - **Exit:** at market at the close of the candle that ends at the end of the window.
  - **Stop / Target:** safety levels, far from the moves of a window, ours: `stop-atr` (3) and `take-profit-atr` (4.5) ATR 14 of the timeframe's candles, from the entry.
  - **Timeframe:** 15m (ours). The agent keeps the 60 days of candles as if the market traded 24 hours a day, plus the ATR seed: 5,919 candles on 15m, and the backtest reads about 94 days before the period. The limit is 20,000 candles: above that, it measures the days that fit, at least `seasonality-min-days`. On 1m, the 20 days do not fit and the agent does not start.
  - **Also accepts:** `timeframe`, `max-bars` (closes before the end of the window), `min-atr-pct`, `stop-value`, `take-profit-value` and the sizing flags (`lot`, `max-risk-pct`...).
  - **Positions opened by hand:** the strategy adopts any position of the asset on its magic number: one opened by hand in MT5 outside a window is closed on the next candle.
  - **On the chart:** each window's t statistic on the entry candle, with the ±`seasonality-min-t` lines.
  - **After a restart:** the position found closes at the end of the last window started before its opening (read from the orders stored by the bot), or right away, if that window has already ended.
  - **Risks:** with 3 windows on 2 assets at |t| ≥ 2, one passes by chance every now and then, and the 60-day samples of consecutive days overlap in 59 days: a window that becomes significant stays so for days, by chance or not. Only out-of-sample backtests can tell. The PM fixing (10:00 New York time) coincides with frequent US releases: the news guard may refuse the entry or close the position within the window.
  - **Measure before turning it on:** the `xtrader seasonality` command (`cmd/xtrader`) measures the same bias on candle files (the CSVs from `xtrader download` or from the candle cache of the controller's backtests, `data/backtests/candles`): for each window, the days, the mean and deviation of the return in %, the t statistic, the fraction of up days and the bias (`alta` (up), `baixa` (down), `-` or `poucos dias` (few days)), and the same table for each hour of the day in the `--zone` time zone. Unlike the strategy, it measures the file's whole period at once, not the last 60 days of each day. Flags: `--csv` (one or more files, required), `--windows` (the format and default of `seasonality-windows`), `--zone` (default `UTC`), `--min-t` (default 2), `--min-days` (default 20), `--from` and `--to` (days as `AAAA-MM-DD` (YYYY-MM-DD); `--to` is the day after the last one) and `--json` (JSON instead of the tables). E.g.: `go run ./cmd/xtrader seasonality --csv xau-15m.csv --from 2026-08-01 --to 2026-11-01`.

- **Volume Profile (`volumeprofile`, `intradayagent`, no LLM):** the "80% rule" of the Market Profile value area (J. Peter Steidlmayer; James Dalton, "Mind Over Markets"), read from the candles' volume. When the session opens outside the previous session's value area and comes back inside it, the rule says the market tends to cross it to the other edge. One position per session and per asset. Only the 70% of the value area (Steidlmayer) and the two 30-minute periods back inside it (Dalton) come from the sources; the other numbers are ours. Defaults, all changeable through the parameters:
  - **Session:** the CME trading day, from `volumeprofile-anchor = 17:00` to 17:00 in the `volumeprofile-timezone = America/New_York` time zone (the 23-hour Globex day of the gold and bitcoin futures; ours: Steidlmayer and Dalton used the exchange's day session, so the "open" tested here is the 18:00 New York reopen, at a low-liquidity hour), following New York's daylight saving time. Candles join the session by their open time. The previous session is the last one with candles (the previous trading day): a weekend or holiday with no candles is skipped.
  - **Profile:** the volume of each candle of the previous session (MT5's real volume or, without it, the tick volume; on CFDs, the tick volume) is spread evenly from the candle's low to its high, over `volumeprofile-bins = 50` price bands (ours; Market Profile uses tick-sized rows) from the session's low to its high. With no volume at all, each candle weighs the same (time at price, like TPOs). The POC is the band with the most volume; on a tie, the one closest to the middle of the session. The value area starts from the POC and, as in the CBOT method (Dalton), compares the two bands above with the two below and adds the pair with more volume (on a side with only one band left, that band; both pairs on a tie, ours), until it gathers `volumeprofile-value-area = 0.70` of the session's volume (Steidlmayer's 70%, about one standard deviation).
  - **Entry:** at market, at the close of the candle that completes `volumeprofile-confirm = 2` consecutive closes inside the value area, after the session opened outside it (reading Dalton's two periods as two closes inside is ours; a common, looser reading is two TPOs inside). The opening candle counts if it closes inside. On the default timeframe, 30m, this gives Dalton's two 30-minute periods. Opened above the area: sell. Opened below: buy. Only on that close: a streak that stays inside does not enter later. It does not enter on the last candle that closes before the end of the session or after it, nor if the session has already traded at the opposite edge (the area has already been filled): these two rules are ours.
  - **Stop:** `stop-atr` (1) ATR 14 beyond the session's extreme outside the value area (the high, after opening above; the low, after opening below); Dalton gives no stop, the 1 ATR is ours. It is always farther than the bracket's stop at `stop-atr` ATR from the close, and may be wide relative to the target (the source sets no minimum risk-reward).
  - **Target:** the other edge of the value area. A position still open is closed at market at the end of the session in which it entered (ours: the source does not say what to do with it), at the close of the last candle that ends before the end, e.g. at 16:30 New York time on 30m, while gold still trades (the candle that ends at 17:00 would close with the market already closed); if that candle is missing, on the first candle after it.
  - **Also accepts:** `max-bars` (default 0, only the end of the session), `min-atr-pct`, `stop-value` and `take-profit-value` (the fixed values replace the profile's stop and target), `take-profit-atr` (when set, the target at that multiple of the ATR from the entry, in place of the other edge of the value area) and the sizing flags (`lot`, `max-risk-pct`...). `trailing-atr` is refused.
  - **Why the CME trading day:** anchored at midnight UTC, gold's Friday session (which closes before 00:00 UTC) would never end, and Friday's trade would be carried into the weekend; the Sunday-night stretch would become a short "previous session" for Monday. With another anchor (e.g. `volumeprofile-anchor = 00:00` and `volumeprofile-timezone = UTC`), use `max-bars` so as not to hold through the weekend.
  - **After a restart:** the session's position opened before it counts toward the limit of one per session, as in `orb`'s `-max-trades`, read from the orders stored by the bot, with the same limitation: a manual entry in the session also counts. The position found is closed at the end of the session in which it was opened.
  - **On the chart:** the high (VAH), the POC and the low (VAL) of the previous session's value area, as points.
  - The "80%" in the name is Dalton's claim, not a result measured here: it is a hypothesis to be measured in the backtests on the real assets.

- **CME gap (`cmegap`, `intradayagent`, no LLM):** trades against the weekend gap, back to the CME Friday close: sells a gap up, buys a gap down, at most one position per week per asset. The hours are those of CME Globex for the bitcoin (BTC) and gold (GC) futures: Sunday to Friday, 17:00 to 16:00 Chicago time, following Chicago's daylight saving time. The idea that gaps tend to fill is a market belief, not a rule with a source: it is a hypothesis to be measured in the backtests before turning the agent on. Defaults, all changeable through the parameters:
  - **Reference:** the close of the last candle that ends by the CME Friday close (`cmegap-close = 16:00`, in `cmegap-timezone = America/Chicago`). On `BTCUSDm`, which trades over the weekend, it is the price of `BTCUSDm` itself at the time the CME futures stopped (not the futures price: the two differ by the basis); on `XAUUSDm`, which closes over the weekend, it is the market's own last price of the week.
  - **Gap:** the close of the first candle opened from the Sunday reopen (`cmegap-open = 17:00`) on, minus the reference. On a market that reopens later, its first candle after that time counts.
  - **Entry:** at market, at the close of that first candle, when the gap is at least `cmegap-min-gap-atr = 0.5` ATR (14) of that candle (ours, no source), against the gap (toward the reference). Only that candle enters: a gap not traded there (smaller than the minimum, or with the entry refused by the trading hours, by a news item or by a daily limit) is not traded later in the week.
  - **Stop:** `stop-atr` (1) ATR beyond the entry, on the side opposite the reference (the default of the strategies with a stop and target, ours).
  - **Target:** the reference (the gap filled).
  - **Time exit:** `cmegap-expiry = 120` hours after the entry (ours, up to the following Friday) or, if it comes first, at the close of the last candle that ends before the next CME Friday close. It does not wait for the candle that ends at the close itself, which may only complete with the gold market already closed. So the position is never carried into the next weekend; with the 120-hour default, the Friday exit always applies.
  - **Also accepts:** `max-bars`, `min-atr-pct` (skips the entry when the ATR is below that % of the price), `stop-value` and `take-profit-value` (the fixed values replace the ATR stop and the target at the reference), `take-profit-atr` (when set, the target at that multiple of the ATR from the entry, in place of the reference) and the sizing flags (`lot`, `max-risk-pct`...). `trailing-atr` is refused. Default timeframe 1h (at most 1 day).
  - **On the chart:** the week's reference ("CME close"), from the reopen to the Friday close.
  - **After a restart:** the position found keeps the stop and target that are at the broker. The `cmegap-expiry` hours count from the opening read from the orders stored by the bot (without it, from the candle on which the position is found), and the exit before Friday still applies.
  - **Caveats:** the spread at gold's Sunday reopen is wide and the candles are mid-price, so real fills are worse. The global trading hours are in Belém time: Sunday 17:00 in Chicago is 20:00 in Belém during Chicago's winter and 19:00 during its summer; if the configured window does not include Sunday night, the strategy never enters. CME holidays (early close, late reopen) are not known, and if the broker's gold hours do not match CME's, adjust `cmegap-close`/`cmegap-open`.

- **Pairs (`pairs`, `intradayagent`, no LLM):** statistical arbitrage between two cointegrated assets, the agent's two assets (`pair`, e.g. `XAUUSDm;XAGUSDm`: the first is leg A, the second leg B; any other number of assets is refused at startup). From the rule of Gatev, Goetzmann and Rouwenhorst ("Pairs Trading: Performance of a Relative-Value Arbitrage Rule", Review of Financial Studies, 2006) come the entry at two deviations, the exit at the crossing and the equal-value legs ("one dollar long and one dollar short"); the spread over a rolling window is ours (the paper uses normalized prices and the fixed estimates of a formation period). In `ols` mode, the spread is the Engle and Granger (1987) residual, as in Vidyamurthy ("Pairs Trading: Quantitative Methods and Analysis", 2004). Defaults, all changeable through the parameters:
  - **Spread:** on each candle both assets have (`timeframe` default `1h`, ours: the paper uses daily candles), with `pairs-hedge`:
    - `ratio` (default): s = ln A − ln B, the logarithm of the price ratio (β = 1), consistent with the equal-value legs;
    - `ols`: s = ln A − β·ln B, with β the slope of the (OLS) regression of ln A on ln B, and leg B sized at β times the value of leg A (see Size); a β ≤ 0 is not traded.
    - In both, over the last `pairs-len = 250` candles in common (the paper's formation period, there one year of daily candles; on 1h, about 10 days), the Z-Score z = (s − mean) / standard deviation over the same candles. The window rolls forward every candle (the paper fixes the formation estimates for the following trading period). Candles of only one asset (feed failure, market closed) are left out of the computation. The decision for a given time waits for the other asset's candle, and a candle that arrives after the other asset has already moved on to the next one is discarded.
  - **Entry:** on the candle on which z falls to −`pairs-entry-z` (default 2, that is, −2) or below (coming from above), buys A and sells B (long spread); on the candle on which it rises to +`pairs-entry-z` or above, sells A and buys B ("when prices diverge by more than two historical standard deviations", in the paper). It enters on the cross, not on the level (ours: avoids reopening right after a time or stop exit, or entering at startup on an old divergence). Both legs at market, A first; if B fails, A is closed right away. One spread at a time. `direction` applies to leg A: `buy` only long spreads, `sell` only short ones. With `pairs-stop-z`, a divergence already beyond it is not opened.
  - **Exit:** both legs at market when z comes back to `pairs-exit-z` on its side (−`pairs-exit-z` on the long spread, +`pairs-exit-z` on the short one; 0, the mean, is our analog of the paper's "next crossing of the prices"), after `pairs-max-bars` (250) candles in common (ours, one formation window; the paper closes at the end of a 6-month trading period; 0 turns it off), or at |z| ≥ `pairs-stop-z` (default 0, off; the paper uses no stop). No stop loss or take profit at the broker.
  - **Size:** with `ratio`, each leg's `lot`, which must balance the values of the two (the paper opens "one dollar long and one dollar short"): value = lot × contract size × price. Without `lot`, each leg is worth half of the `position-fraction` of the free balance, which then must be below 1 (without `position-fraction`, the strategy's default, 0.5, ours: a quarter of the free balance in each leg). With `ols`, leg B is worth β times leg A: with `lot`, leg A uses its own lot and B is β × the value of A, rounded to the nearest multiple of B's lot (B's lot serves as the step: use the volume step, e.g. 0.01, for the most faithful hedge; the entry is skipped if B rounds to zero); without `lot`, the `position-fraction` is split 1/(1+β) and β/(1+β).
  - **Reconciliation:** on every candle of either asset, a leg open alone (after a close by a stop, by the limits, by hand, or by a close that failed) is closed, and so are two legs on the same side: the position is the spread or nothing.
  - **Also accepts:** `timeframe`, `direction`, `lot`, `position-fraction`, `leverage` and `min-position-value`. `stop-atr`, `take-profit-atr`, `max-bars`, `min-atr-pct` and `trailing-atr` are refused; with `stop-value`, `take-profit-value` or `max-risk-pct` the agent does not start (there is no stop).
  - **Positions opened by hand:** a lone leg (opened by hand in MT5, or left over from a close) is closed on the next candle of either leg.
  - **Protection:** the agent's break-even and trailing ([Position protection](#position-protection-breakeven-and-trailing-stop)) **must stay off**: they move and close each leg alone, breaking the hedge (the other leg is closed on the next candle). Do not put a stop or target on a leg by hand in MT5. The news guard and the daily limits can also close one leg before the other.
  - **After a restart:** the two legs found on opposite sides become the tracked spread again, and `pairs-max-bars` counts from the opening of leg A.
  - **Warm-up:** 276 candles in common (the window, the candle before it for the cross, and 10% of slack for candles of only one asset).
  - **Limitations:** the strategy does not test cointegration: choosing two cointegrated assets is the user's decision. The legs are not atomic, and each round trip pays the spread and fees of both. The backtest report counts each leg as a trade (the win rate is per leg). In a command-line backtest, `XAGUSDm` and `ETHUSDm` need `-mt5-contract-size` (the package does not know their contract size).
  - **On the chart:** the spread's Z-Score on the recent candles on which both assets have a candle ("Pairs Z-Score" panel).

- **After a restart:** the open position the strategy finds is tracked with the legs (stop loss and take profit) that are at the broker, read from the orders stored by the bot.
  - The trailing at the broker continues from them (`-trailing-atr` of the signal strategies, like `donchianatr`; `matrend -broker-stop`; `trendconfluence -broker-trailing-atr`), counting the best price since the entry from the loaded candles.
  - `MaxBars` counts the candles since the position's actual opening.
  - The `-max-trades` of `orb` and `asialondon` counts the strategy's own entries in the session and, after a restart, also the positions opened in the session before it, read from the orders stored by the bot (before, the count restarted from zero). Without a restart, a manual trade (opened by hand in MT5) does not count, as always.
  - Limitation: the stored orders do not say who opened the position. So a manual entry made before a restart, in the same session, starts counting after it. Storing the order's origin (a new migration) would close this case; it is a pending decision.
  - The stops on the close (`matrend`, `trendconfluence`) are rebuilt from the entry candle, when it is in the loaded window, and never end up wider than the stop at the broker.
  - Without a known opening time (e.g. a position opened outside the bot), the position is tracked from the current candle, as before.
- **Failed exit:** as in the LLM agent, the position is not left without stop loss and take profit. On MT5, the close goes out with them in place; in paper, they are canceled first and, if the close fails, placed back at the same levels. The close is retried at every candle close (including that of a `matrend` and `trendconfluence` reversal, whose signal has already passed), and a close that leaves the position unprotected is logged as an error.
- **Strategy error (panic):** applies to every agent, the LLM one included. A panic in the strategy or in one of its indicators (for example, an index out of range with the day's data) loses only that candle (or that tick of the forming candle) of the asset, and the bot carries on with the next one. The panic goes to the log with the stack and to the dashboard and Telegram as an error, at most one alert every 10 min for the same call (the next one says how many happened in between; those in between go to the log on one line, without the stack).
  - During warm-up (the candles loaded at startup), the panic would repeat at every start: the agent does not start, the error states the asset, the call and the candle, and the controller relaunches it with a growing wait (see **Supervision** in [Controller](#controller)).
  - In a backtest, the panic ends the backtest with the error, because the result would be wrong.
  - On the dashboard chart, a panic in an indicator leaves only the indicators out; the candle stays on the chart.
- **Candle order:** a candle's close always reaches the strategy before the next forming candle, even when they arrive together. Before, one of them could jump ahead and the close was discarded as late (in the LLM agent, without the analysis of that close).
- **Candle closed on MT5:** applies to every agent, the LLM one included. A candle closes when the terminal already has the next one. Without it (market idle, closed, or terminal disconnected), it closes 2 s after its end by the agent's clock, but only if the terminal confirms it was connected: a tick of the symbol after the candle's end (by the server clock) or the terminal connected to the server (`/health`) after the end, read before the candles.
  - Without the confirmation, the candle stays forming, in the warm-up and in the stream, until the terminal has the next candle or reconnects. A terminal disconnected in the middle of the candle no longer delivers the partial candle as closed. After 10 s like this, the log warns once per candle.
  - With the market idle and the terminal connected, the close is delayed by at most one read cycle (1 s).
  - A candle already delivered as closed that the terminal changes later (for example, rebuilt from the server's history on a reconnection) goes to the log with both values. It is not delivered again, because the strategy has already run on it.
  - Agent clock ahead of the server's by more than 2 s (mitigated, not solved): the lead is estimated from the ticks read (the smallest delay between the tick's server time and the read, among the last 20 new ticks read in the last 30 min), and the `/health` confirmation also waits out that lead, so that the candle closes by the server clock. Without that estimate (right after startup, before new ticks are read, or with no new ticks for more than 30 min) or with a lead above 10 min (treated as the server's time zone, not as clock drift), the agent's clock applies: a candle with no tick after its end can still close early, and the later change shows up in the log, as above. With the clock in sync (up to 2 s of difference), nothing changes, and with the market active the terminal's next candle closes the previous one, as before.
- **Candles kept by the strategy:** applies to every agent, the LLM one included. The bot keeps, per asset, the candles of three warm-ups (`WarmupPeriod`) plus the forming candle, not the whole history since the start. The strategy receives its warm-up at the close, as before. In a strategy that runs on the ticks of the forming candle (the LLM), the indicators run on every tick over these candles, not over a history that grows without limit (in the LLM, about 0.1 ms per tick, versus 4 ms with a month of M1). Recursive indicators (EMA, RSI, ATR, MACD) give the same values they would give over the whole history.
  - Checked before and after the change: the strategies' backtests produced the same orders, and the LLM received the same contexts, including in the interval consultations with the forming candle, except for the last digit of some numbers (sums in talib's order).
- **Protection of several positions on MT5:** a stop loss and take profit (OCO) that protects several positions of the asset is placed, moved and canceled position by position. If one of them fails midway, those already changed go back to their previous levels (when placing the OCO, no levels; when canceling, the levels from before; when moving, the old level). So none is left unprotected while the bot tracks the legs, nor at a level different from the leg's.
  - After that, the account is read again, and the error states the levels of each position (`as before`, `CHANGED` or `WITHOUT PROTECTION`) and whether the rollback failed. A position that was not rolled back also goes to the log as an error.
  - A failure on the first position, refused by the broker, has changed nothing: the error is the broker's, as before.
- **Requests to MT5:**
  - Closing, protecting or closing by an opposite position (close-by) uses the position's ticket, as MT5 requires. The identifier (the ticket of the order that opened it, which never changes) remains the key of the book and the history. The two differ only when the server reopens the position in a service operation (e.g. a swap charge), in which case a request by the identifier would be refused.
  - With a stops level (`trade_stops_level`) or a freeze level (`trade_freeze_level`) on the symbol, the adapter refuses before sending, with an error that states the distances: stop loss/take profit closer to the market than the stops level (for a position, from the bid/ask; for a pending order, from its price); a pending order closer to the market than that level; and a change to a pending order or to a position's levels within the freeze level. Only what changes is checked, and closes and cancellations are never blocked. Without these levels (as on Exness), nothing changes and no extra tick is read. The prices sent are rounded to the symbol's `trade_tick_size` when it is larger than the point (e.g. 0.25 on an index), besides the digits.
  - Partially filled pending order: it stays `PARTIALLY_FILLED` with the requested quantity while the rest can still be filled. Fully filled, it becomes `FILLED` with everything. If the rest is canceled by the bot, canceled in the terminal, or expires, it becomes `FILLED` with the filled quantity, per the history (without the history after 1 min, from the position's volume), and not `CANCELED`. The position keeps the order's levels, and the legs keep tracking it. On Exness (CFD), a partial fill is unlikely.
  - Cancellation of a pending order by the bot: after the removal, the adapter reads the asset's positions (one extra read per cancellation, skipped when the book already knows of the partial fill) to find a partial fill the book had not seen yet. With no position from the order, it becomes `CANCELED` right away, along with the legs. During that read, or if it fails, the order stays `PENDING_CANCEL` and the legs remain, until the next reads of the account and the history tell what happened: `FILLED` with the filled part or, if nothing was filled, `CANCELED` along with the legs. With no position from the order in the account and no history within 1 min, `CANCELED` applies, as for any pending order that leaves the book without history.
  - Limitation: a position that the terminal listed only after that read (unlikely, because the fill reaches the terminal before the removal) may leave the order `CANCELED` in the bot, with the legs canceled, although the position stays in the account with the order's levels.

### Controller

- **Supervision:** launches the enabled agents as child processes, with a new token at each start. With **Reiniciar se cair** (Restart if it crashes) on, an agent that crashes comes back with the settings it was started with (and the current secrets), after a wait that doubles at each crash (from 5 s up to 1 min, and back to 5 s after 1 min running); with it off, the agent stays stopped (see below). On Linux, the agents and the `services` stop together with the controller, even when it is killed (see [Stopping the controller](#stopping-the-controller)).
- **Configuration:** hands the agents their configuration through an internal API, on loopback only.
- **Records:** receives the orders, the decisions and the journal from the agents through a **durable outbox**. The agent writes locally and sends later, and the strategy flow never waits for the database.
  - **Writing to disk:** each record goes to the agent's outbox file (`data/outbox/<agente>-<id>.db`) and is synced (fsync) before the agent moves on. An fsync failure (for example, an I/O error) is a write error, like a full disk: the order is notified and rewritten, and the record is still sent while the agent runs. The file is compacted on open and, while running, when it exceeds 32 MiB and twice its size at the last compaction and no record is pending (so only the set-aside and the held records, which are few, stay in it). Compaction writes a copy, syncs the copy, swaps the file for it and syncs the directory (buntdb's automatic compaction, which syncs none of this, is turned off). The copy is written and synced without holding the agent's writes: a new record waits only for the file swap, which appends to the copy what was written in the meantime and reopens the (small) file, and the log says how long the compaction took. So a power loss loses at most the write in progress, which had not been confirmed yet. The limit is the storage itself: a disk or volume that confirms the fsync without writing (a write cache without a battery, some network or VM volumes) can lose what it confirmed.
  - **Sending:** in batches, in the order the records were written. A batch the controller cannot read (`400`, for example too large) is split in half right away, until the unreadable record is alone, and the others go on without waiting for a new order or decision. On stopping, the agent tries to send what is left for up to 10 s and warns in the log how many records were left for the next start.
  - **Record refused for good:** it is set aside in the outbox file, never resent, with an error in the agent's log, and the following ones are sent. This happens when the controller refuses it (`422`: unreadable record, invalid data, a database constraint or a PostgreSQL limit, such as an index row that is too large) or when it cannot read it alone (`400` or `413`) while it reads the records that come after it.
  - **Record that alone always fails** (`503`, for example a database error the controller does not treat as a refusal): a batch that fails 5 times in a row (or for 5 min) is split in half at each failure, until the first record is alone. After 5 failures and 5 min alone, each new failure of it is followed by a check, without writing, of the next records of the same type (order, decision, journal or prompt status, among the next 1000): if the controller writes those, it is retried right away, and the new failure counts as proof against it. It is only held (set aside for now) after failing for 30 min with 5 proofs in a row; a check that fails (for example, with an unstable database) resets the proofs. While the controller fails with the records of the same type (for example, with every order), or there is no record of the same type after it to check, nothing is held and the queue waits, and the dashboard shows the sending as failing.
  - **Held record:** setting aside a record that fails with `503` is a heuristic (a database unstable for a long time can still get a valid record held), so it is not lost. It stays in the outbox file, with an error in the agent's log, and the following ones are sent. It is resent alone when the controller writes records of the same type again and 15 min have passed since the last attempt (the wait doubles at each new failure, up to 4 h; after an agent restart, right after the first record of the same type is written). Once written, it leaves the outbox; refused by the controller (`422`), it stays set aside for good; if a newer record with the same key is written first (for example, a newer state of the same order or the prompt status), it is discarded, never coming back over the newer one.
  - **Memory:** the outbox keeps all records in memory, as in the file (the pending, the held and the set-aside ones). No pending record is discarded, because each one is the state of an order, a decision or a model query. So the log warning that the records are not reaching the controller becomes an error when the controller has been down for 1 h or more, or when the outbox exceeds 10000 records or 128 MiB; the error says how many records and how many MiB, and repeats every 5 min. An agent that restarts with the outbox above these limits also logs the error. Set-aside records (refused for good) are kept 7 days for analysis, at most the newest 1000, and then deleted, with a warning in the log. Those set aside by an earlier version, which did not store the time, count the 7 days from the agent's start.
  - **Pending records on the dashboard:** the agent's card (in Configurações, the Settings page) shows the records that have not reached the database yet when sending is failing or the oldest is more than 1 min old, the set-aside records and the held ones. The age is that of the oldest pending record, reported by the agent at each send (an agent of an earlier version does not report it after a written batch, and it is left out). It is what the agent reported at its last send; a restarted controller only knows it at the next send.
  - **Keys:** the controller writes each record by the keys of its content (the ID of the order, of the decision or of the journal entry; the prompt status is one per agent). Resending a record duplicates nothing, and a newer state of the same order, decision or status replaces the earlier one (the order never goes back, see below). The sequence (`seq`) only orders the outbox and restarts when it empties and the agent restarts.
  - **Unreadable outbox:** the agent does not start, and the log says which file and what to do. With the parameter `outbox-quarantine` = `true`, the file is moved to `<arquivo>.corrupt-<hora>` (kept for analysis) and the agent starts with an empty outbox; if the file opens and only some records are unreadable, only those are set aside. The records of the moved file do not reach the database (for example, the last state of an order). Remove the parameter afterwards. A file cut at the end (the agent killed in the middle of a write) opens with the earlier records, with a warning in the log.
  - **Order IDs:** the agent reserves the IDs in advance. Without an answer from the controller, it numbers beyond the reservation, and the controller reserves those IDs when it receives the orders. The request for the next reservation is repeated while the controller does not answer, with a warning in the log every 5 min, and stops when the agent stops or the controller refuses the token.
  - An order never goes back in the database or in the agent's memory: a final state (`FILLED`, `CANCELED`, `REJECTED`, `EXPIRED`) is not replaced by an open one, and a state is not replaced by an older one (`updated_at`). So the copy of an order written late (for example, that of a limit moved while the order was being filled) does not undo the fill after a restart. A final state replaces an open one even with an earlier time, because the fill time is the broker's and the time of a modification is the agent's. Resending the same state through the outbox changes nothing, and the refused state shows up as a warning in the log.
  - In the bot itself, the writes of an order do not cross. A modification (limit, stop loss, take profit) or a cancellation that the broker answers after the fill does not rewrite the order as open: it stays as it ended, and the modification returns the order in that state (the dashboard shows the order filled). A state read from the broker before a modification is read again in the next cycle (1 s), instead of undoing it. The cancellation writes the order as the bot tracks it (with the moved price), not an old copy. The dashboard and Telegram receive the states of each order in the order they were written, and a slow consumer (sending a notification, for example) does not hold the bot back. In paper and in the backtest, the local storage also never swaps a final state for an open one.
- **Interfaces:** serves the web dashboard (login with session and CSRF) and the Telegram bot.

### XTrader AI, the supervisor of the agents

**XTrader AI** is a language model (OpenAI or Anthropic) that watches all agents: it analyzes the whole set periodically, can halt an agent with a rationale and chats through the interface. It only acts through structured tools, which the controller validates and executes with the same protections as the other orders. The model never sends an order, never opens a position or loosens a stop, and never sees secrets.

- **Periodic analysis:** off by default. When on (it requires a provider, a model and a key), it runs at each configured interval (from 5 min to 24 h; 15 min in the seed), one at a time: one that coincides with another in progress is recorded as skipped. A model that is down, refuses the key or does not answer in time (`timeout_seconds`, 2 min in the seed) leaves the analysis as failed, without touching any agent, and the next interval tries again. On start, the controller marks as failed the analysis that was left in progress.
- **What the model receives:** a consolidated snapshot, the same as `GET /api/supervisor/overview`, stored at each analysis:
  - when it was generated, with the time zone, and the result windows (today, the last 24 h and each agent's accounting period), with start and end;
  - the MT5 accounts by alias ("conta 1", that is, account 1), with the currency, the balance, the time of the reading and the reason when they could not be read;
  - the exposure computed in code, per account, currency and pair: buys, sells and how many agents on each side;
  - per agent: name, type, strategy, mode, whether it is enabled and running, restarts, last exit, launch error, outbox, block and stop by the supervisor, pairs, timeframe, the parameters that limit risk (lot, leverage, `max-risk-pct`, stops etc.), the daily limits of the prompt, the model and prompt of LLM agents, positions (ticket, side, volume, prices, SL/TP, P/L), pending orders, open and realized result per currency, and the last 5 decisions, with the short reason;
  - the gaps: account not read, agent down.

  Money values always come with the account currency, never summed across currencies or converted. Never in the snapshot: keys, tokens, the bridge URL, the account login or the magic number.
- **Criteria:** the supervisor's instructions, editable, with the seed's default in three groups: operational (agent down, in a restart loop, outbox failing, position without a stop loss), risk (summed exposure in the same direction, position beyond the agent's own limits) and performance (only with a large enough sample, comparing the agent with itself). The financial limits are those of the [daily lock](#daily-lock-loss-and-gain-limits), applied by the system (the supervisor reports the state and does not repeat the action), and those configured in each agent.
- **Actions, always separate:**
  - **Bloquear novas entradas** (Block new entries): the agent does not open or increase positions, and keeps managing those it has (stop, trailing, strategy exits). Nothing is closed or canceled. It applies live, without restarting the agent, and also at the next start of a stopped agent;
  - **Parar o agente quando zerado** (Stop the agent when flat): blocks entries and cancels the pending entry orders; the agent keeps managing the positions and is only turned off when it has no position and no pending order. No position is liquidated or abandoned;
  - **Parar o agente agora** (Stop the agent now): only with confirmation, and refused if any position has no stop loss at the broker. The positions keep their stop loss and take profit at the broker;
  - **Cancelar pendentes de entrada** (Cancel pending entry orders): never the protections (stop loss and take profit) of a position;
  - **Fechar posições** (Close positions): in the periodic analysis, always a proposal for the user to confirm; in the chat, it runs right away when the user asks explicitly and completely (see **Chat**). Only the positions of the application's agents (by the magic number), never manual trades;
  - **Liberar as entradas** (Release entries): only the user;
  - **Arm the breakeven or the trailing of a position** (`protect_position`): only on agents that allow the feature ("XTrader AI pode acionar", XTrader AI can trigger), on a position the agent reported, with the agent's trigger kept (before it, the feature only stays armed and the agent applies it when the price gets there). The reason is mandatory and must treat the reversal as a possibility, never as a certainty. On its own, within the cap of actions per analysis; in the chat, when the user asks. The record keeps the reason, the evidence, the position, the earlier stop, the request and what the broker confirmed (see [Position protection](#position-protection-breakeven-and-trailing-stop)).
- **Autonomy** (configurable): on its own, the supervisor can block entries and stop when flat (the seed's default) and, if turned on, cancel pending entry orders, at most 3 actions per analysis (configurable, up to 10). The rest, and whatever goes past the cap, becomes a proposal for the user, which expires at the next interval. An autonomous action is refused when the snapshot has a gap in the agent or in its account, and the stop only happens when two analyses in a row ask for it for the same agent (the block applies from the first one).
- **Never reactivates:** the supervisor has no way to turn on an agent or release entries. Reactivating the agent in the settings also clears the stop mark and the block it created; a block created separately only goes away with **Liberar** (Release).
- **Chat:** a floating button, on every page, opens the XTrader AI panel. It answers using the snapshot (refreshed every 30 s at most) and the user's last 20 messages; the answer shows the time of the data. The answer arrives by streaming (SSE), written as the model writes it, in Markdown (lists, tables, bold, code and `http`/`https` links, never HTML). Until the text arrives, an animation says what it is doing: reading the agents, analyzing, querying an agent or building a proposal. An explicit and complete request (the action, the named agents or all of them, and the options, such as "close every position and block every agent") runs right away (`execute_action`), with no confirmation on screen: the backend builds the scope from a new reading of the accounts, stores the action as requested by the user (with the user's message as evidence), and the result, checked in the account, shows up in the chat written by the system. If an option is missing (cancel the pending orders? keep blocked? stop when flat or now?), the tool refuses and XTrader AI asks. XTrader AI's own suggestions are still proposals, built with the real list of affected agents, positions (tickets) and pending orders, and executed only with **Confirmar** (Confirm), within 10 min. An ambiguous request (nonexistent agent, more than one agent matching, empty scope) comes back as a question. Through the chat it also reads and sets the daily lock (`get_daily_limits`, `set_daily_limits`), always telling **one limit for the total of all agents** apart from **the same limit for each agent**, and asking when the request does not make it clear. The history is per user; the action log is a single one, visible to everyone.
- **Reliable execution:**
  - a confirmed proposal executes once, even with two clicks or two tabs (the move from "proposed" to "executing" is conditional in the database);
  - actions on the same agent do not cross, and closing several positions is one action at a time;
  - closing for a running agent is done by the agent itself, with "reduce only" orders: if the stop is hit in the middle, the order does not open a new position. An agent that is not running is kept from starting, and the position is closed directly at the bridge, checking before and after that the terminal is on the right account;
  - the result of each item comes from a new reading of the account, repeated for up to 15 s: closed, already closed, still open or unknown. An unknown result (no answer from the broker) is never resent;
  - a position opened after the proposal is not touched; each ticket counts once per account, even with agents on the same account;
  - partial failures stay as **Parcial** (Partial), with the result of each item;
  - the stop when flat is checked every 15 s: it only turns the agent off with the block confirmed in the agent, no position or pending order in the agent and in the account (read in full), seen that way in readings with at least 10 s between the first and the last, and with nothing pending in the outbox.
- **Record:** each analysis (the snapshot sent, the summary, the findings per agent, category and severity, the token usage) and each action (who asked and confirmed, reason, category, evidence, scope and result) are stored in the database. The agent's card and the dashboard bar show the blocked entries and the stop, with who did them (XTrader AI or the user) and the reason.

### Position protection: breakeven and trailing stop

Each agent can protect its open positions with **breakeven** and **trailing stop**, off by default: without configuring them, the agent behaves as before. XTrader AI can **arm** each feature on a position, when the agent allows it.

- **Parameters** (all in multiples of Wilder's **ATR(14)** of the closed candles of the agent's timeframe, converted into each asset's price; an agent with XAUUSDm and BTCUSDm uses the same values on both):

  | Feature | Parameter | What it is |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Breakeven | gatilho (trigger) | favorable move since the entry to activate |
  | | margem (margin) | how far beyond the entry the stop goes, added to the known costs |
  | Trailing stop | gatilho (trigger) | favorable move since the entry to activate |
  | | distância (distance) | how far behind the price the stop stays |
  | | passo (step) | least improvement of the stop to send an update to the broker |

  Each feature also has **Ligado** (On) and **XTrader AI pode acionar** (XTrader AI can trigger), independent of each other. Validation: up to 50 ATR; the breakeven with the trigger above the margin; the trailing with the step up to the distance.
- **Prices:** the move and the trailing use the exit price: the bid on a buy, the ask on a sell (the spread of the MT5 candles). The entry is the position's average price, not the candle close.
- **Breakeven:** with the move since the entry (the best price reached) at the trigger, the stop goes to the entry + margin + known costs (the entry and exit commissions, the exit one estimated equal to the entry one, and the negative swap, converted into price; in paper, the fee), rounded toward the protection side. **This does not guarantee a zero net result:** spread and slippage on the exit, and future commission and swap can leave the result negative. Without a known cost, the dashboard says "custos desconhecidos" (unknown costs).
- **Trailing stop:** active once the move reaches the trigger; then, at each price, the candidate stop sits the "distância" behind the exit price, rounded away from the market, and is only sent when it tightens the current stop by at least the step (and one tick). Deterministic: no model is queried at each update.
- **Coordination:** with protection on or armed in an agent, everything that moves the stop of an open position (the strategy, the LLM agent, the breakeven, the trailing and XTrader AI) goes through the same guard of the order controller: they only **tighten**, comparing with the live level at the broker, read again under the pair's lock. The most protective stop always wins: a less protective request is refused and whoever asked starts following the stop as it is. `llm-widen-stop` has no effect. The manual adjustment from the dashboard stays free and can loosen; the trailing tightens again if its rule says so. The legs of a pending entry are not guarded: they follow the entry.
- **Failures:** nothing is removed before an update. If the broker refuses it, the stop stays where it was and the next attempt waits 10 s; with the answer lost, MT5 returns the earlier levels and the stop is read again, never resent blindly. At most one update every 2 s per position.
- **Position without a stop:** gets one at the feature's level when the trigger is hit (on MT5, keeping the take profit).
- **Restart:** the broker's stop is the state; the best price since the entry is read again from the candles. A position adopted after a restart continues from where it was, without loosening.
- **Where it shows:** in the chart card, below the header, the state of each feature on the position: waiting for the trigger (how far, in ATR), active (the stop), unavailable (e.g., no ATR yet), the last send (requested, confirmed by the broker or refused, with the stop kept) and, when armed by XTrader AI, its badge with the reason. A stop only shows as applied after the broker confirms it.
- **Only with the agent running:** the tracking runs in the agent (paused from the dashboard or with entries blocked, it continues); with the process stopped, the position keeps the stop it has at the broker.

### Account currency

All money in the application is in the MT5 account currency, as the broker shows it (BRL on an account in reais, USD on one in dollars), with no exchange conversion by the application. Price is still price, in the pair's quote.

- **Point value:** how much a move of 1 in the price is worth, in the account currency, for 1 unit of the asset: the symbol's `trade_tick_value ÷ trade_tick_size ÷ trade_contract_size`, as MT5 reports it (the tick value already comes in the account currency), read again every minute. It is what turns a price distance into money.
- **Strategies and LLM:** the free cash (`Position`) is the account's. `stop-value`, `take-profit-value`, `min-position-value` and `max-risk-pct` are in the account currency: "losing 50" with `stop-value 50` on a BRL account is the price distance that loses 50 BRL (50 ÷ (units × point value)). The size by fraction of the balance is `saldo × fração × alavancagem ÷ (preço × valor do ponto)` (balance × fraction × leverage ÷ (price × point value)). The LLM receives the balance, the open P/L and the `value_per_price_unit` in the account currency (`account_currency`), and the prices in `quote_currency`.
- **Dashboard:** net profit, the P/L of the orders and of the position, and limits in the account currency. A close still without the broker's value shows as "aguardando a corretora" (waiting for the broker), left out of the sums. The Total column (price × quantity, in the quote) and the core's fee only show in the paper wallet.
- **Telegram:** `/balance` shows the account and `/profit` sums what the broker recorded (those still waiting, apart). The profit notification waits for the broker's value for up to 60 s; without it, it says it is waiting for the broker.
- **Paper:** still simulated in the pair's quote currency (e.g., USD), with a point value of 1, and labeled as a simulation everywhere.
- **Old values:** money parameters configured before this change were in the quote (USD); they now count in the account currency. The initial agent `matrend` uses `stop-value 5.4` and `take-profit-value 10.8` (BRL, about 1 and 2 in price on one unit of XAUUSDm, with the point value at 5.39 BRL on 2026-10-29).

### Daily lock: loss and gain limits

Maximum loss and maximum gain limits for the day, for all agents summed and for each agent. When a limit is hit, the application **flattens everything and stops**: it closes the positions in scope, cancels the pending entry orders and blocks entries until the day turns. The agents keep running, without opening anything.

- **Scopes:**
  - **all agents** (Configurações › Limites, Settings › Limits): one limit on the summed result, in the accounts' currency, with no conversion. With accounts in different currencies, the same value applies to the result of each currency separately, and when it is hit in any of them, it flattens and blocks all agents;
  - **each agent** (Configurações › Agentes › Limite diário, Settings › Agents › Daily limit): in the currency of the agent's account. An entry only happens if both the global limit and the agent's allow it.
- **Result of the day:**
  - realized since 00:00 (America/Belem, the controller's time zone and the same as the LLM daily limits): the day's exits with swap, commission and fees, plus the commission and fee of the day's entries;
  - plus the open P/L (profit + swap) of the positions open now;
  - read from the deals and positions of the MT5 accounts every 5 s, each trade once per account, even with agents sharing the account;
  - manual positions (without an agent's magic number) do not count and are never touched; agents in paper do not count, but are flattened and blocked with all the others.
- **Trigger:** the limit must be hit in two readings in a row, and the lock triggers once per day and scope. The closing:
  1. blocks the agents' entries and waits for the orders being sent to finish;
  2. cancels the pending entry orders and closes the positions: by the agent itself if it is running, directly at the bridge with the launch locked if it is stopped;
  3. checks in the account. A position that still shows up is closed again, up to 3 attempts with 15 s between them; an unknown result is never resent blindly.
- **Least margin for new entries:** with a maximum loss set, the new entries of the agents in scope are blocked, without flattening anything, when the margin left until the maximum loss falls below the least margin. The margin is the maximum loss plus the worse of the realized result and the result with the open positions, so that a profit that is still open never lends margin. The least margin is set in each limit (Configurações › Limites and each agent's limit), as a % of the maximum loss: 10% by default, 0 turns it off; the screen shows the value in money.
  - **Block:** below the least margin in two readings in a row, entries are blocked (waiting for the orders being sent to finish) and the lock is stored (`daily_margin_guards`, with the trigger and release events in `daily_limit_events`). The open positions keep their stops, including those of the daily loss. In the dashboard band, **Margem mínima atingida: sem novas entradas** (Least margin reached: no new entries), in amber, with the reason and the least margin next to the limits.
  - **Release:** the margin back at the least margin in two readings in a row (for example, with positions closing in profit), the least margin or the limit turned off, or the day turning.
  - **Why:** on 2026-11-17, the daily loss stop closed a position at −99.99 BRL on a limit of 100. Since the lock only triggers from −100.00, entries stayed released with R$ 0.01 of margin; three agents entered together at 08:30, already negative from the spread, and the day closed at −129.59 by the time it was flattened. With the least margin, entries would have been blocked at 08:16.
  - **Limit change:** if the change leaves the margin below the least margin, the preview warns; the block comes in the following readings, without confirmation.
- **Stops at the broker (maximum loss):** with a maximum loss set and the limit's **Proteger com stops na corretora** (Protect with stops at the broker) switch on (in each limit, global or the agent's; on by default), protection does not depend only on the flattening done by the application. Every minute, and right away on a limit change, the controller computes for each scope the margin left: the maximum loss plus the result of the day (realized + open), minus the estimated exit costs (the entry commission and fee of each position). Then:
  - **Split:** the margin is split among the open positions in scope in proportion to each one's loss to its stop now, and the stops move closer to the price in the same proportion. A position without a stop goes in by its exposed value. If the current stops already fit in the margin, nothing changes; a position whose stop is tighter than its share keeps it and leaves the rest to the others. With all agents, each account currency is split separately, like the limit. No position gets the whole margin.
  - **Level:** each position's share becomes a stop level through the symbol's tick value on the account (`trade_tick_value_loss`, otherwise `trade_tick_value`), rounded to the tick toward the price. It stays one tick beyond the broker's minimum distance (`trade_stops_level`); when that distance forces it, the stop is looser than the share, with a note saying so.
  - **Only tightens:** a tighter stop (from the strategy, the breakeven, the trailing or an earlier round) stays as it is. The stop is only moved when it tightens by more than 2% of the distance to it (one tick at least). A position covered by both limits (the global one and the agent's) gets the tighter stop.
  - **Who applies it:** the running agent, through the same path as the breakeven, which answers with the broker's result; while there is a limit stop, the agent's stop guard stays on (the strategy and the LLM do not loosen the stop). A stopped agent, launched by the controller, has its stop adjusted directly at the bridge (`TRADE_ACTION_SLTP`, the take profit kept, the launch locked), checked in the account afterwards; inside the broker's freeze level (`trade_freeze_level`), the adjustment waits for the next round. An agent running outside the controller is left out, with the reason.
  - **Excluded:** the legs of `pairs` (they protect each other) and an agent with buys and sells on the same asset keep only the flattening.
  - **Record:** each requested stop, the margin and the position's share, and the last send (confirmed, refused, no answer or not sent, with the reason) are stored in `daily_stops`; they show in **Configurações › Limites › Stops na corretora agora** (Settings › Limits › Stops at the broker now) and, on the chart, the position's protection shows **Limite diário** (Daily limit) with the stop. A requested stop holds while the position is open, the limit and the switch are on and the day does not turn, and it is handed again to an agent that restarts. Turning off the switch or the limit ends the requests; the stops already placed stay at the broker as they are.
  - **Limitations:** when hit, the stops leave the day near the limit, never exactly at it: slippage and gaps go past the level, and the flattening still applies behind them. With the margin used up (the result at the limit), no new stop is requested: the flattening takes care of the positions.
- **What still applies:** stop loss, trailing, breakeven, closes and reductions. The lock does not touch protection or exit orders.
- **What does not get around the lock:** restarting the application or the agent, turning the agent back on, **Liberar entradas** (Release entries), by the user or by XTrader AI, and resetting the balance. The lock is stored (`daily_guards`) and the entries handed to the agents combine the supervisor's block and the lock's. Only the day turning or an explicit limit change releases it.
- **Limit change:** reevaluated right away. If it would trigger or release the lock now, the screen and the chat show the effect and ask for confirmation; each change is recorded (`daily_limit_events`) with who asked, from where (settings or chat), the reason and the old and new values.
- **Incomplete data:** without reading an account in two readings in a row, entries are blocked, without flattening anything, until the reading comes back. What is missing never becomes zero.
- **Where it shows:** the **Limite diário** band at the top of the dashboard, full width, right below the agent bar (the same for any agent shown; the floating XTrader AI panel does not repeat it); and the **Limites** (Limits) tab in Configurações, which only configures (the global card does not show the state of the day, and the per-agent table shows each agent's). The state and the reason stay in the band: the agent bar and the agents' cards do not repeat the warning. At the top, the band shows the state as a padlock on a badge in the state's color (closed with entries blocked: daily gain or loss reached, or result of the day incomplete; open with them released; the state spelled out on mouse hover and for screen readers) and the flatten button on the right; below, the result of the day highlighted, with the realized and the open, and the limits; last, the limit hit and the time, and until when entries stay blocked (e.g., **Perda diária atingida às 10:27: entradas bloqueadas até 00:00 (Belém)** (Daily loss reached at 10:27: entries blocked until 00:00 (Belém))), or the reason for an incomplete result, and the agents blocked by their own limit. With a limit hit, it takes the color of a gain (green) or of a loss (red); with the result incomplete, amber. With no limit set, it shows only the label, **Sem limite definido** (No limit set) and the button below.
- **Zerar + Cancelar ordens** (Flatten + Cancel orders): the red button on the right of the band (it flattens every position and cancels the pending orders), different from each agent's **×**. It only shows with open positions or pending orders of the agents (while the account reading is not done yet or is incomplete, it shows); on the phone, only the icon. It asks for confirmation in the card itself (saying what it does and what it never touches) and runs an XTrader AI `close_positions` action over all registered agents, stored as requested by the user.
  - **Coordination:** a running agent gets a single request, which holds the entries, cancels the pending entry orders and closes all its pairs at market, in that order; a pending order filled while it is being canceled is closed along with them. The agents are served together, up to 8 at a time, and not one after the other (before, a flattening with 5 positions took about 24 s); the result lists each agent in name order. The same applies to the flattening of the daily limit and of the end of a trading hours window, and the **×** of different agents runs at the same time. The entries stay held for one more candle and are then released: the action never blocks for good, does not disable agents and does not change the limits. A stopped agent has its launch locked, its orders removed at the bridge and then everything it holds closed, including a position opened in the meantime.
  - **Protections:** the close is at market, reduce-only; the position's stop and target stay until it closes.
  - **Scope:** only the agents' positions and orders (by the magic number); manual ones and those of other systems are never touched.
  - **Result:** checked in the accounts, never taken as success just for having been requested: the closed positions, the canceled orders and, under **O que resta** (What remains), each position or order that is still open, failed or has an unknown result. A close with an unknown result is never resent: it shows up to be checked in the account. The button stays disabled while the action runs; a second request, or an agent's **×** in the meantime, is refused (`409`).
- **Limitations:** an order sent before the block can be filled (it is flattened right after); the MT5 history lags a few seconds (a position just closed keeps its last open P/L for up to 60 s); between the reading and the close the price moves, so the final result can go a little past the limit.

### Trading hours

One schedule for all agents (Configurações › Limites › **Horário de operação**, Settings › Limits › Trading hours), with one or more windows per day (e.g., 08:00–10:00 and 12:00–14:00), in HH:MM Belém time, the controller's time zone.

- **Windows:** up to 24. Each one starts before it ends, within the same day: a window never crosses midnight (to cover the night, use 22:00–23:59 and 00:00–02:00). They cannot overlap or touch (10:00–12:00 and 12:00–14:00 become one). They can be saved in any order and are kept in the order of the day. When on, the schedule needs at least one window. The windows are in `trading_hours_windows` (migration 40); the first one is also in the columns of `trading_hours`, for the earlier readers. A single-window schedule saved before still applies as the first window.
- **Agents of each window:** each window's agents button picks one or more enabled agents that can open positions in it. With none checked, the window applies to all. With agents checked, the others are blocked during it, with the reason (e.g., `Janela 08:00–10:00 só para highlow, matrend` (Window 08:00–10:00 only for highlow, matrend)). The agents are in `trading_hours_window_agents` (migration 41), by id: renaming an agent keeps it in the window. The end actions apply to all agents, whichever agents the window has.
- **Assets of each window:** each window's **Ativos** (Assets) field takes the assets the agents can open positions on in it, as MT5 names them, one chip per asset: once an asset is typed, Enter (or `;` or `,`) turns it into a chip, as does leaving the field. Several pasted at once (e.g., `XAUUSDm;BTCUSDm`) become one chip each, a repeated asset does not go in again, a chip's × removes it and Backspace in the empty field removes the last one. A chip that is not an MT5 asset name turns red, with the reason. Empty, the window applies to all assets. With assets, an entry on another asset is refused by the agent's strategy, with the reason (e.g., `Janela 08:00–10:00 só para os ativos XAUUSDm, BTCUSDm` (Window 08:00–10:00 only for the assets XAUUSDm, BTCUSDm)). Exits, stops and targets always pass, and the end actions apply to all positions. The assets are in `trading_hours_window_pairs` (migration 42).
  - **How it reaches the agents:** along with the entries the controller sends (`entries_pairs` and `pairs_reason` in `agentapi.Entries`, in the same revision). The agent keeps the assets in an `agentapi.PairGate`, which the entry filter (`entryfilter`) checks before each strategy entry. In an agent with no filter configured, the filter is installed only for this and does not measure or read anything else. The LLM agent checks the same gate when applying a decision, as it does with the daily limit and the news. In the backtest, the batch's schedule updates the gate at each window.
- **Outside the windows:** no agent opens a position. The entries of all agents are blocked, as in the daily lock: sent right away to the running agents, waiting for the orders being sent, and applied by the stopped ones when they start. When the next window opens, they are released by the agents' runtime. The reason says until when, e.g., `Fim da janela 08:00–10:00: entradas bloqueadas até 12:00` (End of window 08:00–10:00: entries blocked until 12:00).
- **At the end of each window**, as checked **in it**:
  - **Zerar posições** (Flatten positions): closes the agents' positions (a `close_positions` action, of automatic origin, with the same path as the **Zerar + Cancelar ordens** (Flatten + Cancel orders) button: by the agent if it is running, at the bridge with the launch locked if it is stopped, checked in the account);
  - **Cancelar ordens pendentes** (Cancel pending orders): cancels the pending entry orders (along with the close, under the same hold of the entries, or in a `cancel_pending` of all agents when only this one is checked).
  - Unchecked, whatever is open stays as it is, with the stops; only new entries are blocked.
  - Only the agents' positions and orders (by the magic number); manual ones are never touched.
- **Once per window:** the actions run when the window ends after having been open, with what it had checked when it opened or at the last change made while it was open.
  - With the application stopped at the end of a window, they run when it comes back, even if it is already the next window. In that case, entries stay blocked until the positions are closed, and only then does the next window open.
  - Changing the open window while keeping the current time inside it does nothing (the new end applies); changing it so that the current time falls outside every window counts as its end, right away.
  - Turning the schedule off with a window open releases the entries without flattening anything, and turning it on later outside the windows only blocks.
  - Turning the schedule on outside the windows only blocks entries. Restarting the application does not repeat the actions or release the block.
- **Record:** each schedule change, each opening (with the window) and closing, and the end actions of each window (with the window, the action and the result) are stored in `trading_hours_events`; the id of the last event goes into the revision of the agents' entries.
- **Where it shows:** the **Horário de operação** card on the Limites tab (state and windows) and, on the dashboard's daily limit card, a badge with a clock: **Aberto · fecha às 10:00** (Open · closes at 10:00) or **Fora do horário · abre às 12:00** (Outside hours · opens at 12:00), with the windows on mouse hover.

### Exposure per asset

A maximum of agents with a position on the same asset and in the same direction at the same time, for all agents, set by rules (Configurações › Limites › **Exposição por ativo**, Settings › Limits › Exposure per asset): with the rule `XAUUSDm, BTCUSDm | 2`, if `trend` and `orb` are already long XAUUSDm, a third agent does not buy XAUUSDm, but can sell it or buy BTCUSDm.

- **Rules:** each rule has its **Ativos** (Assets), as MT5 names them, one chip per asset (typed like the assets of the trading hours windows), and the **Máximo de agentes** (Maximum agents), from 1 to 100. The maximum applies to each asset of the rule separately, per direction (buys and sells count separately): with `USOILm, UKOILm | 4`, up to 4 agents long USOILm and up to 4 long UKOILm. **Adicionar regra** (Add rule) creates another rule, for assets with another maximum; the trash icon removes it. An asset stays in one rule only, and there are at most 50 assets.
- **No rule:** an asset without a rule has no limit, and with no rule at all the limit is off. A rule left blank is ignored on save.
- **When opened again:** the assets with the same maximum show together in one rule, by name, and the rules by maximum: the controller stores each asset's maximum.
- **Who takes a slot:** each agent with a position on the asset and in the direction, read from the MT5 accounts by the magic number, or with a pending entry order on it (the pending order of an agent that already has a position on the asset protects or adds to the position, and does not take another slot). An agent counts once, however many positions it has. Manual positions (without an agent's magic number) and agents in paper do not count.
- **How it is checked:** before sending each entry, the agent asks the controller for the slot (`POST` to `agentapi.ExposurePath`, with the asset and the side). The controller serves one request at a time, with the accounts read at most 2 s before, and reserves the granted slot for 2 minutes, until the order shows up in the account. So agents entering in the same second never go past the limit together, and the slot of an order that was never sent becomes free again. An agent that is already on the asset and in the direction always gets the slot.
- **Entry refused:** the strategy does not send the order and records the reason, e.g., `Exposição máxima em XAUUSDm: 2 agentes já estão comprados (orb, trend)` (Maximum exposure on XAUUSDm: 2 agents are already long (orb, trend)). Without a reading of some account (bridge not answering, token refused), the entries with a limit are refused (`Não foi possível conferir a exposição: sem leitura de …` (Could not check the exposure: no reading of …)): nothing goes in blindly.
- **How it reaches the agents:** along with the entries the controller sends (`exposure` in `agentapi.Entries`). With the limit on, the entry filter (`entryfilter`) asks for the slot after the other blocks have let the entry through; in an agent with no filter configured, the filter is installed only for this. The LLM agent asks for the slot when applying a decision, after checking the daily limit, the news and the window's assets. A change of the limit applies to the next entries.
- **What is never limited:** exits, stops, targets and protection orders. The limit does not close anything already open: if it goes down with more agents on the asset, only the new entries wait.
- **Where it applies:** only on agents in live. Agents in paper, which have no positions in the account, are never refused and never take a slot, and the backtest, where each agent runs alone, is not limited.
- **Where it is stored:** in `exposure_limits` (migration 43), a single record, with each asset's maximum in `pairs` and who changed it last. The `max_agents` column, from the first version's single maximum for all assets, is no longer read or written (it stays at 0).

### Agents per asset

Which agents can open positions on each asset, for all agents, set by rules (Configurações › Limites › **Agentes por ativo**, Settings › Limits › Agents per asset): with the rule `XAUUSDm, XAGUSDm → orb, trend`, only `orb` and `trend` open positions on XAUUSDm and XAGUSDm; the other agents keep trading their other assets.

- **Rules:** each rule has its **Ativos** (Assets), as MT5 names them, one chip per asset, and the **Agentes que podem operar** (Agents that can trade), picked in a dialog (at least one). An asset stays in one rule only; there are at most 50 rules. **Adicionar regra** (Add rule) creates another; the trash icon removes it; a rule left blank is ignored on save.
- **No rule:** an asset without a rule is free for all agents that trade it; with no rule at all, nothing changes.
- **How it is applied:** the controller computes, for each agent, the agent's assets that the rules allow, crossed with those of the open trading hours window (when the window limits the assets), and sends the list along with the entries (`entries_pairs` in `agentapi.Entries`, with the reason in `pairs_reason`). The strategy (the entry filter) and the LLM agent check the list before each entry, as they already did with the window's assets, and record the reason for the refusal, e.g., `Agentes por ativo: XAUUSDm só para os agentes orb, trend` (Agents per asset: XAUUSDm only for the agents orb, trend). It applies right away, without restarting.
- **No asset allowed:** an agent that cannot trade any of its assets has its entries blocked, with the reason (`Nenhum ativo permitido. …` (No asset allowed. …)), and trades again when a rule includes it.
- **What is never affected:** exits, stops, targets, protection orders and positions already open.
- **Where it applies:** on agents in live and in paper. In the backtest, when the batch checks **Respeitar os agentes por ativo de Configurações › Limites** (Respect the agents per asset of Settings › Limits) (checked by default; see [Backtesting](#backtesting-1)).
- **Renaming an agent** updates its name in the rules.
- **Where it is stored:** in `asset_agents` (migration 48), a single record, with the rules in `rules`, a revision that grows at each change (the agents get the newest one) and who changed it last.

  - below, in tabs: the order history; the model's decisions, with each one's context, and the analyses in which the model was not consulted ("Não consultado", Not consulted), with the reason, and the **Reanalisar agora** (Reanalyze now) button, enabled by the agent's **Permitir reanálise** (Allow reanalysis) switch; and the equity curve (with a paper wallet). The chosen tab is kept in the browser. The card (**Atividade do agente**, Agent activity) has a fixed height, whatever its content: that of the news card, measured as it changes (the chart resized, an LLM agent's last decision), or 640 px when the two are on the same row or the news is hidden. The title, the tabs, the summary and the pages stay in place; only the table scrolls when it exceeds the height, with the header visible (on a phone, the row cards scroll inside the card), and the equity curve fills the card's space.
- **Hiding the values:** the eye button in the top bar hides, on every page, the balances and money values (P/L, balance, profit, daily limits, the values on the Performance page, the position's P&L on the chart), with `•••••` in place of the digits; the same button shows them again. Prices, quantities and percentages stay visible, and so do the gain and loss colors; backtest results, which are simulations, do not change. The choice is kept in the browser, the same in every tab.
- **LLM cards:** **Sentimento do mercado**, **Última decisão do LLM** (Last LLM decision) and the **Decisões do LLM** (LLM decisions) tab appear only in LLM agents; the other cards belong to all agents. In an agent of another kind, these cards stay out of the dashboard without leaving space, but do not count as hidden: their position in the saved layout stays the same, even if another card is moved in the meantime, and they return to it when switching to an LLM agent. When switching agents, the dashboard starts from scratch: while the new agent loads, the placeholder blocks already follow its kind (without the sentiment one in an agent without LLM), and nothing from the previous agent stays on screen.
- **Appearance:** the cards are glass: translucent over the page's light, with the background blurred behind them, a thin border and a soft shadow, in both themes; the chart is drawn over the glass. What sits on top of them (lists, dialogs, the XTrader AI panel, the full-screen chart) stays opaque. On a phone the blur is lighter; without blur support in the browser, or with **reduzir transparência** (reduce transparency) or **mais contraste** (increase contrast) turned on in the system, the cards fall back to the opaque panel.
- **Card layout:** the layout button in the top bar (grid icon) opens the dashboard customization. A card that joins the dashboard after the layout was saved (such as the open P/L) appears in a row of its own at the end, without moving the rest; **Restaurar padrão** (Restore default) puts it in its place in the default layout. The dashboard is made of rows, each row of columns and each column of stacked cards, and any card goes to any row or column:
  - **Moving:** each card gets a bar above it, dragged with the mouse or a finger. Dropped on the left or right edge of another card, it becomes a column next to that card's column; on the top or bottom edge, it is stacked above or below it; in the middle, the two swap places; in a strip between rows (or at the end), it gets a row of its own; in the empty space of a row, it joins as a column at the end of that row. With the bar focused, the keyboard arrows also move the card: up and down within the column (at its top or bottom, to a row of its own; alone in the row, it swaps the row with the neighboring one) and sideways (it leaves the column for a column of its own on that side; alone in the column, it swaps places with the neighboring column).
  - **No reserved space:** a column or row left without cards (because the card was moved or hidden) disappears, and the other cards of the row share the width. The ✕ hides the card (display only: the data and functions continue, and it comes back through the **Mostrar** (Show) button, in the same place).
  - **Widths and heights:** the columns of a row share the width, each with a minimum (300 px the quote, 240 px the sentiment, 280 px the results, 260 px the decision, 360 px the chart and the activity, never more than an equal share of the row). A column alone in its row takes the whole row. In a row of only compact cards (quote, sentiment, results, decision), they have the same height; next to the chart, the activity or a column of several cards, each one takes the height of its own content.
  - **Chart:** it is the only card that resizes, by its edges: the bottom bar changes the height (from 280 px up to the window's, or automatic), the right one the width (from 360 px up to its row's, minus the minimum of the row's other columns, or automatic) and the corner, both. The row's other cards adjust while it is dragged. Alone in the row, dragged to the row's end, it goes back to automatic width and takes the whole row, and a card dropped in the space beside it gets the rest. A saved width larger than the screen can fit is reduced down to the minimum of the other cards, without overflowing the page. The cards stacked with it follow the column's width. The chart redraws at the new size, and moving the card does not recreate it: the zoom and position remain (and so does the tab chosen in the activity).
  - **Smaller screens:** below 1200 px, a row of three or more columns gets two; below 1024 px (tablet), the rows stack, with the chart at screen width (the chosen width does not apply), except the rows of only compact cards (each in its own column), which stay in two columns; on a phone (below 768 px), everything is in one column. The default layout stays as before at every width.
  - Only the bars drag: the buttons, fields and chart of the cards (zoom, dragging the chart, moving orders) keep responding. Outside customization, the dashboard looks as it does without it. The arrangement, the hidden cards and the chart size are stored in `localStorage` (`xtrader.dashboard.layout`, version 2; an arrangement saved in the previous format, of areas, is converted into rows) and come back on reload; without a saved arrangement, with an invalid one, or without `localStorage`, the default layout applies. **Configurações › Layout** (Settings › Layout) shows what was customized, and **Restaurar layout padrão** (Restore default layout) removes only that preference, applying the default at once, including on dashboards already open.
- **Chart timeframe:** a selector in the corner of the chart shows the asset in **1m, 5m, 15m, 30m, 4h or D** (daily), and in the strategy's execution timeframe when it is not among those. The selection changes only the view: the execution timeframe (the agent's `timeframe` parameter, marked with a dot in the selector) remains the timeframe of the candles the strategy receives, of the LLM analyses and of the decisions; it only changes in the agent's settings, with a restart.
  - In the execution timeframe, the chart is the usual one (candles, indicators, orders and analysis). In the others, the candles come from the agent (`GET …/{par}/candles`), the orders and the LLM analysis are aligned to the candle that contains each time, the strategy's indicators (computed in the execution timeframe) are not drawn, and the position, the pending orders and the reanalysis stay the same, in real time.
  - Where the candles come from: in an agent connected to the broker (MT5) or in paper, the closed ones are read from the broker (cached for 2 s, one read serving all dashboards) and the forming candle is built by the agent: above the execution timeframe, from its candles (exact); below it, from the prices the chart received (approximate: open at the previous close, high and low of the prices received, no volume), corrected by the broker when it closes. In a backtest, only the timeframes above the execution one, multiples of it, built from the strategy's candles; the others appear disabled.
  - The dashboard rereads the last candles of the timeframe shown every 15 s and when the period of the last one ends, and carries each event's price to the forming candle. A late response from a previous timeframe or asset is discarded: while the new one loads, the chart stays empty with "Carregando candles de …" (Loading candles of …), never with the candles of another timeframe. The chosen timeframe stays in the address (`?tf=4h`).
- **Slow client:** an asset's snapshot is built at once and only then sent, without holding up the agent: a phone downloading slowly (through ngrok, for example) does not delay the orders, the candles or the other requests. The account currency comes from the connection to MT5, without querying the broker on each snapshot. The candles reach the strategy and the chart through separate queues, in order, and a busy chart does not delay the strategy. The queues have no limit, because dropping or merging candles would change what the strategy sees: a queue with 1000 candles or more (a stuck consumer) logs a warning, at most one per minute. Non-finite numbers (NaN, infinity) of an order or of the position go out as 0, and a chart line with them is left out: the asset's snapshot and events do not fail because of them.
- **Chart window:** each asset's chart keeps the last 5000 closed candles (about 3 days on M1, 17 on M5 and 7 months on H1), plus the indicators' warm-up before them. So the snapshot and the indicators, recomputed at every close and every snapshot, do not grow with the agent's running time. The orders all remain in the dashboard, for the statistics. An order older than the first kept candle appears on the first candle, as already happened with the orders of previous runs. The "Buy & hold" counts only the chart's candles. A backtest's chart keeps all candles.
- **Reconnection:** when the real-time events drop (network, agent restarting) and come back, the dashboard reloads the asset's snapshot, the decisions and the bot state, because what happened during the drop (orders, position, candles) does not arrive through the events. A browser that does not keep up with the events (256 in its queue, for example on a slow network) loses the following ones: the agent ends its event stream, and the dashboard reconnects and reloads the same way, instead of staying out of date. If the connection is refused, the dashboard checks the session: with no session (expired or ended), it goes to the login; with one, or with no answer from the dashboard, it tries again every 5 s.
- **Bot:** start and stop each agent's bot in the state at the top of the dashboard, next to the agent and the pair. The button itself tells the state: **Parar bot** (Stop bot), confirmed in place, with the bot running, **Iniciar bot** (Start bot) with it stopped; with no bot to control, only **desativado** (disabled) appears. It applies only to the agent shown: the others keep trading.
  - **Stopped stays stopped:** the stop (from the dashboard or the Telegram `/stop`) is recorded in `agent_bot_controls` (migration 44), with who stopped it and when, as soon as the agent confirms it. If the agent's process restarts (deploy, host reboot, a crash with **Reiniciar se cair** (Restart if it crashes), the **Reiniciar** (Restart) button or the **Parametrizador global** (global parameter editor)), it comes back with the bot stopped: the agent stops the bot as soon as it starts, before the first candle. Only **Iniciar bot** or `/start` release it again. Before, a restart came back with the bot running.
  - **Bot stopped:** no new order from the strategy goes out, neither entry nor exit. The protection of the open positions (the stop and target at the broker, and moving them), the closing of a position by XTrader AI or by **Zerar** (Flatten) and the cancellation of orders continue. Pending orders already at the broker stay there; use **Zerar + Cancelar ordens** (Flatten + Cancel orders) first, if needed.
  - **Where it shows:** the agent's card in Configurações › Agentes (Settings › Agents) shows **Bot parado** (Bot stopped), with who stopped it, when, and that it stays stopped after a restart.
- **Configurações (Settings), by section (in the top navigation):**
  - **Agentes (Agents):** the LLM ones separate from the strategies, these grouped by family; create, edit, rename, delete, turn on and off, restart and set the secrets.
  - **Renomear** (Rename, a button on the agent's card): only with the agent disabled and stopped. It changes only the name: the agent keeps its ID, its account (comment and magic number at the broker, so the positions remain its own), the model, the secrets, the orders, the decisions, the results and the queue of records not yet sent (the file `data/outbox/<nome>-<id>.db` is renamed with it; if the change fails, nothing changes). The old name stays in `agent_former_names`, so the seeds do not recreate the agent with it, and it is free for another agent. In Telegram, the agent is selected again by the new name (`/agent`).
  - **Resultados** (Results, a button on the agent's card): the results by period and their reset. See [Resetting the results](#resetting-the-results).
  - **Contas MT5 (MT5 accounts):** the accounts of each terminal and the coordinated switch of the active account. See [MT5 accounts](#mt5-accounts).
  - **Limites (Limits):** the global daily limit, the trading hours, the exposure per asset, the agents per asset and each agent's limits.
  - **Notícias (News):** the economic calendar and the blocking window of all agents. See [News](#news).
  - **Prompts:** editor with version history.
  - **XTrader AI:** see [XTrader AI](#xtrader-ai-the-supervisor-of-the-agents).
  - **Telegram:** turns the bot on and off, token and authorized IDs.
  - **Usuários e papéis (Users and roles):** the dashboard users and the permissions by area (see [Security](#security)).
  - **Layout:** the arrangement of the dashboard cards in this browser and the **Restaurar layout padrão** button.
  - **Importar e exportar (Import and export):** the configuration in a JSON file. See [Importing and exporting the configuration](#importing-and-exporting-the-configuration).
- **Navigation and layout:** the top bar leads to the Painel and to the sections of Configurações in a segmented control (next to the brand on desktop, in a second row below 1200 px, a drawer through the ☰ button on a phone), with each section's count; the Painel link goes back to the agent, pair and timeframe left there. The cards follow one pattern (a short highlighted label over the title, values in a monospaced font) and orange marks what is chosen and the main action. Dark and light theme. On a phone, tables become cards and dialogs open as panels coming from the bottom; in a table too wide for the screen, the actions column stays fixed on the right.

### Performance

The **Performance** page (in the bar, next to Painel (Dashboard); `/performance`) shows the result of the trades of the live agents, in the style of the "Resumo de Performance" (Performance Summary) of Nelogica's Profit.

- **Source:** the deals of the MT5 accounts, the same as the closed P/L card's, in each account's currency, without conversion. With accounts in different currencies, each currency has its own summary (the "Moeda da conta", Account currency, selector). Only the agents' positions count (by the entry's magic number); manual ones and paper agents are left out.
- **Trade:** a position whose last close fell in the period and of which nothing stayed open, counted once even if closed in parts. It has asset, agent, side, lots, open time, close time, duration, entry price and average exit price, gross result (the profit of the exits), costs (commission, fee and swap of the entry and the exits) and net. A position opened more than 30 days before the period has no open time and no duration. Unlike the closed P/L card, which sums only the exits, here the net includes the entry commission.
- **Filters:** period (Hoje, Semana, Mês or Personalizado, that is Today, Week, Month or Custom; kept in the browser separately from the card's), agent and asset. The numbers are reread every minute and by the Atualizar (Refresh) button.
- **Resumo (Summary):**
  - net balance and total balance (before costs), gross profit and gross loss, costs and profit factor (gross profit ÷ gross loss);
  - winning, losing and break-even trades, by the result before costs, and the percentage of winners;
  - average per trade (net), average of the winners and of the losers and the ratio between them, largest gain and largest loss, longest streaks and average times;
  - maximum lots and initial capital (the accounts' balance at the start of the period, rebuilt from the current balance minus everything the account recorded since then, deposits included);
  - return on that capital, maximum equity (the peak of the cumulative net) and percentage of time in the market;
  - maximum drawdown of the cumulative net, in value and in % of the equity at the peak (initial capital + peak). Without the path of each trade, it is measured between closes, so peak-to-trough and trade-by-trade give the same number and only one appears.
- **Gráficos (Charts):**
  - equity: the cumulative net, green above and red below zero;
  - the result of each trade;
  - by period: by day up to 31 days, by week up to 180, by month beyond that;
  - by asset and by agent.
- **Operações (Trades):** the list, most recent first, with the running total, MFE, MAE and total efficiency, and **Baixar CSV** (Download CSV), a file separated by `;`, with times in UTC, the filters applied and the entry, exit and total efficiencies.
- **Efficiency (the Gráficos › Eficiência view and its group in Resumo):** the price path of each trade while it was open, read from the broker's candles (the bridge's `/rates`), in the smallest timeframe that fits in 5,000 candles (M1 up to ~3.5 days, then M5, M15, H1, H4, D1).
  - **MFE/MAE:** the maximum in favor and the maximum against, in money in the account currency, valued like the trade itself (the gross result over the price move; with no move, the lots by the symbol's tick value). On the chart, each trade is a bar from MAE to MFE with a dot at the result.
  - **Efficiencies** (those of TradeStation and Profit, over the price range from low to high): entry, how close to the best price it entered; exit, how close to the best price it exited; total, the result over the range (negative on a loss).
  - **In Resumo:** average MFE and MAE, the average efficiencies, the "deixado na mesa" (left on the table: how much the winners gave back from the best point) and the largest MAE of a winner (how far a gain went against it, a reference for the stop).
  - **Limitations:** MT5 candles are bid, and for a short the spread of each candle is added (the price at which it closes); the entry candle and the exit candle may have prices from before or after the trade, and the extremes always include the realized entry and exit. A trade with no known open time has no path. The first access to a period reads the candles of each trade, which can take a few seconds; after that, each trade read stays cached while the controller runs.

### Backtesting

The **Backtesting** page (in the bar, next to Performance; `/backtesting`) runs the agents' strategies over the history of their assets, several at a time, and compares the results under the same conditions. It serves to decide which agents to turn on; turning them on is still done in Configurações › Agentes (Settings › Agents).

- **Isolation:** each run is a separate process of the binary of the agent's kind, in `-mode backtest`, with a CSV feed and a paper wallet.
  - The process does not receive the agent's token, the account and bridge flags or the bridge tokens (`MT5_BRIDGE_TOKEN`, `MT5_MCP_TOKEN`); an LLM agent receives only the key of the model's provider.
  - It never sends an order and never writes to the agents' tables, balances or histories. The controller only reads the candles (`/symbol` and `/rates`) from the bridge of the agent's account.
  - The tests prove this with a fake bridge that records the requests: none reaches the agent, and the controller only makes reads.
- **Configure:** the agents (the incompatible ones appear disabled, with the reason), the assets (a subset, or all of each agent's), the period (up to `backtest.max_period_days`), the out-of-sample validation, the timeframe (the agent's or another, which tests a variation), the initial balance, the fee, the fallback spread, the slippage, the daily limits, the trading hours, the agents per asset and the caps of the LLM agents. Before submitting, the page shows the assumptions and the estimate of LLM calls and cost.
- **Runs:** queued in the controller, `backtest.concurrency` at a time. Each has a status (queued, running, done, failed, canceled, interrupted, LLM cap reached), progress, duration and the error. They continue with the page closed; a controller restart marks the ones that were running as interrupted. Canceling stops the process and keeps the partial report.
- **Results:** one row per agent, with the fit and the validation side by side.
  - Metrics: return/drawdown, net, return %, maximum drawdown (% and value), profit factor, win rate, trades, open at the end (apart), positive months and the validation.
  - No row is "the best": the default sort is return/drawdown, every column sorts, and there are filters for minimum sample and LLM agents.
  - Robustness badges: small sample (fewer than 30 trades), high drawdown (above 20%), degraded in validation (positive fit and negative validation, or validation return/drawdown below half the fit's), non-deterministic LLM, data with gaps, refused orders.
  - The conditions that differ between rows (assets, timeframes, LLM agents mixed with deterministic ones) appear in a notice.
  - An undefined metric (profit factor without losses, return/drawdown without a drawdown) appears as "—", with the reason on mouse hover.
  - A run's detail opens in a panel on the right, over the page, when clicking the agent (it closes with Esc, the × or a click outside; on a phone, at full width), and has the capital curve, the trades with the reason for the entry and the exit (in the LLM, the model's rationale), the positions open at the end, the warnings and gaps, the configuration snapshot and, in the LLM, the calls.
- **Out-of-sample validation:** with a validation of N% (up to 50%), the batch runs each agent twice, on the start of the period (fit) and on the last N% (validation), with the same configuration.
- **Configuration snapshot:** each run keeps the agent's effective parameters (the same flags it runs with, without secrets), the protection, the command and the binary's sha256, and, in the LLM, the provider, the model, the caps, the prices and the prompt (name, version, sha256 and content).

**Simulation assumptions**

- **Data:** MT5 bid candles read from the bridge of the agent's account, with each candle's spread (the fallback spread applies only to candles without it), stored in `backtest.dir/candles` and reused. There is a warm-up before the period: what the agent's strategy asks for (the agent reports the candles and the timeframe with `-print-warmup`, without connecting to anything), with 50% extra headroom plus one day for weekends and market pauses, up to 365 days; at least 1000 candles of the agent's timeframe (up to 60 days), which is also what applies to LLM agents or when the agent does not report it (with a warning). The engine only delivers candles to the strategy after the full warm-up, so a short warm-up would leave the start of the period without trades (e.g. `mtfema`, with EMAs of 4x and 16x the timeframe, asks for 8832 candles of 15m, about 140 days). A long period with a long warm-up may run on M5 instead of M1 (up to 200,000 candles per file). If history is still missing, the run warns "aquecimento incompleto" (incomplete warm-up), counted in candles of the strategy's timeframe. Gaps larger than 3 times the timeframe (and than 2 hours) outside the weekend become warnings and are never filled; without enough data, the run fails with the reason.
- **No future data:** the strategy, the breakeven/trailing and the LLM context only read candles closed up to the simulated instant, including in higher timeframes.
- **Execution:** at the finest resolution the period allows (up to 200,000 candles: M1 when it fits, otherwise M5, M15…). When the broker keeps the finer candles for less time than the period (on the Exness demo, M1 covers only a few months), the run uses the next timeframe that covers the whole period, with a warning, and the detail shows the resolution used; with none, it fails for insufficient data. Buys at the ask (bid + spread), sells at the bid, slippage against the order at market and on stops. A stop with a gap fills at the candle's open.
- **Conservative rule:** with stop and target touched in the same candle, the stop wins; an entry filled in a candle that also crosses the stop exits stopped out in it.
- **Lot and margin:** the symbol's lot step and minimum lot (an order outside them is refused and counted); the agent's leverage sets the margin.
- **Protection and limits:** the agent's breakeven and trailing run on every candle. The batch's daily limits (in the quote currency, America/Belem day) flatten the position and block entries until the day turns. The agents' limits (in the account currency) and the global limit do not apply.
- **Trading hours:** the batch can apply trading hours, with one or more windows and, at the end of each, flatten the positions and/or cancel the pending entry orders, as checked in it. They come filled with those from the Limites (Limits) tab and can be changed for the batch only. They run on the candles' clock, in America/Belem time, with the same rule as production: outside the windows, no entry; at the end of a window that was open, its actions, with the positions closed at the candle that closes at the end, and the exit reason **Fim do horário** (End of hours). Each agent in the batch runs only in the windows that allow it (the ones for everyone and the ones that select it), and a batch with an agent outside every window is refused. They stay in the run's assumptions (`hours.windows`; the older batches, with `start` and `end`, still count as one window).
- **Agents per asset:** the batch asks whether it respects the [agents per asset](#agents-per-asset) of Configurações › Limites (Settings › Limits), through **Respeitar os agentes por ativo de Configurações › Limites** (Respect the agents per asset of Settings › Limits), checked by default. When checked, each agent runs only on the assets the rules allow it: the others stay out of the run, with a note such as `fora pelos agentes por ativo de Configurações › Limites: XAUUSDm` (out by the agents per asset of Settings › Limits), and the page shows before submitting what is left out for each agent. An agent with no allowed asset is refused (take it out of the batch or uncheck the option). The rules apply as they were when the batch was queued and stay in the assumptions (`asset_agents` and `asset_rules`), shown in the results summary. When unchecked, each agent tests all its assets.
- **Agent parameters:** the batch can add parameters to all its agents (`nome=valor`, separated by `;`; a list inside a value uses commas), e.g. the [entry filter](#features) (`regime=trend; equity-curve=20`), to compare the same agents with and without a filter without changing their configuration. An agent parameter with the same name is overridden only in the batch. The parameters the backtest itself sets (the assets, the timeframe, the strategy, the mode, the MT5 account ones) and those of LLM agents are refused, and a batch with parameters does not accept LLM agents. They stay in the batch's assumptions.
- **End of period:** the open positions are valued at market (close − spread − slippage) and shown apart from the realized result.
- **Currency:** the pair's quote currency (USD in XAUUSD and BTCUSD), without conversion: a simulation, not the value in the account currency.

**LLM agents**

- They run with a cap on calls and on cost per run (`backtest.llm_max_calls` and `llm_max_cost` by default, changeable in the batch). The cost is estimated from each response's tokens and the prices in `backtest.llm_prices`; without the model's price, only the calls limit it. Once the cap is reached, the run stops with the status "teto do LLM atingido" (LLM cap reached) and the result up to that point.
- The clock is the simulated one: dates, rate limit and the day's rules follow the candle, not the real clock.
- The context is reduced and flagged: without higher timeframes, correlated markets, economic calendar and trade memory, which would need data from the future or from the database. Candles, indicators, structure/SMC, session, drawings and the memory of the run's own decisions remain.
- Each call is recorded: simulated instant, asset, context sha256, decision, confidence, rationale, tokens and cost.
- The result is not deterministic: another run may give another result, and the model may have seen the period in training.

**Limitations:** no order book or execution queue, no historical swap, spread per candle (not per tick), the execution resolution drops in long periods, and the broker's gaps shrink the sample.

### Persistence

- **PostgreSQL 18:**
  - users, with bcrypt passwords;
  - versioned prompts;
  - agents, MT5 accounts and model configuration;
  - Telegram;
  - orders, decisions with attachments, drawings (`decision_drawings`), sentiment (`decision_sentiments`) and journal;
  - former names of renamed agents (`agent_former_names`);
  - the allowed reanalysis of each LLM agent (`agent_reanalysis`);
  - the positions the core discarded because they were closed outside the bot (`position_discards`);
  - the result accounting periods of each agent;
  - each agent's position protection (`agent_protection`) and the protections XTrader AI armed on the positions (`position_protections`);
  - the agents' news (`news_settings` and `news_rules`): the feature on or off, the calendar's MCP server and its token, encrypted, and the blocking window, the same for all agents;
  - the broker stops of the daily loss: each limit's switch (`daily_limit_stops`, on when there is no row) and the stops requested on the open positions with the last send (`daily_stops`);
  - the least margin for new entries of each limit, in % of the maximum loss (`daily_limit_margins`, 10% when there is no row), and the margin locks (`daily_margin_guards`);
  - the daily lock: the limits of all agents (`daily_limit_settings`, whose `currency` column is no longer used: it stores `XXX`, the ISO code for "no currency", which the CHECK of migration 27 requires with the limit on) and of each agent (`agent_daily_limits`), each day's locks (`daily_guards`) and the log of changes, triggers, releases and flattenings (`daily_limit_events`);
  - the backtests: the batches with the period, the validation and the assumptions (`backtest_batches`) and the runs with status, progress, the configuration snapshot, the summary and the report (`backtest_runs`); the candles and the runs' files stay in `backtest.dir`;
  - the supervisor: the settings (`supervisor_settings`), the analyses with the snapshot sent (`supervisor_runs`), the actions (`supervisor_actions`), the conversations per user (`supervisor_messages`) and each agent's block and stop (`agent_supervision`).
- **Secrets** (API keys and tokens) encrypted with AES-256-GCM, each bound to its place (table, column and record): copied to another agent or another column, it does not open (see [Security](#security)).
- **SQL migrations**, versioned, applied by `cmd/migrator`.
- **Reads when an LLM agent starts:** the last decisions of each asset and the prompt memory (the last journal decisions of each asset) are read through the agent and asset indexes, one asset at a time, only the rows requested: the read time does not grow with months of stored decisions. The links between decisions and orders are all read, because the dashboard shows the decision of each of the agent's orders, and there is one per order.
- **Paper decisions:** they stay in the agent's memory, the last 2000 (the dashboard lists up to 500). In live, they stay in the database; in a backtest, all in memory.
- **Filled order that fails to be stored:** an order the broker already filled never becomes an error because of a write failure, for example with the outbox disk full.
  - The bot keeps tracking it in memory: position, legs and profit.
  - The failure is reported on the dashboard and in Telegram, and the write is retried until it succeeds, with another notification when it completes. Failures of new orders during the same outage go out together, at most one notification per minute, even if an order stays refused forever. Orders stored while another one keeps failing are also reported.
  - When stopping, the agent stops trading and then tries to write again before closing the outbox (see "Stopping an agent" in [Stopping the controller](#stopping-the-controller)). Whatever has not been stored yet appears in the log as lost. The position remains at the broker.
  - Non-finite numbers (NaN, infinity) are stored as 0, also in the local storage (buntdb) of paper and backtest.
- **Fill without a price:** a fill the broker reports without a price (or the close of a position opened by one) has an unknown profit, instead of an infinite profit or a 100% loss computed with price 0. The order is stored and sent to the dashboard with profit 0, the `[PROFIT]` notification says the profit is unknown (in MT5, it carries the result the broker recorded, when it arrives), and the bot's results summary does not count it. The position is tracked normally.
- **Stored position the broker does not have:** on start, the bot rebuilds the positions from the stored filled orders and, in MT5, checks each one against the bot's position (of its magic number) at the broker, without sending any order.
  - A position the broker does not have, for example closed by hand in the terminal or by stop out, is discarded, with a warning in the log. Before, the next entry on the same side averaged with it, and the profit and results came out wrong (the strategies' decisions did not change, because they read the position from the broker).
  - If there are open orders on the other side that may have closed it while the agent was stopped (the stop loss or the take profit), the position stays until they finish: if one fills, the order closes the position with the result; if all are canceled, the position is discarded if the broker still does not have it. A fill on the asset in the meantime ends the check (the position goes on as before).
  - A position on the other side or of another size at the broker stays as it is, with an error in the log, because there is no way to know what changed. The same goes for a bot position at the broker that the stored orders do not show. When the difference (the extra position at the broker, on the same side) fits in the stored open orders of that side, such as a pending entry (limit or stop) filled while the agent was stopped, the log is only a warning: its fill arrives right after, through the order update, and the position becomes the broker's.
  - With the agent running, the check is made when an order on the other side of the position ends without a fill, like the legs MT5 cancels when the position is closed by hand or by stop out. When no open order that could close it is left and the broker does not have the position, it is discarded, with a warning in the log and without sending an order. If an order on the asset is in progress (for example, a market exit not yet recorded), the check waits for the next update.
  - A position without legs (no stop loss or take profit) closed outside the bot while the agent is running generates no event: it stays in memory until the next restart. A position that already received, before the check, a new entry on the same side is not discarded either: the broker shows another size and the position is kept, with a warning in the log.
  - Each discard is stored (`position_discards`, through the outbox; in the buntdb of paper and backtest, alongside the orders) with the asset, the side, the size and the asset's last fill before it. On restart, the bot rebuilds the positions from the filled orders and discards the position again right after that fill. So the following trades are computed on their own, as they were, and their stored profit does not change on every restart. Before, the discard only happened after all orders were rebuilt: the next close was computed against the average price of the discarded position combined with the new one, and that wrong profit (in % and in the quote currency) was stored over the right one. Profits stored this way are corrected on the first restart after the discard is stored. The net profit in the account currency, which comes from the broker, does not change.

## Architecture

```none
                       ┌──────────────── controller (cmd/controller) ────────────────┐
browser ──── HTTPS ──► │ web dashboard (/api, session + CSRF)  Telegram (allowed IDs)│
                       │ Manager: starts/stops the agents of the DB, tokens per start│
                       │ internal API of the agents (loopback, Bearer per agent)     │──► PostgreSQL
                       └──────▲───────────────────────────────▲──────────────────────┘
                              │ config, runtime, outbox        │ proxy of the API of each agent
                     ┌────────┴────────┐              ┌────────┴────────┐
                     │ llm agent       │  ...         │ trend agent     │ ──► MT5 bridge ──► MT5 terminal
                     └─────────────────┘              └─────────────────┘      (tools/mt5bridge)
```

- **`cmd/controller`:** the only process that accesses the database.
- **`cmd/migrator`:** applies the migrations and the seeds (`gobox/database/migrate`), which on a new database set Telegram off and add the prompts and the initial agents (see [Initial agents](#initial-agents)). The configuration lives only in the database and is changed through the dashboard. The controller does not migrate: it checks the schema version. Each seed file starts with a Postgres advisory lock (`pg_advisory_xact_lock`), so two migrators running at the same time apply the seeds one after the other, without duplicating or failing.
- **Agents:** receive the configuration through the internal API (`agentapi`). The parameters become the same command-line flags, and secrets are never passed as arguments. The block on entries arrives through the same API (in the configuration and in the runtime, with a revision that only grows) and takes effect without restarting the agent.
- **XTrader AI** (`controller/supervision*.go`, the supervisor): runs inside the controller. It builds the snapshot from the database, the `Manager`, the bridges and each agent's API, calls the model with tools (`agents/llm/chat.go`, with streaming in `chat_stream.go`) and executes the actions through the `supervisor` channel of the agent's API, which only reduces risk, or directly through the bridge when the agent is not running.
- **Position protection** (`agents/internal/protect`): runs in each agent, called by the engine on every candle (the `xtrader.CandleHook` hook, after the strategy, outside the preload and the backtest), and moves the stop through the order controller's guard (`order.Controller.TightenProtection`, `SetStopGuard`), which only tightens the stop of an open position, comparing it with the broker's live level (`service.ProtectionReader`). The configuration and the armings arrive through the internal API (`agentapi.Protection`, in the `Config` and the `Runtime`), without a restart.
- **Entries blocked in the core** (`order.Controller`): with entries blocked, market orders only reduce the position (`CreateOrderMarketReduce` in MT5 and in the paper wallet, which never open the remainder), and entries, entry pending orders, orders sized in money and an OCO that does not protect a position are refused with `ErrEntriesBlocked`. Protecting, moving stops, canceling and closing still work.
- **`tools/mt5bridge`:** an HTTP bridge in Python to the MT5 terminal, which runs on Wine inside the `mt5` container. It serves one request at a time and protects the orders:
  - identical reads waiting for the terminal together (the same `GET` with the same query, e.g. every agent reading the account's `/positions` every second) are served by a single call to the terminal, made after all of them have arrived: a response is never read before its request. A read that arrives with that call already in progress waits for the next one. Orders (`POST /order`, `POST /check`) and the session are never shared, and each request keeps its own deadline. Before this, with 33 agents on one account, each read waited about 0.7 s in the terminal's queue, and closing a position (quote, order and history) took about 3 s;
  - each order carries a 20 s deadline, in two headers: `X-Bridge-Deadline` (an instant in Unix ms, by the agent's clock) and `X-Bridge-Timeout` (the remaining ms, which the bridge counts by its own clock from receipt). The bridge uses whichever expires first, counted on the monotonic clock. This way, a bridge on another host with a lagging clock does not send the order later. If the terminal is busy or reconnecting beyond the deadline, the order is refused without going to the terminal (`504` with `"sent": false`), and it is never executed after the agent gave up on it. A bridge clock running ahead shortens the deadline: the agent warns in the log when the difference exceeds 1 s;
  - before each order, the bridge checks that the terminal is still on the `--login` account and that a real account has `--allow-real`. If not, it refuses the order (`409`);
  - a list the terminal could not read (positions, orders, history, candles) is an error (`502`), never an empty list. The exception is the candles of a range outside the terminal's history (before its start or beyond "Max. bars in chart"), which `copy_rates_*` refuses as the `CopyRates` documentation says: if the symbol's last candle is read, the range comes back empty, and the download and the pagination continue as at the end of the history.
- **Orders without a response:** each order's comment is the agent's comment followed by `#` and a tag of its own (e.g. `nb:llm#k3f9a2`). The magic number is still what identifies the bot's positions. If an order's response is lost, the agent looks for the order in the account by that tag before raising an error. If it finds it, it continues as if the response had arrived; if it does not show up after the deadline (up to about 22 s), the order was not executed.
  - During this wait, only the other orders of the same pair and whoever sent the order wait (the call waits for the result; in a multi-pair bot, its candles wait too). Order tracking, reads, the dashboard and the orders of the other pairs continue.
  - If the account cannot be read after the deadline, the result is unknown and the pair is blocked for orders that open or close a position until a read of the account tells what happened. Stop loss and take profit can still be moved.
  - If the terminal switches accounts (e.g. through VNC), the agent stops sending orders, and applying the positions it reads, until the account comes back. The account is read before and after the lists of each read.
  - Update both images together: with an old bridge, which does not honor the two deadlines, the agent warns in the log and treats an order that was not found as having an unknown result.

The complete step-by-step guide, with the economic calendar, ports, backup and migration, is in [deploy/docker/README.md](deploy/docker/README.md).

```bash
# PostgreSQL 18 in a container

  -e POSTGRES_PASSWORD="$XTRADER_DB_PASSWORD" -p 127.0.0.1:5432:5432 postgres:18.6-trixie
```

## Configuration

`config.yml` has only what is needed to start and reach the database. The rest lives in the database and is changed through the dashboard.

| In `config.yml`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | In the database (Configurações / Settings)                                                                                                                                       |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `listen`, `database`, `secrets_key`, `migrations`, `bootstrap` (first user), `ui`, `agents` (internal API, directory, command of each type, stop time), `backtest` (concurrency, directory, maximum period and runs, LLM caps and prices), `services` (host bridge), `shutdown_timeout` (whole shutdown), `log` | agents (mode, address, MT5 account, model, parameters, secrets, prompt, protection), MT5 accounts, prompts, limits and trading hours, news, XTrader AI, Telegram, users and roles |

| Variable                                                    | Use                                                                                                    |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `XTRADER_DB_PASSWORD`                                       | password of the PostgreSQL `xtrader` user                                                              |
| `XTRADER_SECRETS_KEY`                                       | 32 bytes in base64 that encrypt the secrets in the database: without it, the secrets must be set again |
| `XTRADER_BOOTSTRAP_USERNAME` / `XTRADER_BOOTSTRAP_PASSWORD` | first dashboard user, created while there is none                                                      |
| `MT5_BRIDGE_TOKEN`                                          | token of the host bridge (`services` in the config)                                                    |

The `${VAR}` references are replaced in the text before the YAML is read, which is why they go in single quotes. This way, any value without `'` works.

A `${VAR}` with no value would become empty text. That is why the controller and the migrator do not start when a variable of `database.dsn` or `secrets_key` is missing (unset or empty), or when an `env` entry of a `services` item is empty, such as `MT5_BRIDGE_TOKEN` (without a token, the host bridge would accept any process on the machine). Any other unset variable produces a warning in the log, except the `bootstrap` ones, which can be removed after the first login.

The processes launched by the controller do not inherit its environment (and, with it, its secrets). Agents receive `PATH`, `HOME`, `USER`, `LOGNAME`, `TZ`, `LANG`, `LC_ALL` and `TMPDIR`, plus `agents.env`, the internal API address, their own name and, on each start, a new token and key. The `services` receive the same basic variables plus their own `env`, never `agents.env`.

### Stopping the controller

- **Times:** on SIGINT or SIGTERM, the controller stops the agents, each with `agents.stop_timeout` (20 s by default) before being killed, and then the `services`, each with its own `stop_timeout` (10 s by default). The whole shutdown fits in `shutdown_timeout`: by default, `agents.stop_timeout` plus the largest `stop_timeout` of the `services` plus 10 s (40 s in the example), and never less than that sum plus 3 s (the controller does not start with a smaller value). Each phase, the agents' and the `services`', can take up to 1 s beyond its own `stop_timeout`, waiting for the output (stdout/stderr) of a killed process that another process outside the group still holds, such as the Xvfb that the bridge's `run.sh` starts in another session; the remaining second covers the rest of the shutdown. Once that time has passed, whatever is still running is killed and the controller exits with an error.
- **Stopping an agent:** on the signal, the agent stops trading before closing the outbox.
  - It immediately refuses any new order from the strategy, including the one from the candle in progress.
  - It waits for the broker calls that have already started to finish and be written to the outbox. The model query in progress (LLM agent) is ended without an order and without a record: the stop is not a model failure and does not enter the decision history, the journal or the counts; only the log says the query got no answer (and a requested reanalysis receives "the agent stopped before the model answered").
  - After that, nothing else reaches the broker, neither cancellations nor protections. Only then does it send the pending records to the controller (up to 10 s; the rest goes on the next start) and close the outbox.
  - A slow broker call holds the stop up to `agents.stop_timeout`. If the agent is killed earlier, what was already in the outbox stays on disk and is sent on the next start.
- **Whoever stops the controller waits longer than that:** `tools/automator.sh stop` (up to `AUTOMATOR_STOP_TIMEOUT`), the compose `stop_grace_period` (45 s, above the container's 30 s `shutdown_timeout`) or the `TimeoutStopSec` of a systemd unit. An installation in `/var/containers/xtrader` made before this change only gets `stop_grace_period` after reinstalling (see "Parar tudo" (Stop everything) in [deploy/docker/README.md](deploy/docker/README.md#operação)); until then, Docker waits only 10 s.
- **Controller killed** (`kill -9`, out of memory, a crash): on Linux, the agents and the `services` receive SIGTERM and stop as in a normal shutdown, including the program that a `go run` runs. An agent launched by the controller ignores SIGPIPE: its log went to the dead controller, so the lines of that shutdown are lost, but the shutdown runs to the end (it stops trading, writes the order in progress and closes the outbox; what does not reach the controller stays in the outbox for the next start). A controller server that fails (dashboard or internal API) also stops everything in order.
- **Leftover processes:** each start of an agent or a `service` has a pidfile in `data/pids` under `agents.dir`. On startup, before launching anything, the controller stops whatever a previous controller left running, as in a normal shutdown. It checks each process's environment to confirm it is really the one that was launched: a PID reused by another program is left untouched. Without Linux (without `/proc`), these processes are only reported in the log.
- **One controller per `agents.dir`:** a second controller with the same directory does not start, because it would take the first one's processes for leftovers.

## Usage

### Web dashboard

- **Dashboard (`/`):** choose the agent and the pair in the top bar, where the bot is also started and stopped.
  - Only the strategies open positions: there is no manual order from the dashboard or from Telegram (the order ticket and `/buy`/`/sell` were removed).
  - Pending orders can be dragged on the chart and canceled in the order list, with the agent's **Painel de trading** (Trade panel) switch on. Starting and stopping the bot does not depend on it.
  - The reanalysis of an LLM agent (**Reanalisar agora**, Reanalyze now) depends only on its **Permitir reanálise** (Allow reanalysis) switch, whether the trade panel is on or not.
  - The chart's timeframe selector changes only the view (see [Chart timeframe](#web-dashboard)); the strategy and the LLM stay on the agent's execution timeframe.
- **Configurações (Settings, `/settings`):** the sections Agentes (Agents), Contas MT5 (MT5 accounts), Limites (Limits), Notícias (News), Prompts, XTrader AI, Telegram, Usuários e papéis (Users and roles), Layout and Importar e exportar (Import and export), in the top navigation, which also open by link (`/settings#prompts`). Each user sees only the sections their role allows.
- **Lists: search and pages.** The lists load from the server only the page shown, 10 items at a time (5 batches in Backtesting › Execuções (Runs)), with the pager below them: Contas MT5, Prompts, Telegram › Usuários autorizados (Authorized users), Usuários do painel (Dashboard users), Papéis (Roles), Limites › Limites por agente (Limits per agent), the XTrader AI analyses, actions and protections (in Configurações and in the chat's Análises (Analyses) tab) and, in Backtesting, the runs and the comparison of the results.
  - **Search:** Contas MT5 (name, login, server, bridge, type, currency, company and agents), Prompts (name, description and the agents that use them), Usuários autorizados (Telegram ID and name), Usuários do painel (user and role) and Papéis (name, description and the areas of the permissions; Administrador (Administrator) matches all of them) have a search at the top of the list, like the one in Agentes: it ignores case and accents, each word must appear in some field, Esc or × clear it, and the note below says how many items were found (or "Nenhum … encontrado para “…”" (No … found for “…”)). The search goes to the server and considers every record, not only those on the page; when searching, the list goes back to the first page.
  - **Filters and sorting:** the comparison of the results of a batch sorts by any column and filters (minimum number of trades, LLM agents) on the server, before paging; changing the filter or the order goes back to the first page. The other lists keep the order they had before (by name, by ID or newest first).
  - **Actions:** create, edit, delete, activate, test and the other list actions stay the same; after each one, the current page is read again (the last page, if the current one became empty).

### MT5 accounts

The MetaTrader 5 accounts live in the database, managed in **Configurações > Contas MT5** (Settings > MT5 accounts).

- **Registration:** identification, login, server and password, the bridge of the terminal that serves the account and its token, **Permitir conta real** (Allow real account) and **Somente leitura** (Read only). The password and the token are encrypted (AES-GCM, with `XTRADER_SECRETS_KEY`, which lives outside the database) and bound to the account: they are never displayed, returned by the API or logged. A secret field left blank keeps its value; removing it is explicit.
- **One terminal, one account at a time:** the **active** account of each terminal is the one it logs into. The controller hands the account to the bridge (`POST /session`), which keeps it without the password. The terminal is identified by the bridge's host and port and by the instance the bridge reports (an identifier written in `data/xtrader-bridge/instance`): several terminals on the same host are independent, two URLs of the same terminal count as one, and accounts on different terminals are active and trading at the same time, without interrupting one another.
- **Coordinated switch:** on the **Ativa no terminal** (Active on the terminal) toggle of each account, the dashboard first shows what the switch does (`GET /api/settings/mt5-accounts/{id}/switch`) and asks for confirmation. When activating another account on the same terminal (`POST .../activate`) or deactivating the current one (`POST .../deactivate`), the controller stops that terminal's agents and waits for them to stop. If the agents have open positions or pending orders, the switch requires `close_positions` (the question: "Existem posições abertas ou ordens pendentes na conta …" (There are open positions or pending orders on account …)); it then closes the positions and cancels the orders (recorded as a `close_positions` action in the XTrader AI history), checks the account and only then logs the terminal into the new account. With `move_agents` (the option "Levar os agentes da conta … para esta conta" (Move the agents of account … to this account), checked by default in the confirmation), the agents of the account that left move to the new one, after the login, and are started on it again; on a deactivation no agent changes account. If no account is active on the terminal (for example, a previous switch failed and the previous account could not come back), the agents that move along are those of that terminal's inactive accounts; the confirmation says which accounts they come from (`from` in the preview). Agents of another terminal never change account. If something remains open, the switch is not made, the current account's agents are started again and what is still open is returned (`409`); if the terminal does not log into the new account, the previous one becomes the active one again (`502`). Positions opened by hand (without an agent's magic number) are not touched. Afterwards, the agents of the active account are started; those of an inactive account stay stopped, with the reason on the card. Changing the login, the server or the bridge of the active account is still refused while the terminal is in use; changing only the password, the token, the name or the options is allowed at any time.
- **Agents:** each agent trades the account associated with it (in the agent's form), receiving the bridge and the token from the account. Changing an agent's account requires the agent to be stopped and without positions or orders on the current account. An agent of an inactive account is not launched, with the reason on the card.
- **Connection test:** checks the bridge, the token and the account the terminal is on; if it is this one, the type (real, demo or contest), the currency and the company confirmed by the bridge. It never switches the terminal's account to test.
- **Reconciliation:** on startup, before launching the agents, and every 30 s, the controller checks each terminal and, with no agents running on it, logs it into the active account (or updates the options). Without the password, when the terminal asks for it, the account shows "o terminal pede a senha" (the terminal asks for the password).
- **Import:** on startup, each bridge with agents still without an account becomes an account (login, server, type and currency read from the bridge itself; the agents' token; allow real account if the bridge already serves a real account), active, with the agents associated. It never duplicates (same login and server) or overwrites an existing account.
- **Log:** creation, changes (with secrets only as set or not), activation, deactivation, deletion, import, agent association, tests and sessions sent are kept in `mt5_account_events`.
- **API:** `GET`/`POST /api/settings/mt5-accounts`, `PATCH`/`DELETE /api/settings/mt5-accounts/{id}`, `POST .../{id}/activate`, `POST .../{id}/test` and `GET .../{id}/events`; the agent gains `mt5_account_id` in `/api/settings/agents`.

### Agents and parameters

**Creating an agent:** use **Configurações > Agentes > Novo agente** (Settings > Agents > New agent). The form asks for:

- type and mode;
- the agent's API address, only on loopback (`localhost`, `127.0.0.0/8` or `[::1]`, e.g. `localhost:5580`), because only the controller on the same machine accesses it;
- MT5 account (bridge, magic number, comment);
- model, for the LLM agent;
- secrets;
- parameters.

**Magic number and comment:** each agent only trades the positions of its own magic number on the account. Without a magic number, it is a hash of the comment; without a comment, `nb:` plus the strategy is used (`nb:llm` for the LLM agent; `nb:trendconfluence`, `nb:adxema`, `nb:atrbreakout` or `nb:orb` when the `strategy` parameter is not set).

- Two agents active in live on the same bridge, with the same magic number and a pair in common, would close and move each other's positions. That is why the dashboard refuses the second one (`409`) when creating, activating or editing. The bridge is compared by host and port (`localhost` and `127.0.0.1` count as the same).
- This also applies to agents that run separately (**Iniciado pelo controller** (Started by the controller) off): they trade the same way, and the dashboard compares them by their saved settings, since the controller does not see them running. Keep the settings of a separate agent equal to the flags it runs with.
- Disabled agents or agents in paper can repeat the magic number: they do not send orders.
- The controller also does not start an agent that conflicts with another one running, for example with data saved before this rule. The card shows **Não iniciado** (Not started) and the reason. This check only sees the agents the controller itself runs.
- To separate two agents, give one of them its own comment (or magic number). Changing the magic number of an agent with open positions makes it stop recognizing them.

**Agent search:** the field above the groups, in Configurações › Agentes, lists the agents of every group that match what was typed: by name, by type (e.g. `rompimento` (breakout), `tendência` (trend)), by strategy, by assets (e.g. `ustecm`) or by the model of an LLM agent, ignoring case and accents; with several words, the agent must have all of them. While there is a search, the agents found appear in place of the chosen group, with how many there are; × or Esc clear the search, and clicking a group goes back to it.

**Parametrizador global** (global parameter editor): the **Parametrizador global** button, next to **Novo agente** (New agent), changes parameters on all agents by key (the flag name without the dash, e.g. `lot`), without touching the other parameters or each agent's settings. Each row has a key and a value; **Adicionar parâmetro** (Add parameter) creates another row (up to 20) and the trash can removes it. All the parameters go into each agent together: for example, `pair` and the `lot` of the new assets in the same operation, without the agent ending up with one and not the other.

- **Key and value:** checked as in an agent's parameters: lowercase letters, digits and `-` in the key, never the key of a dedicated field (e.g. `mode`, `mt5-magic`); the value on one line, up to 1024 bytes, without repeating `chave=` (key=). A key appears only once; a row left blank is ignored. A refused key or value changes nothing (`400`). The screen suggests the keys the agents have, says on how many agents each key is and with which values, and shows before applying how many agents change (each agent counted once).
- **Somente para agentes de LLM** (Only for LLM agents) and **Somente para os agentes ativos** (Only for the active agents): filters of the agents changed. Agents outside the filter do not change or restart; the result only counts how many were left out. With both, only the LLM agents that are on are affected. Without a filter, all agents are affected. An LLM parameter (`llm-`, `calendar-`, `news-`) applied without the LLM filter is still refused on agents of other types, which do not know these flags and would not start with them: they appear as ignored, with the reason.
- **Adicionar para agentes sem o parâmetro** (Add to agents without the parameter): unchecked, only the agents in the filter that already have the key change, on or off (without the active filter); checked, the key is also added to the others.
- **Remover chaves** (Remove keys): in the selector at the top, next to **Definir valores** (Set values). It removes the parameters from the agents in the filter by key, and the agent then uses the strategy's default for them (for example, removing the 30m `timeframe` from an agent designed for 5m).
  - Each row has the key and, optionally, a value. With a value, the key is removed only from the agents that have exactly that value; without a value, it is removed with any value.
  - **Adicionar para agentes sem o parâmetro** disappears in this mode. The preview says from how many agents the key is removed, how many have another value and how many do not have the key.
  - The editing rules still apply: the key of a dedicated field is refused, and an agent whose validation refuses the removal stays as it was, with the reason.
  - As with any parameter change, an agent that is on only applies the removal when it restarts: check **Reiniciar os agentes ativos** (Restart the active agents) or use **Reiniciar** (Restart) afterwards.
- **Reiniciar os agentes ativos:** checked, after saving the parameter, it restarts the agents of the filter that are on and that the controller starts (up to 8 at a time), whether they changed or not; those that are off stay off, and an agent started separately is not restarted (it appears with the reason). Unchecked, the agents that are on and changed get **Reinício pendente** (Restart pending), as in an edit.
- **How it is saved:** one agent at a time, with its row locked, all the parameters in a single write, by the same rules as an individual edit (e.g. a `pair` change that would make two active agents trade the same magic number and pair is refused on that agent). If a parameter is refused on an agent, none changes on it; a refused agent does not stop the others. An agent that already has all the values is not rewritten.
- **Result:** each agent in the filter appears as changed, added, removed (with the value it had), already had the value, ignored or failed (with the reason), with what was done with each parameter (e.g. `lot: alterado (antes 0.01)` (changed, before 0.01), `pair: adicionado` (added); with a single parameter, the previous value beside it), and, with the restart requested, as restarted, not restarted (with the reason) or off. Failures come first; the agents outside the filter are only counted.
- **API:** `POST /api/settings/agents/params` with `params` (a list of `key` and `value`; or `key` and `value` for a single one), `add_missing`, `restart_active`, `llm_only`, `enabled_only` and `remove` (removes the keys, with an empty `value` for any value; status `removed`); the response carries the parameters and each agent's result, with `params` per parameter (`excluded` for the agents outside the filter).

**Which changes take effect immediately:** on a running agent, **Ligado** (On), **Painel de trading** (Trade panel), **Controle via Telegram** (Control via Telegram), **Permitir reanálise** (Allow reanalysis), the prompt and [News](#news) (the switch, the server, the token and the window, on all agents). The others stay as "reinício pendente" (restart pending), applied only by the **Reiniciar** button (or by turning the agent off and on again). If the process crashes in the meantime, the automatic relaunch uses the settings the agent was started with: a pending change (from paper to live, of account, of magic number etc.) never takes effect on its own, and the dashboard stays on the address the process listens on. Secrets are the exception, because they are not trading configuration: the relaunch uses the current ones (a bridge token or a key that was revoked and replaced does not leave the agent crash-looping). Each secret only goes to the same endpoint it was started with: the bridge token to the same bridge, the model key to the same provider and base URL, the calendar token to the same calendar. If the endpoint also changed, the relaunch keeps the old endpoint and secret until **Reiniciar**.

**Permitir reanálise (LLM agents):** allows asking the model for a new analysis between closes, through the dashboard's **Reanalisar agora** (Reanalyze now) and Telegram's `/reanalyze`, with a note for the model. It is independent of **Painel de trading**: turning the trade panel off blocks only moving and canceling orders.

- When off, the button appears as **DESATIVADO** (DISABLED) and the agent itself refuses the request with `403`, whether it comes from the dashboard, from Telegram or from a direct API call. On an agent without an LLM, the response is `404`.
- Through Telegram, **Controle via Telegram** also applies besides it: with the control off, `/reanalyze` is refused like the other commands.
- It only exists on LLM agents: the card and the form only show the switch on them, and the controller refuses to turn it on for another type (`400`). On a new agent, it comes off.
- It takes effect immediately, with the agent running (paper or live), without a restart.
- Outside the controller, the LLM agent only accepts reanalysis with the `-reanalysis` flag.

**When the agent exits on its own:**

- With **Reiniciar se cair** (Restart if it crashes) on, the card shows **Reiniciando** (Restarting) during the wait and, in the note, the last exit and how many times the controller has restarted it since the last start.
- With **Reiniciar se cair** off (at the start of the process), the agent stays **Parado** (Stopped) and the note says why. It only comes back with **Reiniciar**, when it is turned off and on again or when the startup settings change after the exit; changing the trade panel, the Telegram control, the reanalysis or the prompt does not start it. A change saved while it was still running is a pending restart and does not start it either (the controller reads the settings as soon as it sees the exit; a change saved in those milliseconds counts as pending).
- This **Parado** state lives only in the controller's memory: when the controller restarts, it starts every agent that is on again, including the one that had exited on its own.
- Restarting or turning off an agent does not lock the dashboard while it stops: the other changes are applied immediately, and the agent that is stopping is adjusted right after. When shutting down, the controller no longer accepts restarts: a restart in progress does not launch the agent again.

**Parameters:** these are the agent's flags that have no dedicated field. In the form, the name goes without the dash on one side and the value on the other. Each binary lists its flags with `-h`, for example `go run ./agents/llm/llmagent -h`. In lists, the items are separated by `;` (the comma is still accepted). In sizes (`lot`, `mt5-contract-size`), the decimal separator is a dot: a value that may have a decimal comma, such as `lot = 1,5` or `lot = 0,05`, is refused and the agent does not start, because `1,5` with two pairs would become lots of 1 and 5; write `1.5` for one size or `1;5` for one per pair. The comma as a separator is only accepted between sizes with a decimal dot, as in the old format `lot = 0.03,0.01`. In the LLM agent, `lot` and `llm-correlated` have a single format, each pair by name and separated by `;`: `lot = BTCUSDm=0.01;XAUUSDm=0.02` (every pair of `pair`) and `llm-correlated = BTCUSDm=DXYm+XAUUSDm;XAUUSDm=DXYm+USTECm`. One lot for all pairs, the lots in the order of `pair`, one list of markets for all pairs and the comma are refused, and the agent does not start (the reason, with the example, goes to the controller's log).

| Name                   | Value                                             | Effect                                                                                  |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `pair`                 | `BTCUSDm;XAUUSDm`                                 | pairs traded by the agent                                                               |
| `timeframe`            | `30m`                                             | timeframe of the candles                                                                |
| `lot`                  | `BTCUSDm=0.01;XAUUSDm=0.02`                       | fixed lot of each pair by name (on strategy agents, also `0.01` for all)                |
| `leverage`             | `400`                                             | safety cap: refuses a position larger than balance × leverage                           |
| `max-risk-pct`         | `2`                                               | strategy agents: refuses an entry that loses more than 2% of the free margin at the stop (0: no limit) |
| `regime`               | `trend` or `range`                                | strategy agents: only enters in a trend, or only in a range (see [Entry filter](#features)) |
| `equity-curve`         | `20`                                              | strategy agents: pauses entries while the theoretical curve is below its 20-trade average |
| `meta-model`           | `data/meta/matrend.json`                          | strategy agents: only enters with the model's probability above the threshold           |
| `intermarket-symbol`   | `DXYm`                                            | strategy agents: allows buying or selling by the reference against its EMA              |
| `llm-consult-interval` | `5m`                                              | queries the model every 5 min (minimum 10s); required on the LLM agent in live and paper |
| `llm-timeout`          | `2m`                                              | maximum time of a request to the model                                                  |
| `llm-correlated`       | `BTCUSDm=DXYm+XAUUSDm;XAUUSDm=DXYm+USTECm`        | correlated markets of each pair by name, joined by `+`                                  |
| `llm-candles`          | `60`                                              | recent candles sent to the model                                                        |
| `llm-setup`            | `free` or `trend_pullback`                        | decision mode of the model                                                              |
| `llm-pending-expiry`   | `N` (candles, to be set)                          | cancels an unexecuted pending order after N candles (without it, only the model and the rules cancel) |
| `outbox-quarantine`    | `true`                                            | starts even with an unreadable outbox, moved to `.corrupt-<hora>` (see Records)         |

**About `llm-consult-interval`:**

- It is the model's only schedule in live and paper, and it is required: without it, the LLM agent does not start. Do not confuse it with `llm-timeout`, which is the maximum time of a response.
- The schedule follows the clock: with `5m`, the queries fall at :00, :05, :10 and so on, each on the asset's first tick within that slot, with the candle in progress (`scheduled` in the model's context).
- A candle close never queries the model. A schedule slot that coincides with a close (e.g. :15 on a 15m timeframe) is queried on the first tick of the next candle, already with the closed candle in the context; a candle still delivered in progress after its close time (a delayed close at the broker) is not queried.
- The agent's start and a reanalysis take up the slot in which they query: the model is not queried again in it.
- With an interval larger than the timeframe, the candles in between get no query. The memory and the position's candles (`candles_held`) count those candles.
- Each query is a billed request.

### Resetting the results

In **Configurações → Agentes** (Settings → Agents), the **Resultados** (Results) button on the agent's card shows the results by period and allows resetting them.

- **What is reset:** Operações (Trades), Taxa de acerto (Win rate), Lucro líquido (Net profit) and Fator de lucro (Profit factor) of all the agent's assets. After the reset, the dashboard cards and Telegram's `/profit` count only the trades closed from then on, by close time: the `updated_at` of the executed exit order, which is the execution time reported by the broker (in MT5, the deal's). Once executed, that time does not change: the database and the agent keep the one from the first write of the execution, even if a later copy of the same order arrives with another time, so a trade never changes period after it closes.
- **Depósito (Deposit):** the starting balance of the new period, in the currency of the agent's account (for example BRL), fixed in the form; in the paper wallet, the simulation's currency, and the currency is typed only when the agent is offline. It is a single balance for the agent: the results of all assets add up in it, with no split between assets. It is rounded to the cent as typed (`2500.555` becomes `2500.56`) and stored in exact cents (`NUMERIC(16,2)`).
- **What does not change:**
  - the orders stay in the history;
  - open positions and pending orders stay at the broker, and a position open at the time of the reset counts in the new period when it closes;
  - the daily limits and the LLM memory still consider all trades.
- **Previous periods:** they are kept in the database (`agent_periods`) and appear in the same dialog, each with deposit, result per asset and final balance. The results come from the agent's orders, so they only appear while it is running.

### Prompts

- **Where they live:** in the database, with version history. The seed brings the 3 prompts of the strategies that only a language model trades and those created in the dashboard (see [Initial agents](#initial-agents)); the prompts of the coded strategies are in `agents/llm/testdata/strategy_prompts.sql`.
- **Header:** the `# Indicators:` and `# Take profit:` lines define the indicators sent and the take profit handling; `# Pending orders:`, `# Drawings:` and `# Context:` turn on the pending orders, the drawings and the SMC context (see [SMC/ICT, pending orders and drawings](#smcict-pending-orders-and-drawings)).
  - The header is the lines at the top of the prompt that start with `#`, and it is not sent to the model. It ends at the first line of other text. After a blank line, it only continues if the next block of lines with `#` has a key the agent reads (`# Indicators:`, `# Take profit:`, `# Pending orders:`, `# Drawings:`, `# Context:`, `# Daily gain limit:`, `# Daily loss limit:`) or a line that looks like a daily limit in currency (see [Daily limits](#daily-limits)). Another block after a blank line, such as a `## Regras` (Rules) title, already starts the strategy text.
  - A line with `#` in the strategy text goes to the model like the others.
  - The key is the text before the colon, case-insensitive and with spaces tolerated (`# take profit : none` works). If a key repeats, the first line wins, except for the daily limits (see below).
- **Times and structure:** the times of `vwap` (the anchor) and of `range` (start, end and end of trading) follow the clock of the given time zone, including on the days daylight saving time changes (before, on those days, they were off by 1 h). The structure's `atr_percentile` ignores the ATRs still in warm-up (the 0 that talib writes on the first candles), which used to count as the calmest.
- **Switching:** through the agent's card or through Telegram (`/useprompt`), with the agent running, from the next analysis on. Through Telegram, only with the agent's **Controle via Telegram** (Control via Telegram) on (see [Telegram](#telegram)).
- **Deletion:** a prompt used by an agent is not deleted. Deleting a prompt at the same time as it is assigned to an agent (or to a new agent) does not leave the agent with a deleted prompt: the operation that arrives later waits for the other and fails (the prompt is in use, or no longer exists).
- **Prompt invalid for the agent:** it is refused, the previous one stays, and the reason appears on the card.

#### Daily limits

A prompt can set daily limits on the result of each asset, in the header, in the account currency:

```none
# Daily gain limit: 250 BRL
# Daily loss limit: 150 BRL
```

- **Result of the day:** the sum of the results of the asset's positions closed on the trading day, as the broker records them, with swap, commission and fee. A position counts on the day it closes.
- **Trading day:** starts at midnight in the agent's time zone, the `TZ` of the `.env` (America/Belem in the stack), and the model's context carries the name of that time zone. Without `TZ`, the system time zone (`/etc/localtime`) is used. An empty or invalid `TZ` becomes UTC, with a warning in the log.
- **Value:** a number with a decimal dot or comma, without a thousands separator, and the currency code: `1000 BRL`, `1000.00 BRL`, `1000,00 BRL` or `1,50 BRL`. A dot or a comma followed by exactly 3 digits, as in `1.000` or `1.500`, is ambiguous (thousands or decimal separator) and the prompt is refused, with a request for an unambiguous value.
- **Uninterpreted line:** the prompt is refused when a header line looks like a daily limit but the agent would not read it. A line looks like a limit when it talks about a day (`daily`, `diário`) and a limit and carries a value. This includes a key different from the two above (for example `# Daily loss limits: 150 BRL` or `# Limite diário: 150 BRL`) and the same key twice. This way, a misspelled limit does not stay off silently.
  - Only the header is read and can refuse the prompt. A title in the strategy text, such as `## Limite diário: 3 operações` (Daily limit: 3 trades), goes to the model and never refuses the prompt.
  - A currency limit line in the text, such as `# Daily loss limit: 150 BRL` after the first line of the strategy, also goes to the model and is not applied: a warning goes to the log when the prompt is applied.
  - Currency limit (what keeps a block after a blank line in the header, and the warning about the text): the line that talks about a day and a limit and whose value starts, or whose line ends, with a number and the code of an ISO 4217 currency (e.g. `# Limite diário: 150 BRL por dia`, `# Daily loss limit 150 BRL`; `USC`, of cent accounts, also counts). A number of something else (`## Limite diário de perda: 2 ATR`) or a currency that is not the value (`## Daily limit: 2 trades, max 1 EUR pair`, `# Daily loss limit for 2 USD pairs`) does not count: the title goes to the model.
- **Pairing:** each exit counts toward the position it closed. In MT5, the position comes from the ticket, for the orders of the current run and of the history read (60 days). When the history does not say which position an order closed, the exit closes the positions on the other side in order of arrival; a stop or target closes only those opened before it. This applies to orders older than the history or, from a previous run, to the market order that opened a position, and also to a market order of the current run while the exit of one of the positions it closed is not yet in the history. An exit whose entry is not in the agent's orders is left out of the result of the day and the LLM memory, with a warning in the log, without disturbing the following positions.
- **When a limit is reached:**
  - new entries on the asset are blocked until the next day;
  - the model is not even queried without a position, including in reanalyses;
  - an entry returned by the model is refused;
  - the other assets stay free.
- **Open position:** it keeps being managed normally. The limit does not force an exit or touch the stop or the target, and its result counts when it closes.
- **Close without a recorded result:** while the broker has not recorded the result of a close of the day, entries wait, usually for seconds. The agent rereads the broker's history until the result appears: on every check during the first minute, then at growing intervals of up to 5 min, for up to 24 h, with a warning in the log after the first minute.
  - This also applies to a position closed at the broker by the stop, the target or by hand, whose exit is not yet in the history. Its stop and target stay pending in the agent until the history shows the exit; only then is the stop (or the target) taken as executed, with the result, and the other as canceled. After 24 h without the exit, both are canceled and the result is unknown.
  - It also applies after an agent restart in that interval: a bot position that is no longer on the account and whose exit is missing from the history read at startup is awaited the same way, as closed at the agent's start (the close time, before that, is unknown).
  - After 10 min without the result, the agent logs an error and sends a notification to the dashboard and to Telegram, once per close. The block reason then says how long the result has been awaited.
  - Entries keep waiting, at most until the next day, because without that result the result of the day is unknown. When it arrives, another notification says that entries are being checked against the limits again.
- **Manual trades:** those opened by hand in MT5, on an agent's magic number, count in the result but are not blocked.
- **Currency:** the limits must be in the account currency; otherwise, the prompt is refused.
  - The prompt the agent starts with is checked as soon as the account is read. If it is refused, the reason appears on the card and the agent stops, as with another invalid prompt.
  - If the account currency was not yet known when the prompt was applied, the refusal appears on the card when the currency is read, and entries stay blocked until another prompt is applied.
- **Where it applies:** only in live on MT5, where the broker records the results. In backtest and paper, the limits are not applied, and a warning goes to the log.

### News

In **Configurações > Notícias** (Settings > News) is the economic calendar from the MetaTrader 5 MCP server, for every agent, LLM or not:

- **Recurso de notícias** (News feature): turns the calendar queries and the news block of all agents on and off. When it is off, no agent queries or blocks: the model of the LLM agents is told that the news was not read (not that there are no releases).
- **Servidor e janela da notícia** (News server and window): the address of the MCP server (by default `http://mt5:22347/mcp`, the compose terminal) and the window, in minutes before and after a release, with the minimum importance (high; medium and high; or all). The defaults are the ones the LLM agent already used: 15 minutes before and after, high importance. Both at 0: no block.
- **Within the window of a release** for a currency of an agent's assets (for example USD for XAUUSD and BTCUSD):
  - the agent opens no position: its entries are held, regardless of the XTrader AI block and the daily lock, and on all of its assets, because entry control is per agent;
  - when the window starts, the asset's pending entry orders (limit and stop outside a group) are canceled and its position is flattened at market, once per window;
  - when the window ends, entries resume; the guard queries the calendar every 15 s.
  - In an LLM agent, the calendar also goes into the model's context and entries are removed from the allowed actions during the window, as before, now with the global window.
  - A calendar that cannot be read blocks nothing, and the log warns every 15 min. In backtests the guard does not run: results around releases may differ from live. Only the strategies that trade the releases (`newsbreakout`) receive, in the controller's backtest, the scheduled times of the period, read from this same calendar (time, code, name and importance, never the released values). With news off or the calendar unreadable, the backtest runs without them, with a warning.
- **Dashboard news card:** the calendar releases of the major currencies (USD, EUR, GBP, JPY, CHF, CAD, AUD, NZD and CNY), whatever the agents' assets are, read from the same server with the same token.
  - **Period:** Hoje (Today), Amanhã (Tomorrow), Hoje e amanhã (Today and tomorrow, the default), Próximas 24 h (Next 24 h), Esta semana (This week), Próxima semana (Next week) or Personalizado (Custom: from one day to another, up to 31 days), by the browser's clock.
  - **Impact filter:** Baixo (Low), Médio (Medium) and Alto (High), all checked at first; at least one stays checked. Releases without an impact (holidays, for example) are listed with the low-impact ones.
  - The period and the filter are saved in the browser.
  - **Each release:** the time (or "Dia todo" (All day)), the currency, the impact in three bars, the name and the actual (once published), forecast and previous values, in the calendar's unit. Past releases are dimmed, the next one is highlighted and the list scrolls to it. Releases whose impact blocks entries show **bloqueia entradas** (blocks entries), and **entradas bloqueadas agora** (entries blocked now) inside the window; the footer states the window.
  - The card reads the calendar every minute. With news off, without the server or the token, or with the server down, it states the reason, with a shortcut to this tab for those who can see it.
  - In the default layout, it sits beside the chart, with the height of the chart's column, whatever the number of releases the filter leaves. In a layout saved before the card existed, it also goes beside the chart, and it can be moved or hidden like the other cards.
- **Token do servidor MCP** (MCP server token; in MetaTrader 5: Ferramentas > Opções > Servidor MCP, that is Tools > Options > MCP Server): a single one, shared by the agents. It is stored encrypted in the database (`news_settings`), bound to where it is stored, and is never shown, returned by the API or written to the log: the tab only shows whether it is set, with **Trocar** (Change) and **Remover** (Remove). Turning the feature on or off without filling in the token keeps the saved one. When it is on without a token (or without a server), no queries are made, nothing is blocked and the model is told that the credential is missing.
- **Applying changes:** changes take effect immediately for every running agent, through the runtime they follow: the calendar is turned on or off, and the server, the token and the window are swapped (the MCP client opens a new session). An agent that starts receives the current news settings right away. No restart is needed.
- **Testar conexão** (Test connection): on the server and window card, queries the saved server once with the saved token (even with news turned off, to check the credential) and shows **Respondeu** (Responded), **Sem servidor** (No server), **Token ausente** (Token missing), **Token recusado** (Token refused; HTTP 401/403) or **Indisponível** (Unavailable), right below the server field.
- **Earlier settings:** before, each LLM agent had its own token, its own server address (`calendar_url`) and its own window (`news-block-before`, `news-block-after`, `news-block-importance`). The migrator moves the token and the address to the global configuration: with a single value, that value is reused (the token turns the feature on); with none, the defaults remain. With different values (or a token that cannot be opened), none is chosen: the tab warns about the conflict, naming the agents, until the value is saved there, and the old values are kept as they were. The agents' windows are removed: the global one applies. Once handled, the old columns (`agent_llm_settings.calendar_token` and `calendar_url`) are removed by the migrator, on the next run in case of a conflict.

### Telegram

The bot only answers the IDs authorized in **Configurações > Telegram** (Settings > Telegram). The operation commands apply to the chosen agent and require that agent's **Controle via Telegram** (Telegram control) to be on.

| Command                                     | What it does                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `/agents`, `/agent <nome>`                  | lists the agents and picks the one the commands act on                                                         |
| `/status`, `/balance`, `/profit`            | bot state, balance and a summary of the latest trades                                                          |
| `/start`, `/stop`                           | starts and stops the bot                                                                                       |
| `/reanalyze <par> [nota]`                   | asks the model for a new analysis now (LLM agent with **Permitir reanálise** (Allow reanalysis) on)            |
| `/prompts`, `/prompt <nome>`                | lists and shows the prompts                                                                                    |
| `/editprompt <nome>`, `/confirm`, `/cancel` | edits a prompt, with the text in the next message or in a `.txt`; saves only with `/confirm`                   |
| `/useprompt <agente> <nome>`                | switches the prompt of an LLM agent                                                                            |
| `/help`                                     | help                                                                                                           |

The commands that change the strategy also respect **Controle via Telegram**: `/useprompt` requires the agent's control; `/editprompt` requires the control of every active agent that uses the prompt and, if any of them has it off, refuses the edit and lists which ones (disabled agents do not count). `/prompts` and `/prompt` only read and do not require the control.

Editing through Telegram works like this:

- After `/editprompt <nome>`, the bot waits **10 minutes** for the new text (in the next message or in a `.txt`). A text that starts with `/`, such as a mistyped command (`/stauts`), is never taken as content: the bot warns and keeps waiting, and `/cancel` gives up.
- Once the text is received (or if it is already in the lines after the command), the bot shows a summary (lines, bytes and the beginning) and only saves the new version with `/confirm`, also within **10 minutes**. After the deadline, the edit expires without saving anything, and the bot says so on the next message.
- If the prompt is edited elsewhere (on the dashboard, for example) after `/editprompt`, the confirmation is refused and nothing is saved; the message states the current version. The agents' Telegram control is checked again at `/confirm`.
- If the database fails at `/confirm` (while reading the prompt or the agents, or while saving), nothing is saved and the content stays pending: a new `/confirm` tries again, within the same deadline. A definitive refusal (prompt does not exist, invalid content, edited elsewhere or control off) discards the edit.

### XTrader AI

In **Configurações > XTrader AI** (Settings > XTrader AI):

1. **Modelo** (Model): provider (OpenAI or Anthropic), model, **Chave da API** (API key; write-only), optional base URL, reasoning, max tokens and the timeout of each answer. The key is stored encrypted in the database, like the LLM agents' key.
2. **Intervalo** (Interval) between analyses (from 5 min to 24 h) and the **instruções** (instructions): the operational, risk and performance criteria, in free text.
3. **Autonomia** (Autonomy): what the supervisor does on its own (**Bloquear novas entradas** (Block new entries), **Parar o agente quando zerado** (Stop the agent when flat), **Cancelar pendentes de entrada** (Cancel pending entry orders)) and how many actions per analysis. When an item is off, that action becomes a proposal.
4. **XTrader AI ativo** (XTrader AI active): turns on the periodic analyses; requires the provider, the model and the key. **Analisar agora** (Analyze now) runs an analysis right away, even when it is off (with the model and the key set).

The same page shows the **Histórico** (History) of the analyses (summary and findings) and the **Registro** (Log) of the actions, with origin, status and reason. The origin is the one recorded by the backend (`origin` in `GET /api/supervisor/actions`): **XTrader AI**, **Usuário (nome)** (User (name)) or an automatic rule, such as **Limite diário (automático)** (Daily limit (automatic)) and **Horário de operação (automático)** (Trading hours (automatic)). These rules flatten positions on their own and never appear as XTrader AI actions. In the agent bar, **Agente** (Agent) shows whether the agent is stopped and **Entradas** (Entries) whether entries are blocked, each with who did it (e.g. **Parado pelo XTrader AI** (Stopped by XTrader AI), **Bloqueadas por admin** (Blocked by admin)).

The floating button in the corner of the screen opens the chat, with the **Conversa** (Chat) and **Análises** (Analyses) tabs:

- **Questions** ("Which agents are losing today?") get an analytical answer, with the time of the data, written progressively on the screen and formatted in Markdown (for example, a table per agent). Closing the chat panel midway loses nothing: the answer is saved on the server and appears when it is opened again.
- **Action requests** ("Block the entries of orb", "Close all positions") become a proposal card: the scope spelled out, the affected positions (agent, pair, side, volume, ticket, SL/TP and P/L in the account currency) and pending orders, with the positions of stopped agents marked, what the action does and what it does not do and, separately, whether the pending orders are canceled and whether entries become blocked. "Close all" only covers the positions of the application's agents. Closing positions and stopping now require a second confirmation click.
- **Result:** after **Confirmar** (Confirm), the card shows the status (completed, partial, failed or unknown result) and the result of each item, checked against the account. An unknown result is never resent: check the account before asking again.

On the agent cards (in **Configurações > Agentes** (Settings > Agents)), **Bloquear entradas** (Block entries) and **Liberar entradas** (Release entries) do the same as the supervisor, recorded as user actions. An agent stopped by the supervisor shows a badge with the reason and only comes back when a user turns it on again.

> [!NOTE]
> The supervisor uses migrations `000020` to `000024` and the seed `0005_supervisor.sql`: run the `migrator` before starting the new controller (in Docker, the compose already does this). Without the seed, the supervisor stays off.

### Position protection

In **Configurações > Agentes** (Settings > Agents), the card's **Proteção** (Protection) button opens the agent's breakeven and trailing stop: it turns each feature on, sets the trigger, the margin, the distance and the step (in × ATR(14)) and authorizes XTrader AI to arm it or not. **Salvar** (Save) applies the change right away, without a restart. The button is highlighted when protection is on or authorized.

The protections armed by XTrader AI are listed in **Configurações › XTrader AI › Proteções armadas** (Settings › XTrader AI › Armed protections), with the position, the protection, the status (armed and waiting for the trigger, confirmed by the broker, refused by the broker, position closed), the previous stop → the requested one (confirmed or not) and the reason; each analysis lists the protections it armed.

> [!NOTE]
> Protection uses migrations `000025` (`agent_protection`) and `000026` (`position_protections`). The `migrator` applies them on its own when the new image starts, without deleting anything.

### Importing and exporting the configuration

In **Configurações › Importar e exportar** (Settings › Import and export), the configuration is exported to a JSON file (`xtrader-config-AAAA-MM-DD.json`) and imported back from it, on this installation or another one (for example, when changing machines), as in Nelogica's Profit.

- **What goes in the file:** chosen item by item, in a tree by section:
  - prompts: name, description and content;
  - MT5 accounts: login, server, bridge, **Permitir conta real** (Allow real account) and **Somente leitura** (Read-only);
  - agents: kind, mode, API address, enabled, the switches, the parameters, the account (bridge, magic number, comment), the LLM, the prompt and the MT5 account by name, the protection and the daily limit (with the stops at the broker);
  - roles, except Administrador (Administrator), which is fixed;
  - limits: global daily limit (with the switch for the stops at the broker and the least margin for new entries), trading hours, exposure per asset and agents per asset;
  - news, XTrader AI and Telegram (the bot and the authorized users);
  - this browser's preferences: dashboard layout, theme, chart type, hide values and the periods of the closed P/L and of Desempenho (Performance). They live in the browser and only pass through the screen, never through the controller.
- **What is left out:**
  - the dashboard users, because the password is a hash;
  - the active account of each terminal, which is switched in Contas MT5 (MT5 accounts);
  - the run state: bot stopped, blocks and locks of the day;
  - the history: orders, decisions, backtests and events.
- **Items refer to each other by name, not by ID:** an agent points to its prompt and MT5 account by name, and the windows and rules cite agents by name. That is why the file works on another installation.
- **Secrets** (LLM and XTrader AI keys, the password and token of the MT5 accounts, and the bridge, Telegram and news tokens):
  - are only included with **Incluir os segredos** (Include the secrets), encrypted with a password of at least 8 characters (Argon2id and AES-256-GCM). Without the password, no one can open them, not even XTrader;
  - on import, without the right password, the secrets stay as they are and the rest is imported;
  - an area's secrets are only exported and imported for those who can manage that area.
- **Importing:** choose the file. Nothing changes before confirmation.
  - The preview checks each item as its screen would and says whether it is **Novo** (New), **Alterado** (Changed; with the fields that change), **Igual** (Same) or **Inválido** (Invalid; with the reason).
  - It also warns about what the import does beyond the fields: an agent that comes in on and starts trading, an agent that will be turned off, a running agent that needs a restart, a daily limit that would trigger or release the lock now, trading hours that change the windows, and a new account, which comes in inactive.
  - New and changed items come checked. Same items are hidden (**Mostrar os iguais** (Show the same ones)) and invalid items cannot be checked.
  - **Importar N itens** (Import N items) applies the checked items together, in one transaction: if one is refused, nothing is applied, and the preview shows which one. An item that is not in the file is never deleted.
  - Agents end up on or off as in the file: those that are on go back to trading.
  - Afterwards, the same effects as saving from the screen apply: agents launched or stopped, the runtime sent to the agents, the daily lock and the trading hours re-evaluated, XTrader AI and the Telegram bot reloaded.
  - The screens' rules also apply: an agent's kind does not change; an agent's MT5 account only changes while the agent is stopped and has no positions; an active MT5 account does not change its login, server or bridge through the file.
- **Permissions:** exporting a section requires viewing its area; importing requires managing it (roles, through the users area). The tab shows for anyone who can view some area or the dashboard, because of the browser preferences.

### Rebuilding the database from scratch

The migrations in `sql/migrations` create the whole schema, one table per file, in dependency order, without `ALTER`. The seeds in `sql/seeds` insert the settings: Telegram off, the prompts and the agents. On an empty database, `migrator up` leaves everything ready, missing only the sensitive data. The migrations do not continue the ones from before 2026-10-14: an old database (version 22) is refused by `migrator up` and must be recreated. The orders, decisions, journal, accounting periods and discards of the old database are not carried over.

Before that, close the positions opened by the agents. Without its orders in the database, an agent's position that is still open at the broker stays as it is, with an error in the log, and the core does not count it in the profit or the results when it closes.

Build the image with these migrations only when it is time to recreate the database: with it, the `migrator` fails on the old database, and the `controller`, which waits for it, does not start. The old database is at version 22 of the previous numbering, which is now a supervisor migration: the `migrator` tries to apply `000023`, which fails (the `supervisor_actions` table does not exist there), and the database is marked dirty (`dirty`) at version 23, without any new table. To go back to the previous image without recreating the database, clear the mark first: `UPDATE schema_migrations SET version = 22, dirty = false;`.

```bash
deploy/docker/build.sh controller                                                      # in the repository: the image with the new migrations
cd /var/containers/xtrader
docker compose exec -T postgres pg_dump -U xtrader -Fc xtrader > xtrader-$(date +%F).dump  # backup of the old database
docker compose stop controller                                                         # the agents stop
docker compose exec postgres dropdb -U xtrader --force xtrader
docker compose exec postgres createdb -U xtrader -O xtrader xtrader
docker compose up -d                                                                   # the migrator creates the schema and the seeds
docker compose logs migrator                                                           # "schema at version 24 at least"
```

Then, on the dashboard:

1. **User:** if the database has no user, the controller creates the first one with `XTRADER_BOOTSTRAP_USERNAME` and `XTRADER_BOOTSTRAP_PASSWORD` from `services/controller/.env` (at least 8 characters). Recreate the other users in **Configurações > Usuários** (Settings > Users) and delete the bootstrap password from the `.env`.
2. **Agents**, in **Configurações > Agentes** (Settings > Agents): the bridge token (`MT5_BRIDGE_TOKEN` from `services/mt5/.env`) on the agents you will turn on; the OpenAI key on the LLM agents (`llm-trend`, `llm-smc-liquidity` and `llm-support-resistance`); the calendar MCP token, a single one for all, in **Configurações > Notícias** (Settings > News). Then turn on the agents you want: each agent that is on is a controller process trading the account.
3. **Telegram**, in **Configurações > Telegram** (Settings > Telegram): the bot token and the authorized users (the old database had none), and then turn it on.

## API

- **Dashboard** (`/api`): cookie session. Methods that change something require the `X-CSRF-Token` header with the value of the `csrf_token` cookie.
  - An error responds `{"error": "…"}` with the message in Portuguese, shown as is on the dashboard (e.g. `Sessão expirada: entre de novo.` (Session expired: sign in again.), `O agente gold não responde em localhost:5560.` (Agent gold does not respond at localhost:5560.)). The agents' API, passed through by the proxy, does the same. Database validations appear by their detail, without the technical prefix (e.g. `Desligue o agente gold antes.` (Turn off agent gold first.) instead of `store: invalid: …`), and an order refused while entries are blocked appears as **Entradas bloqueadas: <motivo>** (Entries blocked: <reason>) (Telegram recognizes the refusal by this beginning). Only details coming from libraries, the broker or the LLM stay in English (e.g. `Falha no LLM: …` (LLM failure: …)). A request that does not even reach the server (network down) appears on the dashboard as **Sem conexão com o XTrader. Verifique a rede e tente novamente.** (No connection to XTrader. Check the network and try again.), instead of the browser's message.
  - `/api/auth/login`, `/api/auth/logout` and `/api/auth/session`;
  - `GET /api/agents`: the dashboard's agents, each with its type, address, whether it responds, period, supervision and the pairs it trades (`pairs`, from the agent's settings; none for the default pair);
  - `/api/agents/{agente}/…`: proxy of each agent's API (snapshot, SSE events, controls, decisions). The proxy does not pass the browser's cookies, `Authorization`, `Proxy-Authorization`, `X-CSRF-Token`, `Origin`, `Referer`, `Sec-Fetch-*`, `Forwarded` or `X-Forwarded-*` on to the agent. From the agent's response, only the content headers pass (type, length, `Content-Disposition`, cache), never `Set-Cookie`, and only the types the API serves: JSON, `text/event-stream`, CSV, text, the PNG, JPEG, GIF and WebP images of attachments, and downloads (`Content-Disposition: attachment`). Any other type, such as HTML or SVG, becomes `502`;
  - `/api/settings/agents[/{nome}[/restart]]`, `/api/prompts[/{id}]`, `/api/settings/telegram[/users[/{id}]]` and `/api/users[/{id}]`;
  - `GET /api/positions/open`: the agents' open positions on the MT5 accounts, read from the bridges (a read stays valid for 2 s for all tabs), with `totals` (one per currency of the accounts read, in their order, 0 with no position), `balances` and `accounts` (the balances of the accounts read, each account once by login, per currency, and how many there are), `complete`, `positions` (agent, pair, side, volume in lots, prices, profit with swap and the account currency, ticket, whether the agent is running), `pending_orders` (the agents' pending orders on the accounts read, each one once by ticket, manual ones left out) and `missing` (the accounts not read, with the agents and the reason: `unreachable`, `refused` or `failed`);
  - `GET /api/performance?from=…&to=…`: the agents' live trades closed from `from` until before `to` (Unix seconds, up to 400 days), read from the deals of the MT5 accounts from 30 days before `from` until now (the read of each interval stays valid for 30 s): `trades` (position, account, agent, asset, side, lots, `opened_at` when known, `closed_at`, `entry`, `exit`, `gross`, `costs`, `net` and currency, in closing order), `accounts` (account, currency, agents, `balance` and `start_balance`, the balance at the start of the period), `complete` and `missing`, as in `/api/positions/open`. `400` for an invalid interval;
  - `GET /api/performance/excursions?from=…&to=…`: the price path of each trade of `/api/performance` in the same interval, read from the candles of the account's bridge: `excursions` (account, position, `timeframe`, `high`, `low`, `mfe`, `mae`, or `error` with no path), `complete` and `missing`. Each trade read stays stored while the controller runs. `400` for an invalid interval;
  - `GET /api/positions/closed?from=…&to=…`: the profit of the agents' positions closed from `from` until before `to` (Unix seconds, up to 400 days), read from the deals of the MT5 accounts (the read of each interval stays valid for 10 s; deals are read from 30 days before `from`, to find the entry of each position), with `from`, `to`, `totals` (one per currency of the accounts read, 0 with no close), `agents` (agent, amount, account currency, how many positions it closed, whether it is running), `series` (per currency of the `totals`, the `points` with `at`, in Unix seconds, and `amount`, the running total after the exits of that second, in chronological order, the last one equal to the total; no points when there is no exit), `complete` and `missing`, as in `/api/positions/open`. `400` for an invalid interval;

## Security

To report a vulnerability, see [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md), never in a public issue.

- **Login:** passwords in bcrypt, session with an `HttpOnly` cookie (and `Secure` behind an HTTPS proxy) and CSRF protection. After login, it only goes back to a page of the dashboard itself (a `next` with a control character, a space, `\` or another site goes to `/`).
- **Login attempt limit:** it is counted before the password is checked, because bcrypt costs about 250 ms of CPU on the controller, which also runs the agents:
  - **per client address** (strict): one login at a time; after a failure, the next one only 1 s later; from the 5th failure in a row, the wait doubles with each failure (2 s, 4 s, 8 s… up to 5 min). Failures are forgotten 1 h after the last one;
  - **per user** (looser, adding up all addresses, so that an attacker cannot lock out the legitimate user): 10 attempts in a row and then 1 every 30 s. A correct login resets this limit;
  - **at most 2 passwords checked at the same time.**

  A refused login gets `429` with `Retry-After`, without the password being checked, whether the user exists or not (the time for a nonexistent user is the same, with a fake bcrypt). An IPv6 address counts by its /64. The counters live in memory (at most 4096 addresses and 4096 users; when full, a new one is refused until the old ones expire) and reset when the controller restarts. A distributed attack (many addresses) against a single user can delay that user's login while it lasts: use strong passwords.
- **Reverse proxy:** `X-Forwarded-For` and `X-Forwarded-Proto` only count when the connection comes from a trusted proxy, `ui.trusted_proxies` in `config.yml` (default: the loopback, where the host's ngrok runs). The client address, used in the logs and in the login limit, is the last one in `X-Forwarded-For` before the trusted proxies: the earlier ones are supplied by the client itself. In Docker, the host's ngrok reaches the controller through the gateway of the compose network (e.g. `172.18.0.1`), so the container's config trusts Docker's private range, `172.16.0.0/12`; with the dashboard port published only on the host's loopback, no other client arrives through that range.
- **Sessions:** last 7 days, live in the controller's memory (restarting it logs everyone out) and are checked against the database every minute: a user who is deactivated, deleted, has a different password (including one changed through SQL or by the migrator) or has no role ends the session. With the database down, the session continues for up to 5 min since the last check, so that a PostgreSQL restart does not log anyone out; after that, it is refused until the database is back, when it continues if nothing changed.
- **Roles and permissions** (Configurações > Usuários e papéis (Settings > Users and roles)): each user has a role, and the role says what they see and do, per area.
  - **Areas:** Painel (Dashboard), view; Operação (Operation), operate; Chat do XTrader AI (XTrader AI chat), use; Desempenho (Performance), view; and view and manage in Backtesting, Agentes (Agents), Contas MT5 (MT5 accounts), Limites (Limits), Notícias (News), Prompts, XTrader AI, Telegram and Usuários e papéis (Users and roles). The catalog lives in `ui/access.go` and is served at `GET /api/roles`.
  - **Implied:** manage includes view; operate and using the chat include viewing the dashboard.
  - **Operação** covers starting and stopping the bot, moving and canceling orders, the reanalysis, the × and **Zerar tudo** (Flatten all), blocking and releasing entries, and confirming or rejecting the proposals of XTrader AI.
  - **Backend:** each API route declares the permissions it requires (any one of them is enough). Without any of them, the response is `403` ("Você não tem permissão para esta ação." (You do not have permission for this action.)), including on a direct call to the API. Some lists also serve another area: the prompts and the MT5 accounts for those who manage agents, the agents for those who view the limits, the trading hours for those who manage backtests. A test walks through every route and fails if a new one does not declare a permission.
  - **Dashboard:** shows only the allowed pages, tabs and actions. Without manage, the forms are read-only. A page without permission leads to the first allowed one; a role with no area shows "Sem acesso" (No access), with the Sair (Sign out) button.
  - **User without a role:** cannot sign in. The login responds `403` ("Seu usuário não tem um papel atribuído: peça acesso a um administrador." (Your user has no role assigned: ask an administrator for access.)), with no session. No permission is given by default.
  - **Administrador** (Administrator): a fixed role, with all permissions, including the ones created later. It cannot be edited or deleted. The first user (bootstrap) gets this role. On upgrade, the migrator grants it once to all existing users, who keep the access they had.
  - **Lock:** there is always at least one active user with a role that manages users and roles. Deactivating or deleting that user, changing their role or removing the permission from the role is refused (`409`). A role with users cannot be deleted.
  - **When it applies:** a change made through the dashboard applies on the next request of whoever was affected. A change made directly in the database applies at the next session check (up to 1 min). Removing a user's role ends their sessions.
  - **Warning:** whoever manages users and roles can give themselves any role. In practice, they are an administrator.
  - The Telegram bot has its own list of authorized IDs and does not use the dashboard roles.
- **Secrets:** encrypted in the database (AES-256-GCM), write-only on the dashboard and never shown in responses, logs or on Telegram. The bot token is masked in the logs.
  - Each secret is encrypted together with its place (`tabela:coluna:id` (table:column:id), e.g. `agent_llm_settings:api_key:3`), with a version prefix. Someone who can write to the database but does not have the key cannot swap secrets between rows or columns: copied to another place (the model's key as the token of a bridge pointed at another server, for example), the secret does not open, and the agent does not start until it is set again.
  - Secrets saved by earlier versions, without the place, still work and become bound to it when they are saved again (the Telegram token, on every save of the bot settings). Until then, they do not have this protection: to bind all of them at once, set them again on the dashboard.
  - Going back to an earlier version after this requires setting the secrets again: it would read the prefix as part of the secret, and the requests using it fail.
  - There is no rotation of `XTRADER_SECRETS_KEY`: changing it requires setting all secrets again.
- **Agents:** receive a minimal environment and a new token on every start, revoked when they exit.
- **Agents' API:** has no login, so it stays on the loopback only. The dashboard refuses (`400`) an `api_address` outside `localhost`, `127.0.0.0/8` and `[::1]`. An agent saved before this rule with another host (e.g. `0.0.0.0:5580`) is not launched, does not enter the dashboard and is not probed, and its card shows "Não iniciado" (Not started) with the reason; the others continue normally. Fix the address in the agent's settings. Before upgrading, check with `SELECT name, api_address FROM agents;`.
  - **Per-start key:** on every start, the controller hands the agent a random key (`XTRADER_AGENT_API_KEY`, separate from the internal API token) and sends it in `X-Xtrader-Agent-Key` from the proxy, the probe and Telegram. The agent refuses (`403`) whoever does not carry it, so another process on the machine cannot operate the bot. An agent run on its own (e.g. a backtest with `-api`) has no key, and the dashboard accesses it without one.
  - **Browser:** a page from another site opened in a browser on the machine reaches `localhost`, but the API refuses requests with `Origin`, with a `Sec-Fetch-Site` from another site or with another `Host` (DNS rebinding), and only accepts changes in JSON, which a page from another site cannot send without a preflight. The dashboard proxy checks the session and the CSRF and only then removes these headers.
- **Dashboard headers:** every response carries a `Content-Security-Policy` that allows only the dashboard's own files (`default-src 'self'`; the inline configuration script of `index.html` allowed by its SHA-256 hash; `frame-ancestors 'none'`; `object-src 'none'`; `base-uri 'none'`), plus `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff` and `Referrer-Policy: same-origin`. The directories of the dashboard bundle are not listed (`404`). If the bundle does not load, the error page shows a generic message, and the reason goes only to the log (`ui: dashboard bundle unavailable`).
- **Slow connections:** the dashboard, the internal API and each agent's API allow 10 s for the headers (at most 32 KiB) and 30 s for the whole request, body included, and close an idle keep-alive connection after 2 min. The response has no deadline, because the events (SSE) and the long-poll of the internal API last longer than that.
- **Logs:** a message from someone who is not a user of the Telegram bot is refused and logged only by the sender's ID, at most one line per minute (with the count of refused messages in the interval), never the content. An HTTP error from the calendar MCP server keeps only the start of the body (1 KiB), without the token or anything that looks like a key.
- **XTrader AI:** the model never receives keys, tokens, the bridge URL, the account login or the magic number (the accounts go by nickname). A read that fails enters the snapshot only as the reason (`unreachable`, `refused`, `failed`, "its API doesn't answer"); the full error goes only to the log. It acts only through tools whose arguments are validated in the backend: none of them opens a position, increases a position, loosens a stop, releases entries or turns an agent back on. The `supervisor` channel of the agent API only reduces risk and cannot be reached from the dashboard. Closing positions, stopping now and releasing entries require a signed-in user, with the usual session and CSRF. In the chat, the tools that act immediately (`execute_action` and `protect_position`) are offered only to those who can operate, and `set_daily_limits` only to those who manage the limits. A call without permission is refused anyway; those who cannot operate can only propose, and the proposal waits for the confirmation of someone who can. A bridge in read-only mode or on a real account without **Permitir conta real** (Allow real account) refuses the direct closes.
- **Real account:** requires **Permitir conta real** on the agent and on the MT5 account (Configurações > Contas MT5 (Settings > MT5 accounts)), which the bridge applies together with **Somente leitura** (Read-only).
- **Ports published in Docker:** only on the host's loopback, `127.0.0.1:8082` (dashboard, where the host's ngrok tunnels to), `127.0.0.1:5900` (VNC, which only starts with a password) and `127.0.0.1:5432` (PostgreSQL). Docker publishes the ports outside the host firewall (ufw), so a port on `0.0.0.0` would be open to the network even when "closed" in the firewall. From another machine, use an SSH tunnel, e.g. `ssh -N -L 5900:127.0.0.1:5900 <usuário>@<servidor>` for VNC and `ssh -N -L 5433:127.0.0.1:5432 <usuário>@<servidor>` for `psql -h 127.0.0.1 -p 5433` (details in [deploy/docker/README.md](deploy/docker/README.md#acesso-de-outra-máquina)).
- **Dependencies:** `go.mod` pins the toolchain (`toolchain go1.25.14`), with the security fixes of the standard library, and the image builds with Go 1.26.8. To check for known vulnerabilities: `go run golang.org/x/vuln/cmd/govulncheck@latest ./...`.
